5. CONTRADICTIONS, FAILED OR COSTLY (for the lead)
- F27 and F28 show that memory with a model-driven write pipeline can cost more than replaying the whole conversation, and that break-even turns differ from "never" to "first tens of turns" by system and model. This goes against assuming W is cheap. V5's W is a plain Markdown summary, so the question for P1 is what the model call that writes the summary costs per session.
- F8 and F30: any step that clears or trims history rewrites the cache; Anthropic exposes a `clear_at_least` minimum for exactly this reason. This supports V5's gated eviction and masking.
- F4: the 20-block lookback can silently miss a growing conversation's cache read when appended tool blocks push the breakpoint too far. V5's appended definitions make this a live risk; the parameter to log is the cached-token counter per turn.
- F3 and F13: below the model's minimum cacheable length or without 2,050 new tokens (Gemini 4), a "cached" prefix is silently not cached. Both strengthen V5's point about a small core.
- F2 and F11: write multipliers are 1.25x (5-minute or 30-minute) and 2x or 2.5x for longer retention, so "keep cached for returning users" costs more than a plain first-turn write. V5's w range of 1.0 to 1.25 omits the longer-retention cases.
- F23: LMCache's in-process CacheBlend is deprecated; F22 follow-up reports quality loss from chunk-level reuse.
- F29 versus F27 and F28: disagreement on whether full replay is "never economical" or can beat memory, with no common cache pricing. Treat as unsettled.
