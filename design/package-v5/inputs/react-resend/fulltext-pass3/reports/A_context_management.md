## 1. 2610.35496 Hidden Costs of Forgetting (Vallecillos-Ruiz et al.; arXiv preprint, no venue stated)
**Design.** 100-task SWE-bench Verified subset (AgentDiet's own tuning subset, l.~100). Two scaffolds (Trae agent, Mini-SWE-agent), ONE model, DeepSeek-V4-Flash, self-hosted vLLM, temperature 0, 200-turn cap. 21 deletion configs (scope {reasoning, tool output, both} x granularity {turn, token} x amount) x 2 timings {2-turn delay, immediate} = 42 configs per agent, plus 5 uncompressed runs per agent = 4,700 runs/agent, 9,400 total. One run per config per task. Deletion is random (no learned selector).
**Metrics.** Cost is NOT billed: it is token counts from vLLM logs, priced post hoc (l.~203-213). Flat = every prompt token at input price. Caching-aware = longest identical prefix at cached price; price ratio input:cached:output = 1:0.1:5; assumes every matching prefix is a hit ("lower bound on realistic costs"); cache-write premium and minimum prefix length ignored. Baseline cost split: cached 50%, new 14%, output 36% (Mini); 52/14/34 (Trae). Cost was computed only for delayed configs in Table II (immediate costs reported in text only).
**Headline results.**
- Delayed: 37/42 evaluations show no significant solve-rate loss; 21/42 significantly longer runs (all tool-output deletion). Immediate: 19/42 significant solve-rate loss, 34/42 longer runs (Table I, Section IV.B). Immediate numbers are vs the uncompressed baseline (not paired against delayed except the "16 of 42" timing comparison).
- Cost, delayed (Section VI.A, l.~473-480): flat pricing below baseline in 26/42 evaluations, but only 6 significantly cheaper and 3 significantly MORE expensive; caching-aware ratio >1 in all 42, significantly in 26; closest to parity +5% (Table II min 1.03).
- Tool output, turn-level 100%, delayed (Table II): Mini flat 0.71 / cache-aware 1.20; Trae 1.01 / 1.67. Tool-output rows range 1.03-1.70 cache-aware.
- Clean cache-only evidence: delayed reasoning deletion barely changes behaviour (re-fetch 0.7-1.2x, turns about same) yet cache-aware cost is 1.03-1.48 (all 14 Trae ratios >1, 6 significant): re-billing offsets token reduction (l.~488-492, 540-548).
**Assessment checks.**
- "cheaper in 26 of 42 under flat, 0 of 42 under cache-aware; 9,400 runs": ACCURATE as point estimates, but OVERSTATES flat-pricing savings: only 6/42 significantly cheaper (3 significantly dearer). "42 setups" are the delayed configs (21 configs x 2 agents), not all 84 agent-config pairs; the 9,400 runs include the immediate arm and 10 baseline sets. One model only.
- "tool-output deletion with 2-turn delay cost 1.20x (mini) to 1.67x (Trae) vs 0.71x and 1.01x flat": ACCURATE for ONE cell (tool output, turn-level, 100%); this is the worst-flat-vs-cache contrast row, not representative of all tool-output rows (1.03-1.70).
- "cache-write premiums ignored": ACCURATE (l.~255, 600). "so real Anthropic bills would be worse": NOT-FOUND-IN-TEXT as a claim; the paper says relative effect "need not be equal". Plausible by mechanism (more fresh tokens get written) [I] but not shown.
**Generalisation.** Mechanism transfers; multipliers do not (ratio 0.1, perfect hits, 30-40 turn SWE tasks, one model). At the thesis ratio ~0.2 the cache penalty is smaller, behavioural penalty is task dependent; 10-13 requests leave little prefix to lose or save [I].

## 2. 2610.34763 Physical Cost of Context (Yu et al.; NeurIPS 2026 Workshop: Resource-Aware Agentic AI; v2)
**Design.** Mostly a localization study (450 SWE-rebench-V2 cases for quality; 100 synthetic tasks x 3 repeats for resources; Qwen3.6-35B-A3B on vLLM, one DGX Spark, one request at a time). The Mask-5 result is a separate "external patch study": mini-SWE-agent v2.4.6, 100 SWE-bench Verified tasks, a different model/stack (Qwen3.8-27B-FP8, SGLang RadixAttention + DFlash speculative decoding, App. A Table 3), 32-call cap (55-60% of trajectories call-capped, Table 2), ONE trajectory per task per arm, 8 shards. Arms: Mini, Eager (10 retrieved blocks preloaded), Compact (single anchor rewrite), Mask-5 (SWE-agent LastNObservations; masking starts at median call 6).
**Results (Table 2, Section 4.3, l.~385-395).** Mask-5 vs Eager: prompt -33.3%, uncached +608.5% (9.8k->70.8k vs Mini baseline; Eager 10.0k->70.8k), energy +125.6%, wall +36.9%, calls +2.6%; resolved 26/100 vs 29/100 (Eager) and 30 (Mini); paired delta -3.0, 95% CI lower bound -8.6, fails the non-inferiority gate but the interval does NOT exclude zero. Cached share ~96% (Eager) vs 53% (Mask-5); Mask-5 removes 75.1k logical tokens, loses 135.9k hit tokens. Break-even (l.~406-411): uncached change = -75.1k + 135.9k*rho, "turns positive above rho ~ 0.55", rho = fraction of the OBSERVED hits retained in both arms.
**Assessment checks.**
- "cut prompt tokens 33%, raised uncached prefill 608%, solved 26 vs 29 of 100": ACCURATE numbers; OVERSTATES if read as a solve-rate loss (CI [-8.6,+2.6] approx., includes 0; a gate failure, not a demonstrated loss), and the comparison arm is Eager (preloaded retrieval), baseline Mini solved 30.
- "masking pays only when cache hit rate is below about 0.55": INACCURATE wording. rho is a scaling of the observed hit tokens, not the cache hit rate; the observed Eager hit share is 96%, so rho=0.55 corresponds to a hit share of about 53%. Uncached tokens are also not dollars. My derivation [I] in dollars with cached price c (fraction of fresh): delta input cost = +60.8k - 135.9k*c (fresh-token units), break-even c* = 0.447. At c=0.2: Mask-5 input bill ~ +63% vs Eager (10.0+0.2*215.6=53.1k vs 70.8+0.2*79.7=86.7k); at c=0.1 ~ +149%. So for the thesis ratio, masking loses in this setup; but the break-even cache price ratio (0.45) is the figure that should be cited, not "0.55 hit rate".
- "single anchored compaction about neutral": MOSTLY ACCURATE. Patch study: Compact vs Eager resolved 30 vs 29, prompt -6.5%, uncached +22%, energy -2% (no CI quoted in text). Localization (cache off): Compact uses +8.5% prompt, +13.3% calls, +9.4% energy vs Eager; cache-on Compact-Eager interaction -7.1k uncached, CI [-17.3,+2.3] includes 0. "Saves nothing further" is the author phrase; "neutral" slightly generous (it is worse on calls in localization).

Generalisation (2610.34763): mechanism strong (hit share 96%->53%), numbers not transferable (local radix cache, 32-call cap, no provider pricing). Statistics: paired bootstrap over repos/shards; gate = lower 95% bound > -5.

## 3. 2606.17016 TokenPilot (Xu et al., Zhejiang Univ.; arXiv v2 Aug 2026)
**Design.** PinchBench (123 tasks) and Claw-Eval, isolated and continuous modes; GPT-5.4-mini backbone for all methods; baselines include Keep-Last-N, Summary, LCM, Pichay, MemoBrain, AgentSwing, MemOS, LLMLingua-2, SelectiveContext; Vanilla = OpenClaw default (window 500k, compaction trigger 0.5). TokenPilot = (i) ingestion-aware compaction (stable placeholders for volatile prefix fields, tool definitions moved out of the prefix, HTML/log truncation with recovery tool) + (ii) lifecycle eviction every B turns using a Qwen3.5-35B-A3B estimator.
**Metrics.** Real API metadata for cache hit/miss tokens (l.~130), cost = hit*$0.075/M + miss*$0.75/M + out*$4.50/M (ratio 0.1; Appendix A.2). GPT-5.4-mini has no cache-write fee, so "cache-write not accounted" is moot for this provider but means nothing about Anthropic-style billing.
**Statistics.** None found: no repeats, CIs, or tests (grep for repeat/CI/trial: nothing). Single run per cell. Categories scores are noisy (category swings of 10-30 points between methods).
**Results.** Table 1/2: costs Vanilla->TokenPilot: PinchBench iso 8.31->3.22 (-61%), Claw iso 5.16->2.27 (-56%), PinchBench cont 7.24->2.79 (-61%), Claw cont 81.52->10.58 (-87%). Accuracy: 80.5->81.0; 64.5->63.1; 79.2->81.3; 63.4->60.8 (-2.6). B ablation (Figure 6) is on ONE category (Meeting Analysis) of PinchBench in continuous mode, B in {1,3,5,7,9,inf}; B=3 chosen as "empirical optimum" balancing accuracy, memory and call time, no uncertainty shown.
**Decomposition (adversarial).** Table 4: simply replacing volatile fields with placeholders cuts Vanilla from $8.31 to $4.35 (-48%); reduction pass to $2.87. Table 3 (continuous): global level 7.24->4.22 (-42%), local eviction 4.22->2.79 (-34% of the remainder). So most of the headline savings come from fixing a cache-hostile Vanilla (timestamps/paths/tool schemas in the prefix; Vanilla cache hit 38.7% PinchBench, Figure 4), not from batched eviction. Claw-Eval continuous Vanilla (709.8M hit tokens, $81.52) is an extreme baseline. In Table 3, local eviction cut cache-hit tokens 65% yet cost fell.
Keep-Last-N (last 5 turns, triggered at 40% of 500k, i.e. threshold-triggered) also cut real-priced cost ~50% (8.31->4.26 PinchBench iso; 5.16->2.54 Claw iso); only the B=1 ablation is per-step.

**Assessment checks.**
- "eviction every B turns; B=3 best of 1,3,5,7,9 and never": ACCURATE as stated by authors but weak: one category, no uncertainty, B=3 is "empirical optimum" by a joint criterion; also B=inf was the "never" arm (peak cost).
- "cuts cost 56-87% at roughly equal accuracy": ACCURATE vs Vanilla, but attributable largely to prefix stabilization/ingestion compaction (see above); equal accuracy within -2.6 to +2.1 with no CIs. OVERSTATED as evidence for eviction timing specifically.
- "cache-write costs not accounted": ACCURATE (no write term in Eq. 11) but irrelevant to GPT-5.4-mini billing.

Venue [U]: TokenPilot "EMNLP-26 Findings" NOT-FOUND-IN-TEXT. Generalisation: stabilise the prefix and evict in batches transfer; long multi-task sessions do not.

## 4. 2509.23586 AgentDiet (Xiao et al., FSE 2026)
**Metrics.** "$" = tokens priced with the per-model table "considering the discount for tokens in KV Cache" (l.~673); the table (Table 1) lists only reflection-model prices, not the agent models (Claude 4 Sonnet/Gemini 2.5 Pro) [U]; how cached tokens were determined (API usage vs simulation) is not stated [U]; cache-write premium not mentioned. Original costs $0.535, $0.385, $1.277, $0.701 per instance.
**Results (Table 4, l.~865).** Input tokens -39.9..-59.7%; agent cost "$" -28.6..-44.1%; incl. reflection overhead "$+" -21.1..-35.9%. Pass%: 64.5->66.5, 50.5->52.0, 40.0->39.0, 21.7->22.7. Gemini+MSbF steps 57.2->43.9 because the reduction avoids degenerate long-context loops. Table 2 (hyper-search): Delete-all baseline $=0.655 (cost -35%) at Pass 58 vs 65, Random 0.794, steps +13%.
**Assessment checks.**
- "edits only step s-2 which leaves earlier cache intact": ACCURATE in mechanism (l.~490-493): only a fixed recent step is modified, so prefix before s-2 stays cached; the paper itself says the $ saving is smaller than the token saving "due to costs related to output tokens and invalidated KV Caches" (l.~865). Per call, the last ~3 steps are re-billed. Also steps shorter than 500 tokens or with no reduction are skipped, so most calls do not break the prefix.
- "cost -21% to -36%": ACCURATE (21.1-35.9%, including reflection overhead). Cost = estimated from token counts and a price table with cache discount, not invoices (as best determined) [U on cache accounting].
- "pass rate within -1 to +2 points": ACCURATE numerically; no statistical support.
**Disagreement with 2610.35496 [I].** AgentDiet reports net savings; Hidden Costs, re-running its design (delay 2, same 100 tasks) with random deletion, finds none. Likely reasons: AgentDiet skips steps <500 tokens and uses an LLM selector (less re-fetch); different agents/cache discounts (0.1/0.25); undocumented cache accounting.

## 5. 2510.00615 ACON (Kang et al., Microsoft/others)
**Design.** AppWorld test-normal 168 tasks (also OfficeBench, 8-objective QA); ReAct agent gpt-4.1 (also others); compressor gpt-4.1 or distilled Qwen3-14B/8B, Phi-4; temperature 0, seed 42; single run, no CI.
**Metrics.** Peak input tokens (1e3), "Dependency" (sum of squared context lengths proxy, 1e6), steps. API cost (Table 4) = "estimated per task with a gpt-4.1 agent and Qwen3-14B compressor ... excluding input caching" (l.~616-622). Price table (App. B.2): gpt-4.1 $3/M in, $12/M out as stated in the paper (this does not match OpenAI's published gpt-4.1 list price as I recall [U]).
**Results.** Table 1 (agent gpt-4.1, compressor gpt-4.1): No compression 56.0 acc, 16.14 steps, peak 9.93k, dep 5.96M; ACON UTCO history 56.5, 22.82 steps, peak 7.33k, dep 4.69M; FIFO 45.8, LLMLingua 39.3, Prompting 43.5. Table 4: cost $0.331 -> $0.285 (history) / $0.272 (observation); latency 73.24s -> 87.68s (+19.7%) / 101.92s.
**Assessment checks.**
- Accuracy/peak figures 56.0/9.93k vs 56.5/7.33k and naive baselines 45.8/39.3: ACCURATE (Table 1). The difference 56.0 vs 56.5 is 1 task of 168 and unqualified by any CI.
- "$0.331 ... vs $0.285 (14% cheaper)": ACCURATE number but MIXES CONFIGURATIONS. $0.285 is for the Qwen3-14B-compressor setup; the 56.5%/7.33k row uses a gpt-4.1 compressor. The paper's distilled Qwen3-14B history compressor (Table 13) scores 50.0% (not 56.5%; the 56.5% Qwen3-14B row is OBSERVATION compression). The paper states the gpt-4.1 compressor costs $0.045/example (App.), which is about the whole $0.046 saving, so the 56.5% configuration would show about zero net saving [I]. Table 4 reports no accuracy.
- "latency +20%": the +19.7% was measured with a Qwen3-14B AGENT and compressor locally on an A100 (l.~612-622), not the gpt-4.1 agent.
- "appendix says history compression invalidates the KV cache": ACCURATE (App. A, l.~1034-1037: "history compression typically invalidates the existing KV-cache, necessitating a costly re-computation"), asserted, not measured; cost explicitly excludes input caching.

Generalisation (ACON): same benchmark as the thesis but cost is flat/no-cache; supports only that guideline-optimised compression keeps AppWorld accuracy while naive compression hurts. Omitted by assessment: steps rise 16.1->22.8.

## 6. 2510.04618 ACE (Zhang et al., ICLR 2026)
**Design.** ReAct on AppWorld, base LLM DeepSeek-V3.1-671B (non-thinking), same model as Reflector/Curator; offline adaptation (with ground-truth labels) and online adaptation; baselines ICL, GEPA, Dynamic Cheatsheet.
**Results (Table 1).** ReAct 42.4 average -> ReAct+ACE 59.4 (offline with GT labels, +17.0); without GT labels 57.2; online without labels 59.5 (average over test-normal/test-challenge TGC and SGC, not a single accuracy).
**Cache claim (Section 4.7, l.~597-611; App. A.3, l.~1139).** "In our prompt-caching study with the OpenAI API (GPT-5.1), ACE achieves high cache reuse: 91.8% of input tokens are served from cache during evaluation stage, which reduces billed input-token cost by 82.6% relative to counting raw context tokens."
**Assessment checks.**
- "42.4 -> 59.4": ACCURATE; omit that it is offline adaptation with labels, DeepSeek-V3.1, 4-metric average.
- "because the playbook is stable 91.8% hit the cache, cutting billed input by 82.6%": MISLEADING. (1) It is not an ACE-vs-baseline result. The 82.6% is ACE's own billed input vs ACE's raw tokens priced entirely uncached; arithmetic check: 1-(0.082+0.918*r)=0.826 gives r=0.100, i.e. the OpenAI 0.1 cached price applied to the hit share [I]. No cache study of the base ReAct is reported. (2) Evaluation-stage input tokens: Base ReAct 27.46M vs ACE 58.62M (+113.5%; 171.6k vs 366.4k per query; Tables 14-15). ACE roughly doubles raw input vs ReAct; if ReAct's hit share is similar (an append-only loop also hits the cache) ACE is still dearer in bill [I]. (3) The 91.8% was measured with GPT-5.1, which is not the DeepSeek base of Table 1. (4) "Because the playbook is stable" is the authors' framing; any append-only ReAct loop hits the cache. (5) Measurement protocol not described [U].



Generalisation (ACE): shows a stable prefix is cheap (relevant to 469 tool defs) but not that ACE lowers the bill; online ACE rewrites the playbook between samples.

## 7. 2508.21433 The Complexity Trap (Lindenbauer et al.)
**Design.** SWE-agent (+OpenHands check) on SWE-bench Verified (n=500), 5 model configs: Qwen3-32B (thinking, non-thinking), Qwen3-Coder-480B, Gemini 2.5 Flash (thinking, non-thinking). Raw agent vs Observation Masking (rolling window M=10, placeholder) vs LLM-Summary (N=21, M=10); turn cap 250. Hyperparameters tuned on 150-sample subset. Paired bootstrap, 95% CI, 10,000 resamples (Table 4).
**Metrics.** Cost: Gemini via Vertex "cost returned by the API"; Qwen self-hosted, post-hoc cost from "official Alibaba API pricing"; the 32B tariff "does not distinguish between cache hit and miss input tokens, leading to an inflated cost" (l.~776-782). Qwen3-Coder cache treatment is not stated [U]. Cache-write not discussed.
**Results (Table 1/4).** Qwen3-Coder 480B: Raw 53.4 solve, $1.29; Masking 54.8 (+1.4 pp, CI [-1.6,4.4]), $0.61 (-52.7%); Summary 53.8, $0.64 (-50.4%). Gemini 2.5 Flash: masking -56.1% cost, +2.8 pp (CI includes 0); Gemini thinking: masking -57.1% cost, solve -4.0 pp (CI [-7.8,-0.2], significant) and Summary -9.0 pp. Qwen3-32B thinking: cost change n.s. (-9.8%/0%). Trajectory elongation (Section 4.4): Gemini Flash Summary 52 turns = +15% vs Masking (44), +4% vs Raw (50); Qwen3-Coder +15% vs Raw, +13% vs Masking. Summary generation = up to 7.2% of cost, with poor cache reuse (Section 5.2). Hybrid with M=W=10 "degrades cost efficiency due to compounding KV cache inefficiencies" (l.~548-551).
**Assessment checks.**
- "masking halved cost (about 52% cheaper with Qwen3-Coder) with solve rate 53.8 to 54.8": cost ACCURATE (-52.7%). Solve rate WRONG baseline: 53.8 is LLM-Summary; Raw is 53.4 -> 54.8 (+1.4 pp, n.s.). In 3/5 setups the cheapest strategy had higher solve rate, in the Gemini-thinking setup masking significantly LOST 4 points.
- "LLM summarisation lengthened trajectories 13-15%": PARTLY ACCURATE; baselines differ (vs Raw: +15% Qwen3-Coder, only +4% Gemini; vs Masking +13%/+15%). Two models only.
- "priced without a cache split": PARTLY WRONG. True for Qwen3-32B; Gemini costs are API-returned (cache-aware), and masking still saved 56-57% there. This is direct evidence against "per-step masking loses money under cache pricing".
**Interpretation [I].** Rolling masking with window M re-bills about M turns per call but removes all older observations. In a crude model (obs t tokens/turn, fresh price 1, cached c) masked call k costs about M*t vs c*(k-1)*t + t unmasked, so masking pays once k exceeds about M/c: M=2 (Hidden Costs delay), c=0.1 gives k>20; M=5,c=0.2 gives k>25; M=10,c=0.25 (Gemini) gives k>40. SWE-agent trajectories average 44-50 turns with long tails to 250 and 84% observation tokens, so masking pays at M=10; at 30-40 turns with M=2-5 behaviour effects dominate. For the thesis (10-13 requests, c about 0.2, M>=2) k stays below break-even for all but the smallest windows, so per-step masking should not pay [I; ignores system/tool prefix, output and behaviour changes].

## Cross-question: do the papers support "per-step clearing/masking loses money under cache pricing; only batched/ingestion-time approaches have (unreplicated) positive evidence"?
Partly. Supported for SHORT-WINDOW, per-step rewrites on trajectories of ~30-40 turns: Hidden Costs (1.03-1.70x, 0/42 cheaper, clean reasoning-only cache effect) and Physical Cost (uncached +608%; hit share 96%->53%; dollar break-even c*=0.447 by my derivation). Not supported as a general claim:
1. Complexity Trap: rolling per-step masking (M=10) cut API-returned Gemini cost 56-57% and self-hosted Qwen3-Coder cost 53% (Qwen cache treatment [U]); this is per-step masking with positive evidence, on long trajectories.
2. TokenPilot: threshold-triggered Keep-Last-N cut real hit/miss-priced cost ~50% in isolated mode; batch eviction (B=3) is a small share of its own total saving versus prefix stabilization.
3. AgentDiet: fixed s-2 edit with size threshold gives net savings; cache accounting undocumented, no CIs, unreplicated. It is neither batched nor ingestion-time; it is "bounded-suffix edit".
So the split that the evidence supports is not per-step vs batched but: (a) rewrite distance from the tail (suffix re-billed per call) relative to trajectory length and cache ratio; (b) trigger rule (size threshold, window long enough); (c) whether the baseline's prefix is already stable. "Only batched/ingestion-time approaches have positive evidence" is OVERSTATED; "ingestion-time prefix stabilisation" (TokenPilot) is a single-run, no-CI result on GPT-5.4-mini.
**Why they disagree.** Cache assumptions (perfect-hit 0.1 / API-returned / no split / undocumented), window or delay (2 / 5 / 10 / threshold), trajectory length (30-50 vs thesis 10-13), selector (random / LLM / positional), model, and whether behavioural side effects (re-fetch, elongation) were measured (only 35496, 21433).

## Recommendation impact
- Reword the headline: per-step rewriting with short windows loses on short trajectories under cache pricing; at the thesis scale (10-13 requests, c about 0.2) the break-even trajectory length for window M is roughly M/c (>=10 even for M=2) [I], so expect no gain. Do not claim that all masking loses or that batched approaches are proven.
- Thesis action: measure it (cache-read/write/fresh split in the PoC loop); none of the papers separates cache-write premium (all ignore it or use OpenAI-type billing).
