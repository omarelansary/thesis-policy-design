### D2. The break-even model is mis-derived and then said to "fit the published results" (7.2 row 6; 8.1 P4; 9.3 bullet 2)
My derivation (unit observation s, cache-read ratio c, rolling window M; masked turn t>M re-bills the whole window M*s fresh because the edit sits early in the prefix; unmasked turn costs s(1+c(t-1))):
- Per-turn: masking cheaper when t > ~M/c (the doc's number). CUMULATIVE break-even (what matters for a bill): loss accumulated over M<t<M/c is M^2(1-c)^2/(2c)s, gain afterwards is (c/2)(N-M/c)^2 s, so N* = M(2-c)/c, about 1.8-1.9x the doc's M/c. Brute-force check (be.py): M=10: c=0.1 -> N*=172 (not 100); c=0.2 -> 82 (not 50). M=3, c=0.186 -> 20.
- With imperfect hits it moves a lot: c_eff = h*c+(1-h). Copilot's 90% within-turn hit (2608.00101 l.30) gives c_eff ~0.19 (consistent with the doc); at h=55% (across turns) c_eff~0.5, N*=28-30 for M=10; with no cache (c=1, which the doc itself lists as the top of the 0.1-1.0 range, row 4) masking pays from t>M. Cache-write fees (1.25-2.5x) push N* up on Anthropic/OpenAI routes.
- "The published results fit this order of magnitude" is FALSE on the doc's own example: Complexity Trap (2508.21433 l.369: Gemini 2.5 Flash raw 0.41 USD -> masking 0.18, -56%; l.441-442 mean 44 vs 50 turns; Gemini cost "returned by the API", l.775-776) halves the bill at 44-50 turns with M=10, where the model predicts a LOSS (N*~90-170 at c=0.1-0.2). So the observed API cache behaviour is not the c=0.1 perfect-hit case, i.e. the model's inputs are wrong for that stack. Physical Cost (2610.34763 l.390-411) masks at median call 6 and loses on a local prefix cache; its own first-order result turns positive when little of the lost hits are re-served (rho<0.55). Hidden Costs (2610.35496 l.39-41, 84-88): 26 of 42 are cheaper under flat pricing. Pattern: masking pays when effective c is high, loses when c is low. The doc's "cannot pay here" is true only for c_eff<=~0.2 and M>=~2.
- What survives: at 10-13 requests, M=10 cannot pay (almost nothing is masked). At c~0.19 (GLM endpoint mode) no M>=2 pays within 13 turns (N*=M*1.81/0.186 >=19). So for the thesis' routes with decent caches the conclusion holds; for no-cache or poor-cache routes it does not.
- Replace 9.3 bullet 2 last sentences: "Per turn, masking wins once t > M/c; cumulatively it needs about (2-c)M/c turns (172 for M=10, c=0.1). With c_eff = h*c+(1-h) the threshold falls to ~30 turns at a 55% hit rate and to M turns with no cache. The model does not reproduce Complexity Trap's halving at 44-50 turns on Gemini; I take that as evidence that the cache behaviour there was not c=0.1 with full hits, not as agreement." Replace 7.2 row 6 meaning: "...so per-step clearing is not expected to pay on routes with c<=0.2 and hit rate >=90% at 10-13 requests. On routes with weak caching (flat pricing, evictions) it can." Replace 8.2 item 4 "Do not run per-step clearing at 10-13 requests" by "Do not run per-step clearing at 10-13 requests on cache-priced routes (c<=0.2); revisit if a route has no or weak caching."

### D3. "8x longer real sessions" compares unlike units (top note; 7.2 row 5; 8.1 row 4; 9.3 bullet 4; 8.2 item 2)
TraceLab (2606.30560): ~84 steps is steps PER SESSION (357,161 steps / 4,265 sessions; derived, heavy-tailed: p99 137 requests, l.90-98); the paper says "to solve one request, an agent on average takes around 8 steps with 11 tool calls" (l.240-243). An AppWorld task (10-13 requests) is the analogue of a TraceLab REQUEST (8 steps), not of a session, and sessions span human gaps (hit rate falls after >5 min, l.809-813). "Our tasks are 8x shorter than real sessions" is a unit mismatch; per task the lengths are similar. Also 4,265 sessions come from 43 users (l.201). The 55%-60% "sessions are long so the tool block is small" argument also mixes this: Copilot's 14% system-prompt figure excludes tool definitions (the doc notes it) and says nothing about tool-heavy catalogues of 469 tools.
Replace: "TraceLab sessions average ~84 LLM steps over several user requests (~8 steps per request). Our 10-13-request tasks resemble one request; the cross-request cache decay and history growth of long sessions are not represented." Delete "about 8x longer" everywhere.

### D4. "Tool definitions about 55% of the all-tools bill" (9.2) is understated and rests on two incompatible token counts
Recomputed from the traces (an.py; recorded prices; cache allocated prefix-first as in the doc): fixed-prefix cache reads = 66% (MM 3.04 USD) and 66% (GLM 10.58 USD) CONFIRMED; plus fresh prefix 6% / 1% -> prefix = 72% / 67% of the bill. Definition share of the first request: R1 ScaDS counts scaled 457->469 give 65.6k/76.1k = 86% (MM) and 62.6k/72.9k = 86% (GLM). Differencing against the subset arm's agent request (P0 = 5.2k / 4.2k tokens including ~9 tools, so tool-free base ~4k) gives ~72k = 94%. The 6k-token gap is a tokenizer/template difference between ScaDS and OpenRouter-GMICloud, not noise. The doc multiplies 86-94% by 66% only (cache-read part): 57-62%. Applying to the whole prefix: MM 62-68%, GLM 58-63%. So "roughly 55-60%" should be "roughly 58-68% (86-94% of a prefix that is 67-72% of the bill)". Also: the 66% is with a warm cross-task cache (first request cached 63k of 76k MM, 69k of 73k GLM); a cold start raises it. Direction is fine, the number is soft. The "tool-definition share" is not the saving (the subset arm re-adds ~4k base, 9 defs, predictor 4%, and 38-41% output).
Also verify-note: summing glm-b2-control + -rest in the raw dirs gave 58 tasks / 7.26 USD, not 57 / 7.02 (one task appears in both); my splits match the doc's table otherwise (subset MM 10/6/25/17/38/4; GLM 5/2/49/6/35/4).

### D7. Evaluation-power numbers (7.2 row 10; 9.3 bullet 3) hold only as an upper-end assumption
Re-simulation (pw.py; 57 tasks, 19 clusters of 3; mid-probability tasks sized so expected identical-run disagreement d = sum 2p(1-p) = 6 or 12; 8 impossible tasks; arm-effect heterogeneity tau 0 or 0.15, cluster ICC 0 or 0.5; percentile cluster bootstrap over 19 clusters), 95% half-width of the paired difference, runs per arm 1/2/4:
- d=6: +-7.3-7.8 / +-5.4-5.8 / +-3.7-4.3 ; d=12: +-11.1-11.5 / +-7.9-8.3 / +-5.4-6.5.
- So "+-8 at 2 runs" is the d=12 end; at d=6 it is ~+-5.5. Cluster structure barely matters because task-level difficulty cancels in a paired design; what matters is d and treatment-effect heterogeneity. Cluster bootstrap with only 19 clusters is anti-coverage (a t-interval with 18 df would be ~7% wider); so the real half-widths are slightly larger than listed.
- "3-point margin needs ~130 scenarios at 2 runs": scales as (8/3)^2*19 = 135 for d=12, but only ~65 for d=6. State the range 65-135.
- "Identical-run disagreement implies an SD of 3-4 points" is the SD of ONE arm's pass rate (sqrt(sum p(1-p))/57 = sqrt(3..6)/57 = 3.0-4.3), not of the paired difference (4.3-6.2 at 1 run). Say which.
- "9 runs for 2 points, 36 for 1" CONFIRMED (2602.07150 l.633, Table 2 l.586-591) but it is for small open models (Qwen3-32B, DeepSWE-preview, Devstral-2) at 16-64% pass rates on 500 tasks; variance scales with p(1-p), AppWorld pass ~80% gives smaller SD per task; do not transfer without the caveat. The SD 0.7-1.8 and range 2.2-6.0 CONFIRMED (l.283-298).
- "Net gain of 5 passes with 9-13 flips is consistent with zero": 7:2 and 9:4 splits give exact p ~0.18 and ~0.27 CONFIRMED.
Replace 7.2 row 10 last clause: "...a paired, cluster-aware 95% interval at 57 tasks and 2 runs per arm is about +-5.5 to +-8 points depending on the identical-run disagreement (6-12 tasks), slightly wider with only 19 clusters; it excludes only losses beyond that."

## C. Unsupported generalisations / misleading comparisons
- Everything in section 2 (66%, 104%/129%) is two open-weight models, one route (GMICloud via OpenRouter), 57 dev tasks, one run per cell, warm cross-task cache. Section 9.2 row 4 "the share is route-specific" is right; yet the verdict table's "High: reproduced to the cent" speaks to arithmetic, not to generality.
- "Per-step clearing cannot pay at 10-13 requests" (generalised from c<=0.2, full hit).
- "Incomplete subsets raise the bill" (one code-agent preprint, tokens, wrong contrast).
- Tokens vs dollars: Physical Cost (GPU time), Quesma (tokens saved vs bill), 15.1M vs 7.4M (tokens), AppWorld-UL/AWO (user-interaction metric; model-specific). The doc flags most; the 15.1M/7.4M one it does not.
- Mixed agents: 2-of-39 (code agent, DeepSeek) is placed beside 13-of-19 (function-calling, MiniMax); doc admits not reconciled; the policy recommendation (keep supervisor functions always offered) leans on GLM 0-of-22 only, which is a single model's predictor behaviour.
- "Estimation-only reporting is the right choice": follows from noise only if the sample stays at 57 tasks; the doc's own numbers say 65-135 scenarios would make a 3-point margin testable. "Right" should be "forced by the current sample size".

## D. Final verdict
Defend: premise correct (re-send is real; no billing relief found); r (cache-read ratio) is the lever and varies ~40x; the 66% reproduction; bottleneck moves to history/output once the tool block shrinks; Hidden Costs / Physical Cost corrections (9.1 rows 2-3); ToolChoiceConfusion and Bai (30x -> 2x, not re-checked by me) corrections; harness-base 74.7% cannot be a tool-definition share; Exgentic/AppWorld-UL downgrades; "pilot first" for the code arm (as a cost-capping choice).
Soften: P4 "cannot pay at 10-13" (conditional on c<=0.2, high hit rate, and M>=~2); tool-definition share (58-68%, soft); "8x longer" (drop); "estimation-only is right" (forced by n); power numbers (give ranges); "nothing found removes the charge" (add vendor mechanisms with no cache-priced evidence); TheMCPCompany steps.
Retract: "an incomplete set also raises the bill" (top note, 8.1, 9.1 r5 effect, 9.3 b1, 7.2 r7 and its derived "predictor misses are a cost" rationale) until measured on stored R2/R3 records; "published results fit this order of magnitude" for the break-even model; the M/c figure as the break-even (use (2-c)M/c cumulative).
