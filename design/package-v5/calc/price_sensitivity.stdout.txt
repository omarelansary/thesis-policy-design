Reading (from the tables above):
  1. Over the whole observed price range, n* at T = 10 stays within 11-13 (D=1), 20-24 (D=3) and 52-65 (D=10): the break-even depends on
     how many searches a session makes, not on which price ratios apply.
  2. At T = 3 the direction reverses: a weaker cache discount (higher r) RAISES n* (D=10: about 71-99 at r=0.14, 118-134 at r=1.0), because
     every client-side search is an extra request that re-reads the history at r.
  3. The write premium w (1.0 to 1.25) moves n* by up to about 8 tools and the output ratio o (3.1 to 7.5) by up to about 20 (both at T=3, D=10); at T=10 by at most about 11.
  4. The seed break-even h* is likewise driven by session length and matches per search K, not by prices: K=3 gives 0.10-0.25 for T <= 10 and
     0.34-0.57 for T = 30-100; K=1 gives 0.12-0.35 for T <= 10, 0.57-0.77 at T = 30 and 'never' at T = 100, at every price in the range.
  Absolute dollar prices do not enter; only the ratios r, w, o do (design/package-v5/11-price-ranges.md). The arithmetic does not depend on the model.
