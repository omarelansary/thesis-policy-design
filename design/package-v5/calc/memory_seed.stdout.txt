Part 1: break-even hit rate h* of memory-seeded tools (seeding pays when more than h* of them are needed)
  discovery mode client
       r T=3      T=10     T=30     T=100   
     0.1 0.23     0.32     0.54     0.99    
    0.05 0.25     0.31     0.45     0.80    
   0.025 0.27     0.30     0.39     0.62    
  discovery mode server
       r T=3      T=10     T=30     T=100   
     0.1 0.33     0.45     0.72     never   
    0.05 0.31     0.38     0.54     0.92    
   0.025 0.30     0.34     0.43     0.68    
Reading: a seed must be needed in roughly a third (client-side search, short sessions) to a half (server-side
search) of returning sessions to pay; in long sessions with r = 0.1 it may never pay, because a needed seed
is carried longer than the tool it replaces. Lower read prices (0.025) favour seeding.

Reading: against re-sending, caching saves most of the cost after a few requests; against not adding the
tokens at all, the cost grows with every request (0.1 of a fresh send each) and is paid again after a gap.
