Reading: the break-even depends on how many matches a search appends (K) as much as on session length. With
K = 1, seeding needs a third to a half of seeds used in short sessions and may never pay in long ones at r = 0.1;
with K = 3 it needs about a fifth to a half. The arithmetic does not settle whether seeding pays: it gives the
threshold that P1 must compare the measured precision h with, using the observed K, T, history size and mode.

Reading: against re-sending, caching saves most of the cost after a few requests; against not adding the
tokens at all, the cost grows with every request (0.1 of a fresh send each) and is paid again after a gap.
