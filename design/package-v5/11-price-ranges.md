## 2. The three ratios that decide the bill's structure [F, ratios [D]]

|---|---|---|---|

**Scope of these ranges.** They are the span over the provider pages that were fetched (DeepSeek, Z.ai, MiniMax, Alibaba, Groq, Fireworks, Together, DeepInfra, Baseten, Cerebras, CoreWeave), not over every host of these models (Artificial Analysis lists 16, 26 and 12 hosts for gpt-oss-120b, GLM-5.3 and MiniMax-M3). Stored text holds points outside them: Fireworks's size-based price for unlisted models is one price for input and output (o = 1.0, no cache discount); one aggregator row for GLM-5.3 (Makora, "$0.14 - $0.10 - $4.40", reading r = 0.71 and o = 31) is unattributed and unresolved (it may be a typo for $1.40). The D-04 grid is a convenience span, not a bound.
Reasoning tokens bill as output [F] (Alibaba and Z.ai pages), the same as DPR-0006 and as the university API reports them (`10` section 2). The frontier vendors of DPR-0006 (FND-0039) have r of 0.1 to 0.025 and w of 1.25 to 2.0. Open-weight hosts in the stored set span a **wider** r range (0.02 to 0.50, some hosts list none) and mostly list no write fee; Alibaba explicit mode has the same w (1.25) as the frontier five-minute class. The 7:2:1 weighting of the benchmark blends is discussed in section 5a and `13` section 4.

## 3. Documented cache rules that the arithmetic leaves out [F]
- **Minimum cacheable length** (stated by Groq, MiniMax, Alibaba, OpenAI and Anthropic; none found on the stored Z.ai and DeepSeek pages): Groq 128-1,024 tokens, MiniMax 512, Alibaba 1,024, OpenAI 1,024 (GPT-5.6 and later), Anthropic 512-4,096 by model. The scripts' core prefix is 1,550 tokens: above most of these, below some. An uncached core is the variant already computed in D-01.
- **Lifetime**: Groq 2 hours, Alibaba explicit 5 minutes (implicit unstated), Z.ai "reasonable time limits", DeepSeek "a few hours to a few days"; the university models were measured at 75 minutes to 5 hours (`10` section 9).
- **Hit rate is a measured quantity per provider**, not a constant (Artificial Analysis states that routing "can materially change effective cost"; `13` section 4). The scripts assume that a repeated prefix hits; that is their stated simplification.
- **Time-of-day price**: DeepSeek's page doubles every price at peak hours on weekdays (off-peak is half). The ratios are unchanged, but dollar levels carry a 2x factor that the cases below do not show.
- **Block granularity**: only full blocks are cached, so a prefix just over a block boundary loses up to one block (`13` section 3).

## 5. What the ranges do to the V5 arithmetic (D-04, `calc/price_sensitivity.py`) [D]
D-04 covers **r 0.02 to 1.0, w 1.0 and 1.25, o 2.1 to 7.5** (output in `calc/price_sensitivity.stdout.txt`; three cells recomputed by hand by the challenger, RV-0024). Everything else is fixed as in the script header: core 1,550 tokens above the minimum cacheable length, definition size d = 250, history H = 5,000, search output q = 150, a warm cache that always hits.
- **Long enough sessions (T = 10): the regime break-even depends mainly on searches per session.** Deferral beats exposing every tool above 11-14 tools with one search, 19-28 with three, and 47-77 with ten. Over r alone (w = 1.0, o = 5) n* moves at most 1.2x (D = 10: 65 to 54); over D it moves about 5x (12 to 60).
- **Short sessions (T = 3): the cache ratio matters.** n* rises with r by 1.5x to 1.65x for one to three searches (D = 3: 26 to 43) and about 2x for ten searches (50-85 at r 0.02, 97-121 at r 0.5, 117-134 at r 1.0), because every client-side search is an extra request that re-reads the history at r. Cells with more searches than requests (D = 10 at T = 3) are not a plausible session; they only show the direction.
- **w and o move n* by a few tools** where searches do not exceed requests: w (1.0 to 1.25) by at most 7 tools (T = 10) and 3 (T = 3); o (2.1 to 7.5) by up to 27 (T = 10, D = 10, r 0.02) and 9 (T = 3, D up to 3).
- **The seed break-even with three matches per search depends on session length and little on r:** 0.08-0.21 up to 10 requests, 0.21-0.55 for 30-100. **With one match per search and 30 or more requests, whether seeding pays depends on r:** at 100 requests it is 0.54 (r 0.02), 0.94 (r 0.10) and never for r of 0.14 and above (hand check: saving 180 at r 0.10, -48 at r 0.14). The correction found by the literature review is included: an avoided client-side search also saves the core-prefix re-read.
- **Consequence (narrow):** over the varied ratios, V5's break-evens move mostly with searches per session, matches per search and session length, except in short sessions and in one-match long sessions where r also matters. This does **not** show that the design is independent of model or host: minimum cacheable length (an uncached-core variant exists in D-01 but not in D-04), cache lifetime (a returning user after a gap meets a cold cache on short-lived hosts), the token size of a definition and the hit rate are host properties that D-04 does not vary. It is a computed result, not a published one (`13` section 4).

## 5a. Benchmark blends (wording corrected after RV-0024)
- Artificial Analysis blends prices at **7:2:1 cache-hit:input:output**, labelled "General agentic (recommended)", and says it is "assuming" that ratio; no basis is stated. It is an **assumed weighting**, not a measured mix. Other sources measure a cache-read share of input tokens of 46-92% (Requesty: cached_tokens / input_tokens) or of all tokens of about 60-90% (OpenRouter, a sweep, one agent study); AA's 70% (78% of input) lies inside either span. Their output share is about 0.5-1% of tokens by different measures (a sweep, a newsletter, a mean run, and one vendor's 100:1 input-to-output ratio), against AA's 10% (`13` section 4). V5's per-session mixes are to be measured, not assumed.
- AA measures a cache hit rate per provider ("as reported by the provider's API", median of four weeks) but the stored page shows it only as charts [U].
- The figures "up to 8.9x, 21.6x, 4.4x" (gpt-oss-120b, GLM-5.3, MiniMax-M3) are spreads in the **blended 7:2:1 price** across hosts. For gpt-oss-120b the extremes (CoreWeave 0.044, Cerebras 0.39) both list no cache price, and the blend reproduces only if that price is taken as the input price, so nine tenths of the weight then sits on the input price (0.03 against 0.35). The spread says little about hosts that discount.

## 6. What this does not show
No price of the university's own service exists (it reports tokens only). Promotional and "limited-time" prices change. MiniMax-M3's write price and any gpt-oss-120b cache price on CoreWeave are not stated [U]. Hit rates, minimum lengths and lifetimes of the university route are measured only where `10` reports them. The r = 1.0 case, kept in D-04 as the no-discount extreme, is supported by the absence of a listed discount on several official pages, not by a printed price.
