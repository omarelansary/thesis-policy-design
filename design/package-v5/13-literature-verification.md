# 13. Literature verification of the price ranges, the cost equations and the cache mechanics (AUTH-020 amendment of 2026-10-27)

Status: provisional, lead, 2026-10-27. Done at the human's request (INT-T-012, REQ-U22-18): verify `11-price-ranges.md` and V5's equations by a multi-agent literature review with scientific and primary-source citations, not by an experiment. **Method:** four scouts (web discovery, no writes; prompts in `inputs/scout-prompts-literature-20261027.md`), the lead stored every page cited (about 60 pages, manifest rows with URL, time and hashes; files `evidence/literature-20261019/v5-lit-*`), three evidence-stewards checked the scouts' claims against the stored text (`findings/SUM-0045`, `SUM-0046`, `SUM-0047`; claim lists in `inputs/claims-batch-*-20261027.md`), and one challenger reviewed the result (`reviews/RV-0024.md`, section 7). Scouts and stewards are model work; no human has checked a row. No API call or experiment was made.

## 1. Result in brief
- **The price ranges in the first version of `11` were partly wrong, and are corrected in `11`.** It had used benchmark and aggregator pages. On the providers' own pages the cache-read ratio reaches down to 0.02 (not 0.14), no page prints a cache price equal to the input price (several list none, which is the no-discount case in practice), an official page gives a GLM-5.3 cache price I had marked "not found", and Qwen3.8-27B's official price differs by 44% from the one I used.
- **The break-even conclusions did not change in kind, but price matters more than I first wrote.** Recomputed over the corrected ranges (D-04) they depend mainly on searches per session and session length at 10 requests; at 3 requests the cache ratio r roughly doubles the break-even, and for one-match long sessions r decides whether memory seeding pays. D-04 varies only r, w, o, so it does **not** show independence of the model or host (`11` section 5, RV-0024).
- **One real error in my own arithmetic was found by the review and fixed:** the memory seed break-even left out the core-prefix re-read that an avoided client-side search saves (`calc/memory_seed.py` revision 4, `price_sensitivity.py` revision 2). The thresholds moved by 0.01 to 0.03.
- **The cost equation has published precedents; the break-even equations do not.** The four-class structure of DPR-0006 is printed in several papers (section 4). No published break-even for client-side tool discovery or for memory seeding was found **in the stored set and the scouts' reported results** (the scouts' queries are not stored); the nearest published thresholds are Anthropic's "10 or more tools" / "more than 10k tokens" rule for tool search and the keep-alive break-even `I_max = tau(w/r - 1)` (2607.19214). The scripts remain the lead's derivations, marked as such.
- **The prefix-cache mechanics are documented in serving-system papers and docs, and they explain most of the probe results but not all** (section 3).

## 3. Cache mechanics: what the literature predicts about the probe results (steward batch C, FND-0045)
14 claims: 8 confirmed, 3 partly, 3 corrected. Corrections to the scout: PagedAttention's all-or-nothing rule evicts a request's blocks, not prefixes; SGLang offers four eviction policies; CacheBlend reuses "regardless of prefix or not"; the OpenAI 1,024-token minimum is for GPT-5.6 and later; the "15 minutes" of a serving trace is an inter-arrival statistic, not a lifetime.

| Probe fact (`10`) | Predicted by the literature | Not predicted |
|---|---|---|
| Cached counts are multiples of a block (16 on gpt-oss-120b, 64 on GLM-5.3, 128 on MiniMax-M3) | Block-granular caching with full blocks only: vLLM ("We only cache full blocks"), Mooncake, LMCache, OpenAI; 16 equals vLLM's default block size and 128 equals OpenAI's rounding for earlier models (a coincidence of numbers, no source ties either to these models); SGLang's page size is a deployment setting (default 1) | Why 64 and 128; why models on one route differ; that MiniMax-M3 writes a 128-token block on every request |
| On GLM-5.3 and MiniMax-M3 a change invalidates the cache from the change onward | Chained block hashes (vLLM), radix longest-prefix match (SGLang), Mooncake, DeepSeek ("only matches the prefix part"), OpenAI | The template order of tools and system text on either model (only the Harmony format of gpt-oss is stored: tools in the developer message after the instructions) |
| gpt-oss-120b is not monotonic in the edit position | **Nothing predicts the values.** Candidates stated in sources, none shown for this route: only some prefixes are stored (DeepSeek's page: units persisted at request boundaries, at common-prefix detection and at fixed token intervals); replica-local caches (OpenAI, "A Year in LLM Serving", CacheRoute); sliding-window layers with a 128-token band (gpt-oss model card; vLLM hybrid manager), which alone do not explain a cap of 64 | The values 64 / 3,248 / 3,808; why a late edit behaves like an early one |
| Lifetime: present after 75 minutes everywhere; gone after about 5 hours on two models, present after 5.5 on one | Lifetimes of minutes to a few hours are documented elsewhere (OpenAI 5-10 minutes up to 1 hour, 30 minutes or 12 hours on GPT-5.6+; Groq 2 hours; DeepSeek "a few hours to a few days"; Alibaba explicit 5 minutes) | Any lifetime of the three university models |
| Hit lengths could vary with replicas, routing or eviction | Per-instance caches, cache-blind routing and load-dependent eviction are documented (a trace study; cache-aware routing raising the served hit rate from 64.1% to 93.2% in one simulation) | Anything about the university deployment (replicas, router, other tenants); whether hit lengths repeat on this route is unknown |
Consequence: the probes on GLM-5.3 and MiniMax-M3 are **consistent with** a prefix hash chain as in vLLM, SGLang and Mooncake, which is what V5-R02 assumes; DeepSeek's documented unit rule (a hit needs a persisted prefix unit; a first request after a change gets no hit) would behave differently and is not excluded, and OpenAI, Alibaba and Groq state hits are not guaranteed. gpt-oss-120b stays unexplained: no stored source explains it. The observed block sizes 16, 64, 128 match vLLM's default and OpenAI's rounding only as a coincidence of numbers; a multiple of 32 for Qwen in `10`, Mooncake's 512 and LMCache's 256 match none of the three. The minimum cacheable length is stated by Groq, MiniMax, Alibaba, OpenAI and Anthropic (not found on the stored Z.ai and DeepSeek pages) and is **not** in the first-order arithmetic of D-01 to D-04 except through the "uncached core" variant of D-01 (not in D-04): the scripts' 1,550-token core is above Groq's, MiniMax's, Alibaba's and OpenAI's minimum (1,024 or less) and below Anthropic's largest (4,096).

## 4. The cost equation, the 7:2:1 weighting and the lead's break-even arithmetic (steward batch B, FND-0044)
29 claims: by the steward's table 24 confirmed (five of them with added detail or limits: B8, B12, B14, B25, B27) and 5 partly (B10, B13, B15, B19, B29); none corrected, none absent. (The steward's hand-back message counts 22 and 7; the table is used here.) Details the scout missed: B8's $3,550 / $1,730 are the project total (agent-only row $3,114 / $1,394); B24's keep-alive equations are in section 5.3; the Mem0 token saving comes with a lower answer score (J 66.88 against 72.90 for full context); the Anthropic tool-use prompt ranges from 264 to 675 tokens, not 286 to 675.

**DPR-0006's equation against the printed forms** (`C_r = π_F·F + π_W5·W5 + π_W1·W1 + π_R·R + π_O·O + T`):
| Term | Printed in the literature |
|---|---|
| F fresh input, W cache write, R cache read, O output, summed over requests | **Yes**: 2607.12161 Eq. (1) (with write 1.25, read 0.1), 2604.22750 Eq. (2), 2608.01347 section 7 (fresh, read, write, output plus a per-trajectory tool charge); Anthropic's page states the usage fields |
| W5 and W1 as two write classes | Price pages only (Anthropic 1.25 and 2.0; OpenAI 1.25 and 2.5 on GPT-5.6+); every printed equation has **one** write class. The W1 term is DPR-0006's own extension |
| T server-tool charges | Only as a per-trajectory `C_tool` (2608.01347); tool search "isn't metered as a separate server tool" on Anthropic's page |
| S storage | Prose only (2601.06007: Gemini $4.50 per million tokens per hour "accounted for separately"; AA "billed separately") |
| X time-based charges | No counterpart found |
| Reasoning inside O | **Not settled by the sources**: 2607.12161 reports thinking inside the output field on one model and in an 8.7% unattributed residual on another; this matches DPR-0006's own [U] and its falsifier |
| Implicit caching without a write term | 2604.22750 Eq. (4) (read at 0.2 of input, no write), i.e. DPR-0006 with W = 0; the same paper prints a cached price of 0.1 of input and uses 0.2 in the equation (unexplained) |
Terms present in some papers that DPR-0006 and the scripts leave out: a **hit rate ρ** per request (2607.15516: ρ 0.83 at 2,053 tokens, 1.00 at 4,096 on one model, a function of N and prompt size), a **keep-alive cost** `(I/τ + 1)·r` against `w` once, with `I_max = τ(w/r − 1)` (2607.19214), a marginal cost of a delivered tool token `w·p_in + N_future·r·p_in` (2607.12161 Eq. 4: the same structure as the scripts' definition term `D·(w + (t/2)·r)`, called "a bounded conceptual model" there), and **minimum cacheable length** (section 3). These are named omissions of the scripts, not errors in them.

**The 7:2:1 weighting is an assumed weighting, not a measured mix.** Artificial Analysis writes "assuming a 7:2:1 ratio of cache hit, input, and output tokens" and labels it "General agentic (recommended)"; no basis is stated. Measured shares elsewhere (units differ): cache-read share of tokens 64% (a benchmark sweep), "just under 90%" (OpenRouter, September; about 60% in January), 46-92% by agent (Requesty, cached_tokens / input_tokens), about 90% in one agent study's mean run; **output share about 0.5-1% of tokens** (the sweep, OpenRouter's "around 1%", Manus's 100:1 input-to-output ratio, the mean run), against AA's 10%. The 10.4% in 2607.12161 is a share of the **bill**, not of tokens. So cache-read share brackets AA's 70%; output share and fresh-input share do not agree with 10% and 20%. Hit-rate definitions differ across AA ("cacheable input tokens"), OpenRouter and Requesty. Consequence: `11` uses 7:2:1 only as the benchmark's blend, never as V5's mix; V5's per-session mixes are to be measured.

**What `11` said about the benchmark, checked.** The 7:2:1 statement and "AA measures hit rate but the stored page holds no values" are supported. The 8.9x / 21.6x / 4.4x figures are real but are spreads in the **blended** price (AA's page: 0.39/0.04 is 9.75 on the rounded values it prints; the challenger reproduced 8.86 from 0.044 and 0.39), not in input or output price. For gpt-oss-120b both extreme hosts list no cache price and the blend reproduces only if that price is taken as the input price, so the spread is mostly an input-price spread (0.03 against 0.35); `11` section 5a words it that way.

**Counterfactual list-price pricing is a citable method with limits.** 2608.08654 prices every run at "public per-token list rates published by OpenRouter ... captured on 3 August 2026", uniformly, including two locally served models priced "counterfactually, as what renting equivalent capacity would cost", keeps real spending separate, and reports a 90%-cache-discount sensitivity. Limits: a v1 preprint; it does not say which rate each local model used; its models are local, not a free hosted API; cached input is billed as input. Two more papers use a dated list (2605.28108, 2511.23455). Whether a named list on a stated date is acceptable for the thesis is the human's decision.

**The lead's own derivations, read by the steward against `regime_bill.py`.** `memory_seed.py` (revision 4) and `price_sensitivity.py`'s `hstar` count the same two re-reads as `regime_bill.py` client mode (history `H·r` and core prefix `P·r`), plus `o·q` and the K−1 carry; no double count found. All three scripts omit (a) the re-read of definitions appended by earlier discoveries on the extra request, and (b) the search call's own q output tokens becoming input on later requests, so the saving of an avoided search is understated by about `q·(w + remaining·r)` (q = 150); the seed scripts also have no uncached-core variant (the P re-read is always priced at r). All are small and none changes a conclusion in direction; they are stated as omissions, not corrected, because correcting them would move the thresholds by a few hundredths and they are not first-order.
**No published break-even was found for client-side tool discovery or for memory seeding;** the scripts are the lead's derivations, labelled [D].

## 5. What remains unverified after this review
- **Every price is a page on 2026-10-27**; promotional prices change, and no page was compared with a later version.
- **The university API's actual cache behaviour** beyond the three probes (`10`): hit rate across replicas, gpt-oss-120b's pattern, lifetime on the three untested models.
- **The mix of a real V5 session** (searches per session, matches per search, session length, how often a seed is right): unmeasured; the break-evens are conditional on them.
- **B5's `Input_total`, B16's `C_tool`, B29's "no schema token counts"** and whether Epoch holds dispersion content were not checked; no stored file was compared with its live page; the three scripts were read by the steward, not run (the lead ran them).
- **All scout and steward work is model work** and no human has checked a row; the manifest hashes show what was stored, not that a page is correct.
