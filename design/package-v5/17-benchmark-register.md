## 1. Critical reassessment of the request and of the lead's own proposal (before any search)
Labels: **[keep]** a safeguard the lead holds necessary; **[drop]** a restriction the lead holds unnecessary; **[refine]** kept in another form; **[flag]** bears on V5 and goes to the human. All [I] unless a source is cited; section 2's methods check tests them against the literature.

**1.1 Where the human's request is kept as a necessary safeguard.**
- [keep] *Construct first.* A benchmark is judged against the construct of the experiment's claim, not by its reputation or leaderboard.
- [keep] *Version lock.* Paper version, repository commit, dataset revision, date read; errata and later versions recorded.
- [keep] *Exploratory versus confirmatory.* Pilots and loop items may inform choices; the confirmatory protocol and its held-out selection are frozen before the final claims are tested. Corollary the lead adds: the held-out items must be disjoint from every item looked at in pilots, and inspecting a benchmark's *properties* (catalogue size, schemas, licence) is not the same as looking at its held-out *outcomes*.
- [keep] *Provenance of criticism.* Authors, independent reviewers and interested parties (vendors, competitors, authors of a rival benchmark) are kept apart, and apart from what this workspace has confirmed itself.
- [keep] *Native versus modified.* What the benchmark provides is kept apart from what V5 would change (request layout, catalogue padding, sessions added).
- [keep] *Subsets are not representative by default.* A subset's distribution on the properties that matter is reported beside the full set's.

**1.2 Where the lead pushes back on the request.**
- [refine] *"Prefer established, independently evaluated benchmarks" for real-world claims.* In this niche (MCP servers, large catalogues, prompt-cache cost) most benchmarks are months old and few have an independent evaluation; the older, more vetted ones tend to have small catalogues and a higher risk of being in open-weight models' training data. Independent evaluation is therefore scored as one property, weighed against construct fit, not a preference that dominates it. *Revised after CH-0155:* section 6 shows the other side too: the scrutinised benchmarks have documented and partly fixed defects (tau2-bench-verified, Toolathlon-Verified, MCPMark "Verified", BFCL ground-truth fixes), while the youngest have unknown error rates; so independent scrutiny counts **for** a candidate (known errors can be avoided or corrected), and "few independent evaluations exist" is a reason not to make it a hard requirement, not a reason to discount it.
- [flag] *"Real-world" has no stated target.* The thesis question is not supplied (`requirements/THESIS_QUESTION.md`) and no target deployment is named; V5 uses catalogue regimes (R-small, R-mid, R-large) and session patterns as its proxy for deployments. The register can say "representative of benchmark population X under V5's regimes", not "representative of real use".
- [flag] *Statistical adequacy needs a target difference.* Whether a sample is adequate depends on the difference it must resolve, and the human decided there is no margin (REQ-U21-07), while `05` section 3 says the end-to-end sample "is chosen so that the accuracy differences the loop could not resolve can be resolved", which presupposes one. The register reports, per candidate, the pool available for a held-out set and the difference resolvable at that size; adequacy then depends on the human choosing either an estimation-only reading (report the difference and its interval, no pass or fail) or a margin.
- [refine] *The four categories (synthetic tasks, synthetic tools, subsets, extensions) are not one axis.* A benchmark supplies up to four separable components: **tasks** (prompts with gold), **tool definitions** (the catalogue), **environment** (execution and state), **scorer**. An option can take some components from a benchmark and build others (for example real MCP definitions from a public catalogue with constructed tasks). Every candidate is therefore described by where each of the four components comes from.

**1.3 Where the lead withdraws or changes its own proposal.**
- [drop] "Never aggregate across benchmarks" and "prefer two benchmarks for the headline claim" as rules. [refine] Effects measured in a common unit (for example the paired bill ratio between arms, or a paired accuracy difference) may be synthesised across benchmarks with their heterogeneity shown; raw scores from different scorers are not pooled; the number of benchmarks follows the claim.
- [refine] "Choose before seeing results" was too blunt; it applies to the confirmatory choice and its held-out set, not to exploration.
- [drop] "Up to 7 scouts, about 150 pages" as the measure of the study; replaced by completion criteria (section 2.3).
- [drop] The claim that P5 "needs no MCP-Atlas, made-up tools are enough". P5 measures billing, which depends on how many tokens real definitions take; definitions can come from public catalogues without running their tasks. P5's options are compared like every other item's.

**1.4 What both of us had overlooked.**
- *The outcome is the bill, but benchmarks are built for accuracy.* The properties that drive V5's bill (tokens per definition, tool-result sizes, turns per task, sessions, volatile content in tool results such as timestamps) are rarely reported and must be read from data or code.
- *Harness control.* Many benchmarks ship their own agent loop and prompt; V5 needs its own request layout (a fixed tools array, discoveries appended, `04`). Replacing a benchmark's loop is a modification; it makes published leaderboard numbers incomparable (V5 compares none) and may break assumptions the scorer makes.
- *Scorer reliability.* LLM-judge scoring, possibly with a judge served by the same university API, against deterministic state or unit-test checks; for confirmatory claims a judge's agreement must be known.
- *Floor and ceiling.* If tasks are too hard or too easy for the open-weight models on the route, accuracy differences become invisible and bias the reading toward "nearly the same".
- *Contamination* of public benchmarks in open-weight training data (matters for accuracy, little for billing mechanics).
- *Our own selection bias.* V5 is this workspace's design; choosing where it looks good is the fault the human described in papers. Safeguards: rankings use properties only, are written before any V5 run on the benchmark, and an independent agent ranks the same cards without seeing the lead's ranking (section 2.2).
- *Choices already written into `05` before any register.* P3 names BFCL's withheld-function design, P1 names ATRBench as the closest stored design and P4 names MCPMark. [flag] These become candidates assessed here, not choices.
- *Licence, terms and live services.* Gated data, servers that need keys, and live web services (not reproducible, and a source of uncached variance) are properties to record.

## 2. Study design and completion criteria
### 2.1 Waves
1. **Discovery (6 scouts, parallel):** M methods literature; A MCP and large-catalogue benchmarks; B function-calling, multi-turn and stateful tool benchmarks; C multi-session, memory and personalised tool use; D catalogue sources, tool retrieval, long-horizon and large-output agent benchmarks; E criticism (audits, errata, issues, forums) of a seed list drawn from the workspace. Prompts: `inputs/scout-prompts-benchmarks-20261101.md`. Every page cited is stored by the lead (`v5-bm-*`).
2. **Property inspection (up to 2 inspectors):** read-only reading of dataset viewers, repository files and licence files for the shortlist, to confirm native properties (counts, schemas, sizes, harness structure).
3. **Verification (stewards):** claims checked against stored pages; material criticisms checked against their primary source.
4. **Register and rankings (lead), independent ranking (one design-proposer, blind to the lead's ranking).**
5. **Review (design-challengers):** one methods critic, one adversarial reviewer.

### 2.2 Ranking rules (stated before any discovery)
Per experiment: the construct (section 4); hard requirements (a candidate that fails one is rejected with the reason); scored properties; ranked candidates with their four components' sources, the modifications V5 needs, the trade-offs and the uncertainty. Independent evaluation, recency, contamination risk, scorer reliability, reproducibility, licence and cost to run are scored, none decisive alone.

### 2.3 Completion criteria (checked in section 10)
- CC1 Every selection rule cites a stored methods source or is marked as the lead's reasoning.
- CC2 Every benchmark named in the workspace for tool use, MCP or multi-session work, and every one the scouts find, is triaged in or out of scope with a reason.
- CC3 Each in-scope benchmark has a property card with every field filled or marked "not found in the places read"; versions locked.
- CC4 For each shortlisted candidate, a search for independent criticism, errata and issues was made; material criticisms are checked against their source and labelled by provenance.
- CC5 Per experiment: construct, hard requirements, ranked candidates (including constructed options), rejected options with reasons, modifications needed, uncertainty.
- CC6 The independent ranking and the challengers' reviews are recorded with every disagreement, resolved against evidence or left open for the human.
- CC7 Proposed V5 changes are listed with evidence; nothing in V5 changed.
- CC8 Unresolved limitations are listed.

**How to read the Verdict column (CH-0154).** "CONFIRMED" means the steward found the quoted wording on the stored page; it does not mean the rule is validated. R1, R3, R5, R7 and R9 are the lead's rules **informed by** those passages, and each says more than its source: R1 (no source prescribes per-experiment choice), R3 (the freezing of padding and prompt variants is the lead's addition), R5 (Miller's setting is question answering), R7 (Gill is reading comprehension), R9 (one leaderboard case, not a measured rate). They are labelled [I].

**Rules added after RV-0027 [I] (CH-0150, CH-0156).** R11 *Clustering:* the unit of independence is named per experiment (task, user, server or catalogue), and errors are clustered on it; task counts are not treated as independent units where tasks share a user, server or history (Miller's "up to 3X", m01). R12 *Multiple comparisons:* each experiment names one primary comparison before it runs; the rest are reported as secondary with no claim of significance. R13 *Route drift:* paired arms run interleaved in time on the same model build, and the model identifier and date are logged per request (HAL's warning that providers may swap weights, d11), so drift between arms is not mistaken for an effect. R14 *Combining stages:* a loop item supports only its own stage; a claim about the whole policy comes only from the end-to-end study, never from adding loop-item effects.

**What the methods literature does not settle [U].** How to pool effects across agent benchmarks; preregistration specific to agent evaluation; clustered errors when tasks share servers or users; judges for tool-call correctness; sample size for a bill ratio. These are reported as open, not filled by the lead's preference.

**Revision 2 of the requirements (after DPR-0010 section 1 and RV-0027; the table above is revision 1 and is kept as written).**
- *Numbers for "above n\*" and R-mid (DPR-0010 X4).* `calc/price_sensitivity.stdout.txt` gives n* of 11-14 tools (D = 1), 19-28 (D = 3) and 47-77 (D = 10) at T = 10 and d = 250; `04` defines R-mid as above n* with the tool block within the window. Working reading for ranking: **at least 100 definitions declared, the number logged**; a 74-tool catalogue is above n* only for few searches per session.
- *Floor and ceiling become a hard requirement for every accuracy construct (P2, P4 accuracy half, E2E; CH-0140).* Checked on a baseline-arm pilot on items disjoint from the confirmatory set (allowed by section 1.1); reported scores of other model generations do not count. The "Open-weight range" column of section 5 is context only.
- *P3 H1 (DPR-0010 X2, CH-0147).* No stored benchmark supplies equivalent alternatives, so as written H1 rejects everything. Revised: gold = the tools the reference solution uses; equivalents are adjudicated on the pilot before the confirmatory run, and calls outside gold that still succeed are reported.
- *P3 H3 and P5 H3 are V5-harness duties, not candidate properties (CH-0147);* they move under M0.
- *P1 H5 (DPR-0010 X3, CH-0147, CH-0148).* V5's seed rule depends on **tool recurrence** (later sessions needing tools called before), not on informational need. Revised H5: recurrence measured from gold calls and reported, with a no-memory arm; DolphinBench's with-and-without-history test becomes a scored property. P1's effective sample is **users**, not sessions (R11), and where users are constructed, h and recurrence are set by the construction, so results are stated conditional on them ("at recurrence r ...").
- *P5 H1 and H4 (DPR-0010 X6, CH-0147).* Definition size is a swept variable anchored on real definitions; "licence allows use" means local research use permitted and no redistribution of the material needed.
- *E2E H5 and H6 (DPR-0010 X5, CH-0149).* H5: no live drift between paired arms and repeats (frozen or replayed tool results). H6: sessions of several **user** turns, native or by a declared construction, since the bill is reported per turn and 13 of 22 cards are single-prompt tasks; a held-out reserve is kept for the confirmatory run.
- *P4 does not require a tool catalogue (DPR-0010 X1, CH-0145),* so coding benchmarks are candidates for P4.

**Synthetic and constructed options are candidates under the same rules.** For P5 and P3 a constructed catalogue or constructed task cells may isolate the variable better than any benchmark (INT-T-021 section 2); for P1 and E2E a returning-user axis exists in no benchmark found (scout C section 4), so some construction is unavoidable and must carry a construction method, validated ground truth and bias controls; a synthetic-only result is not reported as real-world evidence.

**Reading [I], revised after CH-0142, CH-0143, CH-0144.** The verdicts above mean the wording is on the stored page, not that the error rates are measured on a common basis: the figures come from different protocols (an expert audit of 496 tasks; Epoch's review of 50 items that stops at a threshold, 5-15 items per category; single GitHub reporters), on benchmarks that were audited because they are prominent. They show that **every audited benchmark in this set has documented scorer or ground-truth errors**; they do not give a general error rate. The benchmarks without criticism are mostly the youngest (ATRBench, Momento, VitaBench 2.0, MPT, ETAPP), which is absence of scrutiny, not evidence of quality. Consequences: a confirmatory claim needs a scorer check on the subset actually used (R4); and pairing arms on the same tasks cancels scorer error **only where the arms produce comparable outputs**. Where arms differ in call patterns (deferral adds search calls; BFCL's response check penalises redundant calls, b03; MCP-Atlas judges only the final answer; tau2 drops malformed calls from the denominator, e04), scorer error can differ by arm, so the scorer check is done per arm. Provenance: "I" for a GitHub reporter means only that no tie to the benchmark's authors is shown; several reporters link their own related repositories, which is noted where seen (CH-0144).

## 9. Proposals for the human (V5 changes reported, not made)
1. **P3's design (`05` P3):** it names "BFCL's withheld-function design adapted to deferral"; BFCL's protocol edits the tools list and replaces the user turn (I2 item 5, checked [O]), the operation V5 forbids. Proposal: describe P3 as "a target tool deferred and findable by the client's search, with absent, wrong-tool and oracle cells", with BFCL as a precedent only.
2. **P1's design (`05` P1):** no benchmark found combines at least 100 declared definitions with tools that recur across a user's sessions (n* itself is 11-77 at T = 10, so a 74-tool catalogue such as ATRBench's is above n* for few searches per session; CH-0168); P1 needs a declared construction, a recurrence grid, users as the unit, and results stated conditional on recurrence. Proposal: say so in `05` P1, and drop "ATRBench ... closest stored design" in favour of section 7's wording.
3. **Statistical framing: decided by the human (INT-T-022, REQ-U1101-02): estimation-only, no margin.** Reporting recipe from I3 item 6 (Miller m01; Singh k23; an ICML 2026 audit k24; Benavoli et al. k25-k26): the paired item-level difference with a 95% interval clustered on users or scenarios; the design's minimum detectable effect stated before the run (this replaces `05` section 3's "sample chosen so that differences can be resolved"); churn (items that flip) beside the net difference; optionally the difference shown against several fixed widths as sensitivity, never as a threshold; no claim of "equivalent" (that needs a margin). With AppWorld's test_normal (168 tasks) and an assumed 20% discordance, the paired 95% interval is about ±7 points if tasks were independent; they are 56 scenarios of 3 tasks, and if variants move together the interval widens to about ±9.6 to ±11.8 points (CH-0161; the within-scenario correlation is unmeasured) [I, arithmetic, the discordance assumed].
4. **Regimes by tokens:** n* and the window limit depend on tokens per definition, which vary widely between sources (ToolRet 175 tokens per document, MCPVerse about 200-266 per tool, PolicyLayer 1,864 per server); P5 should measure them per route model before R-small, R-mid and R-large are fixed for any experiment.
5. **Benchmark choice per experiment** from section 7, including the two open choices (P1's native check; P4's vehicle and scope).
6. **Licences (made consistent after CH-0164):** Toolathlon and TheMCPCompany have no licence file, so no permission to reuse is shown; AppWorld is Apache 2.0 with encrypted bundles and a request not to post derived plain text, so its data stays out of the repository (the human's remote is private but pushed); MCP-Atlas's code is MIT and its data CC-BY-4.0, but definitions captured from its 36 servers fall under each server package's own licence, which nobody checked; VitaBench 2.0's code is MIT and its dataset licence is not shown; MPT is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (non-commercial). The legal effect of any of these is not assessed here.
7. **Downloads:** using any candidate means fetching its data (AppWorld `appworld download data`, MCP-Atlas Docker image and dataset, captured MCP definitions); that is beyond this amendment and needs an authorization.
8. **P2's mechanism (`05` P2):** `allowed_tools` is rejected on this route (`10`), so "a subset offered at a fixed cached prefix" has no server mechanism yet; P2's design should name the client-side means it tests (for example the subset named in an appended message) before a benchmark is chosen for it (CH-0168). **R1 part 1 (one sample per cell):** the rejection comes from the LiteLLM proxy for all ten ScaDS models and both request shapes, before any model is reached; OpenAI accepts the Chat Completions shape, keeps the cache (3,328 of 3,426 tokens cached on both models) and kept both models inside the subset; ScaDS's `/responses` accepts the shape without error but gpt-oss-120b called the excluded tool (the other nine made no call; their text was not recorded).
