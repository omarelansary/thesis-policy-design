# 03. Short comparison: graph G against C-A, expose-all and vendor deferral

Status: provisional, lead's assessment [I], revised after the challenger's review (CH-0038, CH-0042, CH-0043; see `04`). Everything about effectiveness is a hypothesis. Criterion: the working objective of AUTH-006, cumulative model-visible input tokens over complete trajectories subject to end-to-end success not below the reference by `delta` (undecided), on the mean and within strata. Tool count, token categories, billed cost and cache behaviour are recorded separately and are not in the criterion.

## 1. Is G a fair thing to compare with the others?
Only partly, and the comparison has to say so. **G is not a fourth policy beside C-A, expose-all and vendor deferral: it is a family of run-time configurations (R1-R6) plus a procedure (D1-D4) that selects among arms which include those three.** It therefore cannot "lose" to them in the ordinary sense: if expose-all is feasible and cheapest, G returns expose-all. What G can fail at is different (section 3). The table compares the *run-time realisations* that G, C-A, expose-all and vendor deferral each produce, and separately what it costs to find the right one.

## 2. Should C-A be the principal proposal? No: a component and a comparator; and why that is not an asymmetry in G's favour
1. **Applicability of its distinctive rule.** Rule A1 needs need statistics from a feasible reference. Where the catalogue exceeds the window no reference run exists and the rule has no input (errata 4). G's **D2 is the same stage** (the ordered core), skipped under the same condition; G does not depend on it.
2. **The reasons "heuristic ordering, no safety property, known pattern" apply equally to G's D2.** They are not reasons to prefer G over C-A. The demotion rests on applicability and on G's other stages not needing need statistics; it is not a verdict that C-A is worse.
3. **What survives of C-A:** the ordered-core stage (D2), the runtime-information discipline, the equal-size and swept frequency-core arms that keep a rule honest, and the measurement habit of the A0 probes. C-A remains an arm and a comparator inside G.
4. **Both lose the F1 reference at large scale.** Noninferiority is then relative to a named substitute for every arm, G included; what differs is only that G's mechanisms do not need `p_t`.

## 3. Should C-B be the principal proposal? Not adopted; its parts are conditional stages
C-B (system-run query-conditioned selector plus a system trigger, never asking the model) is closest to the supervisor's staged, context-dependent, re-adding picture. The evidence does not justify selecting it: retrieval conditions scored **below a curated pool** in DynamicMCPBench Appendix N (four models, single attempts, intervals about ±8; relative to flat, `rag:32` matched on the one model that ran flat) and **below full exposure** in ComplexMCP (two LLMs, one embedder) and in ToolMenuBench's keyword filters (synthetic) (EXT-0053, EXT-0026, EXT-0028; the last two from the AUTH-004 evidence map); on tasks the retriever never surfaced, `pass^3` was 0.0% for every one of the four models at `rag:8` and about one pass in 1,905 single attempts (EXT-0053, steward V6); a two-stage router matched single-stage retrieval in success with no token data; **no stored source supports a system-side trigger**. G keeps C-B's parts as R2 (per-request narrowing), R2 level 2 and R6 (trigger), each **present only if D4 selects it**.

## 5. What would make each lose, and what G itself can fail at
- **Expose-all loses** when the catalogue exceeds the window, or a restricted arm is feasible and cheaper in tokens at noninferior success. Under the token criterion it is **not** "cheap because cached": caching changes billing, not what the model reads. (A natural-replay cell shows token reduction without a billed advantage, so the two are recorded separately and neither is inferred from the other.)
- **Vendor deferral loses** to an equal-size or swept frequency core if one beats or ties its hand-pick at lower design cost; and to any arm if models do not initiate search.
- **C-A loses** where its rule is dominated by the swept or equal-size frequency core, or where no need statistics exist.
- **G fails** in three ways the others cannot: (i) **tournament cost**: `O_dev / N_dep` exceeds the saving over a fixed alternative (a small hand-picked core plus vendor search, the fewest-components rival, has the smallest selection cost, but naming the 3-5 most-used tools needs usage knowledge, the same estimator gap as errata correction 4); (ii) **selection error**: D4 picks a worse arm than a fixed alternative because of multiplicity, a winner's-curse effect or too little data (error rates unknown, experiment E6); (iii) **no benefit over a stage-free configuration**: if D4 returns search-only or expose-all, the "staged, context-dependent" content of G is empty for that regime. The strongest rival to G is therefore *a small hand-picked core plus vendor search*, at small catalogues *expose-all*, and, where a rare stratum is absent from the mean, *a static core without recovery* (which the stratum guard is there to catch).

## 6. What this comparison does not show
Which arm is best: the record does not discriminate. G's content over vendor deferral is the explicit selection procedure, the optional per-request narrowing and trigger stages as testable arms, and the discipline (runtime-information table, sweep arms, estimands, stratum guard); that is a methodological structure, not a demonstrated improvement.
