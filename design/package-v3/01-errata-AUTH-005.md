## 0. Working objective for this revision (an instruction, not a finding)
By the human's instruction in AUTH-006 (not a supervisor quotation; the noninferiority margin `delta` is **not** decided): **minimise cumulative model-visible input tokens over complete trajectories while preserving end-to-end task success, including all decision and discovery overhead.**
- Primary cost estimand `C_tok(a)`: expectation over tasks of the sum over turns of the model-visible input tokens of the whole prompt (system, tool block, contract, history, tool results), plus the input tokens of every decider call (selector, judge, classifier) and every discovery or recovery exchange. Complete trajectories: extra turns count.
- Primary constraint: end-to-end success `Y(a) >= Y_ref - delta` (formulation F1), `Y_ref` from the reference policy or a named substitute.
- **Recorded separately, never merged into one number:** tool count per turn; token categories (tool metadata, contract and scope statement, history, tool outputs, selector/judge, discovery, recovery); billed cost under named prices; cache behaviour (hit rate, read and write tokens).
- Consequence for v2: v2 used billed cost as the default criterion ("O1") with exposure count as an alternative ("O2"). The cache terms `w`, `r`, `pi_c`, `c_def` in v2's A1 describe **billed cost**, not model-visible tokens; they leave the criterion and move to the billed-cost report. (Model-visible tokens exclude deferred definitions that are not placed in the context window; that is the vendor's description of deferral, EXT-0009, a vendor claim.)

## 1. Correction 1: success and cost were combined in one equation
**Where.** v2 `03-evaluation-outline.md` sections 3 and 4 (and DPR-0005 6.2-6.3, HYP-0011, HYP-0012; v2 `07` slide 6 by implication).
**What it said.** Population net effect `w (V - P1) - (1 - w) U` with break-even `w* = U / (V - P1 + U)`, and for two initiators `w_SM = dU / (dR - dP + dU)`.
**Defect.** `V` is a gain in success probability (dimensionless); `P1` and `U` are costs (tokens or money). The sums and ratios add and divide quantities in different units. No exchange rate was stated or justified, although the primary rule (F1, noninferiority-constrained) deliberately has none. The "break-even prevalences" had no meaning.
**Corrected treatment.** Keep two estimands per arm `a` and stratum `s` in {M (miss), N (no-miss)}: success `y_a^s` (probability) and cost `c_a^s` (tokens per trajectory). With prevalence `w` of stratum M in the declared population:
- `Y_a(w) = w y_a^M + (1-w) y_a^N` (success units); `C_a(w) = w c_a^M + (1-w) c_a^N` (token units). Both are linear in `w` and are never added.
- **Decision under F1 at the estimated prevalence `w_hat`:** (1) feasible set `Phi = { a : Y_a(w_hat) >= Y_ref(w_hat) - delta }`; (2) among feasible arms choose the lowest `C_a(w_hat)`; if `Phi` is empty report the frontier and conclude that no arm meets noninferiority. Intervals are carried through both steps.
- **Break-even prevalences exist only within one unit.** *Admissibility* (success units): let `l^s = y_ref^s - y_none^s` be the success loss of the no-recovery arm in stratum `s`. No recovery becomes inadmissible when `w > w_adm = (delta - l^N) / (l^M - l^N)` (valid when `l^M > l^N`; with `l^N = 0` it is `delta / l^M`). *Tokens:* recovery uses fewer expected tokens than no recovery iff `DeltaC(w) = w dc^M + (1-w) dc^N < 0`; a crossing exists in [0,1] only if `dc^M` and `dc^N` have opposite signs (then `w_C = dc^N / (dc^N - dc^M)`). If recovery costs more tokens in both strata (the usual case) it is never cheaper and is justified, if at all, only by admissibility at `w_hat`.
- **Initiators** (system trigger `S` vs model-emitted request `M`): `DeltaY(w) = Y_S - Y_M` and `DeltaC(w) = C_S - C_M`, each a function of `w` in its own unit. `S` dominates `M` at `w_hat` iff `DeltaY >= 0` and `DeltaC <= 0` with one strict. Otherwise apply the F1 rule above; report the two zero-crossings `w_Y` and `w_C` separately. A mechanism with higher recovery and higher token cost is not dominated, and no scalar break-even between them exists without an exchange rate.
- **Exchange rate.** A scalar `J = lambda DeltaY - DeltaC` (`lambda` in tokens per unit success) would give a single break-even `w_J`. **No exchange rate is justified in the record or by the human; none is adopted.** If one is ever adopted it must be stated, justified and varied in a sensitivity analysis.
- **Prevalence** is a transportability report: the curves over `w` show how conclusions move with the deployment's miss prevalence; the test is at the declared population's estimated `w_hat`.
**Records superseded:** the equations above in v2 `03` sections 3-4, DPR-0005 6.2-6.3, HYP-0011/0012 falsifier wording that refers to a "net". The falsifiers' logical form (negation of the claim at the estimated prevalence) stands, with "preferred under the pre-registered F1 rule" now meaning the two-step rule above.

## 2. Correction 2: the missed-tool probability is not a bound on success loss
**Where.** v2 `01-policy-candidate.md` sections 2, 4 (notes under the decision table), 8 and 9; DPR-0004 4.2 "mapping of epsilon"; v2 `05` section 2 last row; v2 `06` A6.
**What it said.** `M(F) = sum over deferred tools of p_t q_t` is the expected unreached-need mass; the expected success loss is "at most `M(F)` times the loss when a needed tool is missing, at most 1"; hence `epsilon := delta` is a "sufficient, conservative mapping", conservative "only if the frequency estimates are unbiased".
**Defects.** (a) `M(F)` is a *coverage surrogate*, not a success loss: it concerns whether a needed tool is reached, not whether the task then succeeds. (b) The inequality omits the interference term, so it is not an upper bound on the success difference. (c) `delta` is a margin on the *total* success difference; spending all of it on one source of loss leaves nothing for the others, so `epsilon = delta` could not be "conservative" even under exact estimates. (d) Unbiasedness is not the only requirement (see the list). The claim exceeded the evidence.
**Corrected statement.** Let `E_ref` be the event that the reference policy succeeds on a task and let `R_t` mean tool `t` was called in that successful reference run ("needed"). For deferred tools `t` define `p_t = P(R_t)` and `q_t = P(t not reached by C-A | R_t)`. Then, **only under assumptions A1-A6 below**,
`P(Y_ref = 1, Y_CA = 0) <= M(F) + I`, hence `Y_ref - Y_CA <= M(F) + I`,
where `I = P(Y_ref = 1, all needed tools reached, Y_CA = 0)` is the **interference term** (failures that occur although every needed tool is reached: contract and scope-statement tokens, search turns and the turn budget, distraction by appended results, argument errors with unfamiliar schemas, changed trajectories). `M(F)` bounds only the **coverage-attributable** part. **`I` is not bounded by anything in the record.**
- A1 Needed means called in a *successful* reference run, over a *reference trajectory*; C-A's trajectories differ, so its needs differ.
- A2 An unreached needed tool causes failure (no alternative route); if alternatives exist the term over-counts (conservative in that one direction only).
- A3 `p`, `q` are the true values for the deployment population; sample estimates need upper confidence bounds; the dev population is the deployment population (transportability).
- A4 `q_t` does not depend on the surface (see correction 3: it does).
- A5 The union bound holds for any dependence; the sum may exceed 1, in which case the bound is vacuous.
- A6 Gains where the reference fails and C-A succeeds are ignored (this makes the bound a bound on *gross* loss).
**Consequences.** `epsilon := delta` is **withdrawn**. `epsilon` is a tuning parameter of a candidate generator, not a safety guarantee. Noninferiority is established **only end-to-end**: the surface is chosen by sweeping the candidate list produced by rule A1 and picking the smallest-token surface whose *measured* held-out dev success satisfies `Y >= Y_ref - delta`, then confirming on a disjoint test set. v2's "empirical sweep" arm (P-sweep) becomes the selection stage, and A1 supplies the ordering of tools only.

## 3. Correction 3: A1's greedy core-selection rule is not cost-minimising, and has no safety property
**Where.** v2 `01` section 4 (A1 row, "Step 2"), DPR-0004 4.2.
**What it said.** Step 1: keep `t` in the core iff `keep_t <= disc_t`. Step 2: while `M(F) > epsilon`, move to the core the tool with the largest `p_t q_t / keep_t`.
**Analysis.** With per-session costs `keep_t` (cost of keeping `t` visible) and `disc_t` (expected cost of discovering it when deferred), the constrained problem is: choose the deferred set `D` to minimise `sum_{t not in D} keep_t + sum_{t in D} disc_t` subject to `sum_{t in D} m_t <= epsilon`, `m_t = p_t q_t`. Equivalently **maximise `sum_{t in D} (keep_t - disc_t)` subject to `sum_{t in D} m_t <= epsilon`: a 0/1 knapsack.** The *incremental* cost of moving `t` from deferred to core is `keep_t - disc_t` (the lost saving), **not** `keep_t`. So (i) Step 1 is optimal only for the unconstrained, additive problem; (ii) Step 2's index uses the gross cost `keep_t`, which is the wrong denominator; the index that matches the fractional relaxation is `m_t / (keep_t - disc_t)` (largest first, over tools with positive saving); (iii) even that greedy is a heuristic for 0/1 knapsack and not optimal.
**Demonstration (illustrative numbers, tokens per session; not empirical).** Four tools `(keep, disc, m)`: `t0 (21, 3, 3)`, `t1 (95, 6, 5)`, `t2 (23, 9, 8)`, `t3 (96, 93, 7)`, `epsilon = 18`. Exhaustive search: optimum cost 114 with core `{t3}`. Rule as written moves `t2` and costs 125. The incremental-index greedy finds 114 here. On 3,000 random six-tool instances (uniform integer parameters, illustrative) the rule as written was suboptimal in 1,713 and the incremental-index greedy in 1,023. This shows non-optimality, not magnitude.
**Couplings that break the independence assumed by the formulation.** (a) `q_t` depends on the deferred set `D` (competition in the ranker): for a *fixed-score* ranker, removing competitors cannot lower a remaining tool's rank, so `q` estimated on a larger deferred set is conservative; for rankers with corpus statistics, and for model behaviour (the scope statement changes with `D`, as does the model's tendency to search), the direction is **unknown**. (b) Co-needed tools: one search returns `kappa` results, so `disc_t` is not additive across tools needed in the same session. (c) `p_t` are correlated; the union bound tolerates this for coverage, not for cost. (d) Trajectory length `T_bar` depends on the surface.
**Established properties: none.** No optimality, no approximation ratio, no safety property of the resulting core. A1 is **a heuristic ordering of tools**; the cut is made by the end-to-end sweep (correction 2). Whether the ordering beats the frequency ordering is the question the sweep arms answer.
**Token form (replaces v2's billed-cost form under the working objective).** Let `T_bar` be expected turns per trajectory, `tau` the expected number of turns remaining at the moment of a discovery (content added at a turn is re-read in every later turn), `d_s` the tokens of one search exchange, `kappa` the search width, `L_D` the mean length of a returned deferred definition. Keeping `t` visible costs `keep_t = L_t T_bar` tokens. Discovering it costs `disc_t = p_t (1 - q_t) tau (d_s + L_t + (kappa - 1) L_D)` tokens in expectation (reached cases; unreached cases are failures handled by the constraint, not tokens). Deferring any tool also incurs a common overhead `(L_S + sigma) T_bar` (search interface schema and scope statement per turn) that is shared by all deferral designs. Saving from deferring `t`: `s_t = keep_t - disc_t`. The knapsack, counterexample and couplings above apply unchanged. All terms are in tokens.

## 4. Correction 4: C-A is not applicable to the original large-catalogue problem as specified
**Where.** v2 `01` sections 2, 4 (definitions under the table), 7 (row `p_t`), 9; DPR-0004 4.2 "Where `pi_ref` cannot run".
**Defect.** Rule A1 needs `p_t` (need frequency) and `q_t`, estimated from successful dev runs of the **reference policy** (expose-all). The original problem is the large catalogue: the closest vehicle's flat catalogue is about 292k tokens by the repository's estimate and exceeds the context of 2 of its 4 matrix models (S4G line 28; the supervisor's own example is 10,000 tools). There the reference run cannot be made, `p_t` is censored, and v2 resolved this by restricting the claim to catalogues where the reference (or a named substitute) runs, "which is also where the rival is feasible". That restriction removes C-A from precisely the regime that motivates the thesis; it is not a limitation to be accepted but a mismatch between the candidate and the problem.
**Corrected conclusion.** C-A's *core stage* is applicable only where need statistics exist (from a feasible reference, a substitute, or logs of any previously deployed policy; logs from a policy that exposes a subset are **censored** by that subset and need exploration to estimate need for never-exposed tools, which C-A does not specify). For the large-catalogue problem the core stage is **skipped** (it may be revisited when logs accumulate). What remains is a search interface with a scope statement, optionally with query-conditioned narrowing: neither needs `p_t`. C-A therefore cannot be the principal proposal for the original problem; it is a component and a comparator (see `02`, `03`). **Minimal missing decision for the core stage at large scale: a legitimate source of need statistics (labelled usage) that is not the evaluation set and not the reference run.** What would resolve it: a usage log from deployment or a separate training catalogue with outcomes, collected under an exploration policy.
**Not claimed:** that need statistics cannot be obtained, only that the record specifies no estimator for them at large scale.

## 5. Correction 5: the Toollery "cache multiplier" is a fitted, study-specific parameter
**Where.** v2 `02` section 3 (regime facts, "far below the vendor-page multipliers"), section 5 (the break-even hit rate, "770 uncached-token equivalents"); v2 `04` row 11; v2 `05` section 2 (second row); v2 `07` slide 2; DPR-0004 3.2a; DPR-0005 section 2.
**What it said.** A linear model "implies a cache-read multiplier of about 0.0088", which was then used as a price for reasoning about what C-A or expose-all costs ("at that multiplier the whole warm tool block costs about 770 uncached-token equivalents"; "C-A cannot win on billed tool-block cost at that price").
**Corrected statement.** In Toollery Table 15 (EXT-0062: single-step BFCL replay, per-request means, 600/300/300 calls; the paper's model, pricing and candidate-list construction are **not in the stored text**), a two-parameter linear model (base price per token from the cache-suppressed full-library row; a read multiplier from the warm rows) reproduces the six cells: fitted multiplier 0.00866 from the forced-warm full-library row and 0.00887 from the natural full-library row (2.5% apart); the remaining cells are reproduced within 2% (lead recomputation; the proposer and challenger derived the same). This is a **fitted effective parameter of that study** under a linear model that omits write premiums, latency and any threshold behaviour, with no stated uncertainty. It is **not** a verified billing rate and says nothing about any provider tested later. Statements that treat it as a price are replaced as follows: (a) billed-cost arguments are written as functions of the unknown read multiplier `r` (the closed forms stand as algebra); (b) the regime in which a restriction pays in *billed* cost is "a function of `r`, cache state, traffic and horizon, to be measured per provider"; (c) the vendor-page multipliers (EXT-0021) are the vendor's published prices, not measurements, and are not evidence about Toollery's study; (d) under the working objective (model-visible tokens) the multiplier is not in the criterion at all, so this correction affects the **billed-cost report** and the claim "cached expose-all is the strongest rival", which was a billed-cost claim (see `03`).
**Records superseded:** the sentences quoted above.
