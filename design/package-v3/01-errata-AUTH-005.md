## 0. Working objective for this revision (an instruction, not a finding)
By the human's instruction in AUTH-006 (not a supervisor quotation; the noninferiority margin `delta` is **not** decided): **minimise cumulative model-visible input tokens over complete trajectories while preserving end-to-end task success, including all decision and discovery overhead.** Made operational as follows (choices of the lead, open to change):
- **Primary cost estimand `C_tok(a)`:** expectation over tasks of the sum, over every model call of a trajectory, of the input tokens of that call as reported by the provider's usage fields (system prompt, tool block, contract, history, tool results), **plus** the same for every LLM-based decider call (selector, judge, classifier) and every discovery or recovery exchange. A discovery that causes an extra model call counts that call's whole input. Trajectories are complete and include failed ones; cost is never conditioned on success. Non-LLM compute (embedding, retrieval, latency) is recorded, not in the criterion.
- **Design-time cost.** The cost of running the design-time arm comparison (node D4 of the graph) and of tuning any estimator or selector is reported as `O_dev` and charged as `O_dev / N_dep` per deployed trajectory (`N_dep` a human declaration), with a sensitivity analysis over `N_dep`. `C_tok` is reported with and without it.
- **Primary constraint, mean and strata.** End-to-end success `Y(a) >= Y_ref - delta` (formulation F1) **and** the same noninferiority within each pre-registered stratum (formulation F7) with undecided stratum margins. Without the stratum guard a mean margin can be met by dropping exactly the rare capability that reintroduction exists to recover (tail dilution), or by failing the most expensive tasks. Strata are fixed before any run (a rare-capability stratum and a trajectory-length stratum at least); they are not defined by what an arm exposed.
- **Recorded separately, never merged into one number:** tool count per call; token categories (tool metadata, contract and scope statement, history, tool outputs, selector/judge, discovery, recovery); billed cost under named prices; cache behaviour (hit rate, read and write tokens).
- **Consequence for v2.** v2 used billed cost as the default criterion ("O1") with exposure count as an alternative ("O2"). The cache terms `w`, `r`, `pi_c`, `c_def` in v2's A1 describe **billed cost**, not model-visible tokens; they leave the criterion and move to the billed-cost report. Deferred definitions that are not placed in the context window are not model-visible (the vendor's own description of deferral, EXT-0009, a vendor claim). Natural-replay evidence that token reduction need not reduce billed cost (EXT-0062: 2,196 vs 87,074 input tokens, billed $0.000819 vs $0.000749, difference inconclusive) is a reason to record the two separately, not evidence for either.

## 1. Correction 1: success and cost were combined in one equation
**Where.** v2 `03-evaluation-outline.md` sections 3 and 4 (and DPR-0005 6.2-6.3, HYP-0011, HYP-0012).
**What it said.** Population net effect `w (V - P1) - (1 - w) U` with break-even `w* = U / (V - P1 + U)`, and for two initiators `w_SM = dU / (dR - dP + dU)`.
**Defect.** `V` is a gain in success probability (dimensionless); `P1` and `U` are costs (tokens or money). The sums and ratios add and divide quantities in different units. No exchange rate was stated or justified, although the primary rule deliberately has none. The "break-even prevalences" had no meaning.
**Corrected treatment.** Two estimands per arm `a` and stratum `s` in {M (miss), N (no-miss)}: success `y_a^s` (probability) and cost `c_a^s` (tokens per trajectory). With prevalence `w` of stratum M in the declared population:
- `Y_a(w) = w y_a^M + (1-w) y_a^N` (success units); `C_a(w) = w c_a^M + (1-w) c_a^N` (token units). Linear in `w`; never added.
- **Decision under F1 at the estimated prevalence `w_hat`:** (1) feasible set `Phi = { a : the lower confidence bound of Y_a(w_hat) - Y_ref(w_hat) is >= -delta, and the same holds in every pre-registered stratum }`; (2) among feasible arms choose the lowest `C_a(w_hat)` (with `O_dev / N_dep` charged); if `Phi` is empty report the frontier and conclude that no arm meets noninferiority.
- **Break-even prevalences exist only within one unit.** *Admissibility* (success units): let `l^s = y_ref^s - y_none^s` be the success loss of the no-recovery arm in stratum `s`. Then `Y_ref(w) - Y_none(w) = l^N + w (l^M - l^N)`, and no recovery is inadmissible when this exceeds `delta`, i.e. when `w > w_adm = (delta - l^N) / (l^M - l^N)` provided `l^M > l^N`. Edge cases: if `l^M <= l^N` the loss does not grow with `w` and there is no crossing; if `w_adm` falls outside [0,1] the no-recovery arm is admissible for all `w` (or for none, according to `l^N` against `delta`). *Tokens:* recovery uses fewer expected tokens than no recovery iff `DeltaC(w) = w dc^M + (1-w) dc^N < 0`; a crossing in [0,1] exists only if `dc^M` and `dc^N` have opposite signs (then `w_C = dc^N / (dc^N - dc^M)`). If recovery costs more tokens in both strata (the usual case) it is never cheaper and is justified, if at all, only by admissibility at `w_hat`.
- **Initiators** (system trigger `S` vs model-emitted request `M`): `DeltaY(w) = Y_S - Y_M` and `DeltaC(w) = C_S - C_M`, each a function of `w` in its own unit. `S` dominates `M` at `w_hat` only if the confidence interval of `DeltaY` lies at or above 0 **and** the interval of `DeltaC` lies at or below 0 (dominance is stated on intervals, not point estimates; an interval containing 0 is a tie, not a win). Otherwise apply the F1 rule above; report the two zero-crossings `w_Y` and `w_C` separately. A mechanism with higher recovery and higher token cost is not dominated; no scalar break-even exists without an exchange rate.
- **Exchange rate.** A scalar `J = lambda DeltaY - DeltaC` (`lambda` in tokens per unit success) would give one break-even `w_J`. **No exchange rate is justified in the record or by the human; none is adopted.**
- **Strata when no core exists.** The reference partition in v2 was "outside a dev-frequency core". With no need statistics (correction 4) that definition is empty. Strata are then defined scorer-side by the **group (server or namespace) of the reference-required tools** and, where statistics exist, by rarity; fixed before the run, applied identically to all arms. The prevalence `w` is the share of the miss stratum under whichever partition is declared.
- **Prevalence** is also a transportability report: curves over `w` show how conclusions move with the deployment's miss prevalence; the test is at the declared population's `w_hat`.
**Records superseded:** the equations above in v2 `03` sections 3-4, DPR-0005 6.2-6.3, HYP-0011/0012 falsifier wording that refers to a "net".

## 2. Correction 2: the missed-tool probability is not a bound on success loss
**Where.** v2 `01-policy-candidate.md` sections 2, 4 (notes under the decision table), 8 and 9; DPR-0004 4.2 "mapping of epsilon"; v2 `05` section 2 last row; v2 `06` A6.
**What it said.** `M(F) = sum over deferred tools of p_t q_t`; expected success loss "at most `M(F)` times the loss when a needed tool is missing, at most 1"; hence `epsilon := delta` is a "sufficient, conservative mapping", "conservative only if the frequency estimates are unbiased".
**Defects.** (a) `M(F)` is a *coverage surrogate*; it does not concern whether a task succeeds once a tool is reached. (b) The inequality omitted the residual term, so it did not bound the success difference. (c) `delta` is a margin on the *total* success difference; spending all of it on one source of loss leaves nothing for the others, so `epsilon = delta` could not be conservative even with exact estimates. (d) Unbiasedness is not the only requirement for estimating `M(F)`.
**Corrected statement.** Let `Y_ref`, `Y_CA` be the success indicators of the reference policy and of the candidate on the same task. A tool `t` is *reference-needed* (`R_t`) if the **successful** reference run called it. For each deferred tool define `p_t = P(R_t)` and `q_t = P(t is not reached in the candidate run | R_t)`, where "not reached" means its definition is not in the model's context when it is needed, **for any cause**: ranker miss, model non-initiation of search, search-budget or turn-budget exhaustion. Then, with no assumption beyond these definitions:
`Y_ref - Y_CA <= P(Y_ref = 1, Y_CA = 0) <= M(F) + I`, with `M(F) = sum_t p_t q_t` and `I = P(Y_ref = 1, Y_CA = 0, every reference-needed tool is reached)`.
The first inequality drops the gain term `P(Y_ref = 0, Y_CA = 1)`, so it bounds the **gross** loss; the second splits on whether some reference-needed tool is unreached and applies the union bound (valid under any dependence; the sum may exceed 1, then it is vacuous). `I` is a **residual**, not "interference": it contains interference (contract and scope-statement tokens, search turns and budgets, distraction by appended results, argument errors), route divergence (the candidate's trajectory needs a tool the reference did not, and misses it), and any other failure with all reference-needed tools reached. **Nothing in the record bounds `I`.**
What makes the *estimate* `M_hat(F)` usable as a surrogate (these are conditions of estimation and slack, not assumptions of the identity): (i) upper confidence bounds for `p` and `q`; (ii) transport from dev to the deployment population; (iii) `q` is estimated **for the configuration being evaluated**, because it depends on the deferred set, ranker, contract and model (correction 3); (iv) the **unseen tail**: tools never called in dev have `p_hat = 0` but not `p = 0`, and when many rarely needed tools are deferred their summed true mass can be large; a missing-mass term (for example a Good-Turing-type estimate of the probability that a session needs a tool unseen in dev) must be added, otherwise `M_hat` is biased **down**, which is the unconservative direction; (v) alternative routes make the coverage term conservative (an unreached reference-needed tool need not cause failure) but do not bound `I`.
**Consequences.** `epsilon := delta` is **withdrawn**. `epsilon` is a tuning parameter of a candidate generator, not a safety guarantee. Noninferiority is established **only end-to-end**: A1 supplies an ordering of tools and the surface size is chosen by sweeping that ordering on dev and taking the smallest-token surface whose *measured held-out dev* success (mean and strata) satisfies the constraint, then confirming on a disjoint test set. v2's sweep arm (P-sweep) becomes the selection stage; A1 contributes only the ordering.

## 3. Correction 3: A1's greedy core-selection rule is not cost-minimising and has no safety property
**Where.** v2 `01` section 4 (A1 row, "Step 2"), DPR-0004 4.2.
**What it said.** Step 1: keep `t` in the core iff `keep_t <= disc_t`. Step 2: while `M(F) > epsilon`, move to the core the tool with the largest `p_t q_t / keep_t`.
**Analysis.** With per-trajectory costs `keep_t` (cost of keeping `t` visible) and `disc_t` (expected cost of discovering it when deferred), the constrained problem is: choose the deferred set `D` to minimise `sum_{t not in D} keep_t + sum_{t in D} disc_t` subject to `sum_{t in D} m_t <= epsilon`, `m_t = p_t q_t`. Equivalently **maximise `sum_{t in D} (keep_t - disc_t)` subject to `sum_{t in D} m_t <= epsilon`: a 0/1 knapsack (covering) problem.** The *incremental* cost of moving `t` from deferred to core is `keep_t - disc_t` (the lost saving), **not** `keep_t`. Therefore (i) Step 1 is optimal only for the unconstrained additive problem; (ii) Step 2's index uses the gross cost `keep_t`, the wrong denominator; the index matching the fractional relaxation is `m_t / (keep_t - disc_t)`, largest first, over tools with positive saving; (iii) even that greedy is a heuristic for the 0/1 problem.
**Demonstration (illustrative numbers, tokens per trajectory; not empirical; checked by hand by the challenger).** Four tools `(keep, disc, m)`: `t0 (21, 3, 3)`, `t1 (95, 6, 5)`, `t2 (23, 9, 8)`, `t3 (96, 93, 7)`, `epsilon = 18`. Exhaustive search over all cores meeting the constraint: optimum cost 114 with core `{t3}`. The rule as written moves `t2` and costs 125. The incremental-index greedy finds 114 here. On 3,000 random six-tool instances (uniform integer parameters, illustrative; the challenger did not re-run it) the rule as written was suboptimal in 1,713 and the incremental-index greedy in 1,023.
**Couplings that break the independence assumed by the formulation.** (a) `q_t` depends on the deferred set `D` (competition in the ranker): for a *fixed-score* ranker, removing competitors cannot lower a remaining tool's rank, so `q` estimated on a larger deferred set is conservative; for rankers with corpus statistics, and for model behaviour (the scope statement changes with `D`, as does the model's tendency to search) the direction is **unknown**. (b) Co-needed tools: one search returns `kappa` results, so additive `disc_t` overstates the joint cost of deferring tools that one search retrieves together; this biases the rule **toward a larger core**. (c) `p_t` are correlated; the union bound tolerates this for coverage, not for cost. (d) Trajectory length `T_bar` and the context length at discovery depend on the surface.
**What is and is not established.** Not established: optimality of A1 as written, any approximation guarantee for it, and any safety property of the resulting core. The corrected index belongs to the family of greedy heuristics for the minimum knapsack-cover problem, which have known guarantees in the **additive, independent** model; that model does not hold here (couplings), so no guarantee transfers. A1 is **a heuristic ordering of tools**; the cut is made by the end-to-end sweep (correction 2).
**Token form of A1 (replaces v2's billed-cost form under the working objective; revised after CH-0035).** Notation (tokens): `L_t` definition length; `T_bar` expected model calls per trajectory; `I_disc` expected input tokens of the call at which the model emits a search request, **excluding** the appended content (the whole prompt at that point: system, core, contract, interface, history); `tau` expected number of calls *after* that call, each of which re-reads the appended content; `A_t = d_s + L_t + (kappa - 1) L_D` the tokens one discovery of `t` adds to the context, where `d_s` is the search request plus result wrapper **excluding** the returned definitions, `kappa` the search width, `L_D` the mean length of a returned deferred definition; `phi_t` the tokens spent on unsuccessful searches in sessions that need `t` but fail to reach it.
- Keeping `t` visible: `keep_t = L_t T_bar`.
- Deferring `t`: `disc_t = p_t (1 - q_t) [ I_disc + tau A_t ] + phi_t`. The first term inside the bracket is the **extra model call** a discovery causes (it re-reads the whole prompt once); the second is the persisting content, re-read by every later call. (The first draft of this record omitted `I_disc`; the challenger showed that this alone can reverse the sign of the saving for tools with short definitions in long contexts.) `phi_t = p_t q_t B_fail (I_disc + d_s tau)` with `B_fail` the mean number of failed searches; it is a failure-path cost, distinct from the success constraint.
- Saving from deferring `t`: `s_t = keep_t - disc_t`. A tool is worth deferring only if `s_t > 0`.
- **Defer-at-all test.** Deferral needs a search interface and scope statement every call, a common overhead `Omega = (L_S + sigma) T_bar` (`L_S` interface schema tokens, `sigma` scope-statement tokens). Defer anything only if `sum over the chosen deferred set of s_t > Omega`; otherwise the configuration is expose-all (or the static core without an interface). This test is part of the rule.
- The knapsack, counterexample and couplings above apply unchanged. All terms are in tokens; the same expressions with prices and cache multipliers give the billed-cost report.

## 4. Correction 4: C-A's core rule is not applicable to the original problem as specified
**Where.** v2 `01` sections 2, 4 (definitions under the table), 7 (row `p_t`), 9; DPR-0004 4.2 "Where `pi_ref` cannot run".
**Defect.** Rule A1 needs `p_t` and `q_t` from successful dev runs of the **reference policy**. The original problem is the large catalogue; on the closest vehicle the flat catalogue (about 292k tokens by the repository's estimate) exceeds the context of **2 of its 4 matrix models** (S4G line 28), so a reference run exists for the other two; the supervisor's own example (10,000 tools) is larger still. Where the reference cannot run, `p_t` is censored, and v2 resolved this by restricting the claim to catalogues where the reference (or a named substitute) runs, "also where the rival is feasible". That restriction removes C-A from the regime that motivates the thesis.
**Corrected conclusion.** The core stage is applicable only where an estimator of need exists. The record specifies none for large scale. Candidate estimators, **none specified or validated here**: (a) need labelled on a **disjoint training catalogue or split** with outcomes (class D; legitimate only if kept apart from evaluation); (b) **group-level** need (server or namespace), where statistics are denser than per tool; (c) **unseen-mass** estimators for the long tail (correction 2 (iv)); (d) a reference run on **sub-catalogues** that fit, composed; (e) logs of a previously deployed policy under an **exploration** rule (logs from a policy that exposes a subset are censored by that subset). **Minimal missing decision for the core stage at large scale: a legitimate source of need statistics that is neither the evaluation set nor the reference run.** What would resolve it: one of (a)-(e), shown on a catalogue small enough that the reference runs to recover the reference-based estimates.
**Scope.** The same absent reference weakens the **noninferiority claim of every arm** at large scale: F1 is then relative to a named substitute and is a weaker claim, for G and C-A alike (see `03`). What differs is that G's mechanisms do not need `p_t`; C-A's core rule does.
**Not claimed:** that need statistics cannot be obtained, only that the record specifies no estimator for them.

## 5. Correction 5: the Toollery "cache multiplier" is a fitted, study-specific parameter
**Where.** v2 `02` sections 3 and 5, `04` row 11, `05` section 2 (second row), `07` slide 2; DPR-0004 3.2a; DPR-0005 section 2.
**What it said.** A linear model "implies a cache-read multiplier of about 0.0088", then used as a price: "at that multiplier the whole warm tool block costs about 770 uncached-token equivalents"; "C-A cannot win on billed tool-block cost at that price".
**Corrected statement.** In Toollery Table 15 (EXT-0062: single-step BFCL replay, per-request means, 600/300/300 calls; the model, pricing and candidate-list construction are **not in the stored text**) a two-parameter linear model (base price per token from the cache-suppressed full-library row, a read multiplier from the warm rows) fits as follows: **two cells are used to fit** (cache-suppressed and forced-warm full-library rows); the natural-replay full-library row gives an independent value of the multiplier (0.00887 against 0.00866, 2.5% apart); the natural-replay and forced-warm restricted rows are two further out-of-sample predictions (within 2%); the cache-suppressed restricted row is a near-trivial token-ratio check. So there are **three non-trivial checks, not six reproductions** (lead recomputation; the challenger reproduced the fit but not the simulation in correction 3). The value is a **fitted effective parameter of that study** under a linear model that omits write premiums, latency and threshold behaviour, with no stated uncertainty. It is **not** a verified billing rate and says nothing about any provider tested later. Billed-cost arguments are therefore written as functions of the unknown read multiplier `r` (the closed forms stand as algebra); the regime in which a restriction pays in **billed** cost is a function of `r`, cache state, traffic and horizon, to be measured per provider; the vendor-page multipliers (EXT-0021) are the vendor's published prices, not evidence about Toollery's study. Under the working objective the multiplier is not in the criterion at all; this correction affects the **billed-cost report** and the v2 claim that cached expose-all is the strongest rival, which was a billed-cost claim (see `03`).
**Records superseded:** the sentences quoted above.
