# B. Evidence-to-decision analysis

Status: **provisional**, lead-assembled from DPR-0002/0003 (design-proposer), the challenges CH-0007..0019 and review RV-0002 (design-challenger), and the lead's evidence map SUM-0002 (`00-evidence-map.md`, an **unverified single-reader** map; the evidence-steward ran out of turns, see README). "(read)" = the proposer or lead read the extract text; "(map)" = relied on the map without re-reading; "(challenger)" = cited by the challenger from governing copies and not re-read at the original. Vendor documentation is a vendor claim, not a measurement (CAVEATS 8).

Reading key for the **Status** column: *supported* = a read extract states it at its own level; *plausible* = reasoning plus partial evidence; *untested* = a hypothesis with a falsifier; *unsupported* = the evidence does not back it; *open* = a human or supervisor choice.

## B2. What the evidence cannot yet justify
- A rule for **when** restriction pays, a threshold, or a stage count.
- Any claim that a second filtering stage pays, or that it does not.
- That models call a search interface when a needed tool is absent.
- That a system-side trigger recovers misses at lower price than model initiation.
- Billed-cost savings from any restriction under caching.
- Novelty of any combination (see D).

## B3. Evidence not to lean on
Dynamic ReAct efficiency/accuracy claims (CAVEATS 10); Tool Attention's "empirical" figures (ledger C3-28); the Anthropic "85%" and "49->74%" figures (ledger K4, K10); CMTF's 85.7% as support for a runtime policy; Speakeasy's "100x" as a task-level cost measurement; every supervisor example number (10,000 tools, stage numbers, tier sizes).
