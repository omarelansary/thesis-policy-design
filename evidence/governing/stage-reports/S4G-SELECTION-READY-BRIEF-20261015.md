## 1. Bottom line
1. No candidate is invalid, and none is *established* as novel or feasible. All six remain scientifically credible in some role; they differ in whether they can carry a thesis alone.
2. **Standalone-capable** (can be the primary question): C2, C3 (restricted to client-side classes), C6 (only under a policy-design reading of the task). **Supporting** (explains or protects a primary question, weak alone on the closest vehicle): C1, C4, C5.
3. 5. The closest vehicle (DynamicMCPBench) supports parts of A and B with modification, C with substantial modification, and C4 not at all. ## 5. Option cards
### 5.1 C2: the value and price of a recovery path
- **New knowledge (exact):** how much of the success lost to an omitting first exposure a recovery path restores, what it adds to cumulative model-visible tokens by category and to turns, against (i) no recovery and (ii) a static exposure matched on cumulative tokens, measured separately on tasks with a miss and without, with natural and induced misses reported apart.
- **Nearest prior art and remaining novelty uncertainty (F):** FitText (pass rate 57.6 static, then 62.1 single-pass, 70.7 multi-turn re-retrieval, 77.6 and 84.3 for two other dynamic variants; tokens 26.1k for single-pass to 60.5k for the last variant, static-arm tokens not reported; confounded by a raw-query static baseline, an LLM judge on a simulated server, internal inconsistencies); ComplexMCP (iterative `retrieve_tools` is on-demand discovery from the start; 36.88 vs 27.65 at k=60 vs 55.31 full for one model; the Claude gain +0.70 is inside one task's resolution); PlanBench-XL (enforced extra exploration: most models improve by less than 5 points); SING/MCP-Zero; Executor; provider tool search. Unk: vendor-internal evaluations; the worth of "distinctive design elements" if novelty (D-g) is read as a new mechanism **[User][Sup]**.
- **Principal falsifier:** recovery restores little, or is dominated by the equal-cumulative-token static comparator, or restores nearly all at negligible price (question closed). All reportable. A separate threat (not a falsifier): results describe one harness and prompt.
- **Minimum valid design (I; illustration, not a plan):** one effect-scored vehicle; initial exposure by a restricted policy with natural misses (the vehicle's own rag at small k produces them) plus an induced-miss stratum; arms no-recovery, recovery, static at matched cumulative tokens (calibration tolerance stated before running), all-tools reference where feasible; strata miss and no-miss; at least two models of different strength; repeats; paired analysis with tokens by category; restored fraction with Fieller intervals. Parametric size: about 157 paired tasks per contrast at a 10-point margin, discordance 0.20, one run (P2 arithmetic; the margin is open and smaller margins need many more).
- **Vehicle:** **needs modification** (moderate): a deterministic search/describe tool or a miss-triggered system recovery, a loop in which the tool list can change, a turn budget decision (recovery calls consume turns), per-call usage, costed rag embeddings, real schemas. Replay can serve a recovered reference tool deterministically; recovery to an unexercised alternative cannot be scored. Caution: in the induced-miss stratum the no-recovery arm is near zero by construction, and an agent that finds an unexercised alternative is scored as failing, so replay may inflate the measured value of recovery; this must be reported as a limit of the vehicle, not of recovery.
- **Other candidates needed as support:** C1's forced-absence arm (to define the miss stratum and loss budget); C5 as a pre-registered robustness section. C4 not needed.
- **Unconvincing outcomes:** gain only under injected misses; matched comparator badly calibrated; one model; no no-miss arm; a replication of FitText with the same simulator-and-judge confounds; recovery gains that are explained by the replay design (reference-tool re-surfacing credited, unexercised alternatives scored zero) or by the turn budget.

### 5.2 C3: exposure-policy classes on the (cumulative tokens, success) plane
- **New knowledge:** which client-side exposure-policy classes (static full where feasible, retrieval-restricted, deferred or hierarchical) are non-dominated on cumulative tokens by category and end-to-end success over trajectories, and whether the non-dominated set changes with catalogue size and model strength.
- **Nearest prior art and uncertainty (F):** DynamicMCPBench Appendix N (rag/hier/flat at 150 tasks, flat on one model); Skill Blocks (controlled, but skills, not tools); ToolMenuBench (synthetic); MemTool; compgateway (tokens, no success); vendor claims. Unk: the increment may be a reporting increment (token categories, repeats, scale); transfer from skills to tool catalogues is assumed by C3 and not examined.
- **Principal falsifier:** one class dominates throughout; differences within noise at available power; the frontier unstable across models or harness.
- **Minimum valid design:** at least three classes on one vehicle at two catalogue sizes, two models, repeats; non-inferiority and token contrasts per class pair with an intersection-union test; token vector by category including rag/hier overhead; session length is **not** available on this vehicle (single-task runs), so the horizon is per trajectory unless built (Unk). Margin open.
- **Vehicle:** rag, hier, flat and description-level classes **exist** (they are the classes Appendix N already ran, so the increment is instrumentation, scale and repeats, not the classes); a deferred class with an on-demand schema needs the same changing-tool-list build as A; instrumentation, real schemas, overhead costing (including `--desc-level b` normalisation calls) **need modification**; provider-native and code-mediated classes **cannot run in this loop** and their accounting comparability is unidentified (programmatic results are excluded from provider usage), so they are a scope boundary or need a different harness.
- **Support needed:** C5 is effectively inherent (accounting conventions); C1's presence term supplies the size covariate; C2 only if recovery belongs to a class.
- **Unconvincing outcomes:** reproduces Appendix N with finer tables; flat arm infeasible on most models so the baseline is a curated pool; implementation quality confounded with class; margin unattainable; replay scoring and the 12-turn budget unaddressed; a client-side-only design that omits the existing code-mediated project the supervisor names as a baseline, without saying so.

### 5.3 C6: a stepwise exposure-policy family
- **New knowledge:** which steps of an ordered staged-disclosure policy pay (under a reading of "pays" fixed before running), whether they interact, and whether a composed policy chosen without looking at the evaluation tasks is non-dominated against all-tools and a strong static restriction.
- **Nearest prior art and uncertainty (F):** Executor and vendor deferral (shipped mechanisms); Skill Blocks hybrid stubs (controlled, skills; net saving sign varies with regime); the on-device ablation (a compound token-saving step lowers tool-invocation F1 in two categories; Combined best only in some; non-additive); ITR (model unnamed); Tool Attention (projected success). Mechanism novelty is low by construction. Unk: the supervisor's meaning of "each step saves tokens" (per step, or collectively over turns) **[Sup]**.
- **Principal falsifier:** no step pays net of overhead; the composed policy is dominated by static restriction or all-tools; step effects unstable across models; or the "pays" reading was chosen after seeing results (invalidates the claim).
- **Minimum valid design (I):** a small number of steps (not fixed here), add-one and leave-one-out ablations with a regime factor (catalogue size, load probability), a held-out composition test, symmetric tuning of the static baseline, a pre-registered reading of "pays" from the five admissible ones (a)-(e) in the quality addendum. Interactions need fractional designs once steps exceed a few.
- **Vehicle:** **needs substantial modification**: tiered visibility (name, description, schema), expansion trigger, recovery (shared with C2), per-call logging; description-level normalisation covers only part. Replay can serve expansions of reference tools.
- **Support needed:** C2 (the recovery step is C2's toggle), C3 baselines, C5.
- **Unconvincing outcomes:** per-step results that are token arithmetic; composition tuned on the same tasks; steps that merely replicate what vendors ship on one model; replay-only conclusions with no discussion of the unexercised-route and turn-budget effects. (Whether the supervisor intends his illustrative tiers as hypotheses is a question for him, not an assumption here.)

## 8. Strongest objections I see before the critic (to be tested, not conclusions)
1. Replay scoring (success only on routes the single reference exercised) and the 12-turn budget may distort every comparison that involves discovery or recovery (A, B and C), in directions that are not established. If they cannot be bounded, the options are additional recorded reference traces, the simulator or live mode, each giving up some determinism.
2. A's core effect is partly covered; its distinctive elements may be judged a methodological increment unless the matched-cumulative-token comparator proves hard to calibrate and the result is surprising either way.
3. The distinctions among A, B, C may be cosmetic: all three need the same instrumentation and recovery build, so the real choice is one of claim type and scope, not of cost.
4. Classifying C5 as "not standalone" depends on the arithmetic objection; if non-arithmetic content (evaluator error, prefix mutation) can be reached from existing logs, C5's role could be larger.
5. The "minimum designs" use parametric illustrations that depend on discordance, a margin and a replay-usable task count, none of which are known.
