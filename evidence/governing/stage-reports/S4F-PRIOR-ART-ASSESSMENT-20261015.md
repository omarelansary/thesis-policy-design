## C1. Absence versus presence of tools

**Question as framed:** how is end-to-end success loss under restricted exposure split between absence of required tools and presence of other tools, as simple effects with an interaction.

**What the sources establish (F)**
- DynamicMCPBench (dev-branch matrix doc, Appendix N/S of the paper). Reachability (every required effect has at least one member of its equivalence set exposed) is a hard gate: 0 passes in 1,064 single-attempt unreachable cells, 1 in 1,905 pooled (e9.1 doc). Success rises monotonically with retrieval budget for all four models; k=8 loss versus curated is -16.0 to -23.3 points; flat (all 1,168) on one model costs 8.0 points with a per-cell interval of about +-8 (e9.1 doc; paper Table 7). The decomposition into "retrieval loss" and "distraction alone" is labelled in the repo document as **post-hoc, not pre-registered**. Appendix S sweeps the share of spurious alternatives in the offered pool from 0 to 1 over five models (n=350 per cell; accuracy stays within its interval) and runs a six-strategy distractor ablation on two models; the pre-registered hypothesis that hard negatives would raise the server-attribution error (SAE) rate by at least 15 points was rejected (observed gap 0.6 points; paper L1198-L1240). Scope stated by the authors: "Both probes vary the composition of a pool that already contains the tools each task needs." These probes vary composition at a fixed pool; they do not vary presence or count.
- ComplexMCP (`r3-complexmcp`): 47 stateful MCP tasks, 315 tools, three runs per model. Distractor count is swept from zero to all tools on four models, and retrieval truncation is evaluated separately; the two are not crossed; whether the gold tool is always present in the sweep is not stated; numbers appear only in a figure (worker-reported, U).
- Enrich-Retrieve-Rank (`s4f-2b-enrich-retrieve-rank-2608-22695`, appendix C): on a 7,278-tool registry Match@1 about 0.39 is explained as about 0.56 (gold in the top 15) times about 0.70 (reranker accuracy given presence). Selection only, no task success.
- PlanBench-XL (`s4b-wd-planbenchxl`): before failed non-progress calls the model had already retrieved a useful tool in 78.0% (default) and 71.1% (block) of cases, and recently retrieved tools account for 74.1% and 63.6% of non-progress calls; "the bottleneck is therefore not only tool discovery" (L655-L690). Blocking replaces tools with alternatives while discovery stays on (L289-L304).
- ToolMATH (stored): presence conditions (gold-only, gold-present, distractors-only) across models, but tasks are solvable without tools and the Adaptability figures come from three non-frontier models. CAM-DF: a 7-read-tool setting. ITR (`s4-itr…`): tools-correct 74/57/45 (baseline) versus 84/82/76 (ITR) at 8/40/120 tools, with count, tokens and absence confounded. NTILC (`s4f-2b-ntilc…`): in-context top-1 falls 1.000 to 0.870 as tools go from 10 to 250 and registry tokens from 667 to 11,575, with count and tokens confounded.
- Direct 2x2 searches (three logged queries): none found. The nearest conditions are "no tool" versus "distractor only" with the needed tool always absent, measuring hallucination rather than success (SimpleToolHallu, via search result; not fetched).

**Vehicle caveats that bear on C1 (2a):** on the vehicle, absence is failure by construction (single-trace replay cache; unexposed tools rejected), distractors outside the reference trace carry stub schemas, and pool size co-varies with similarity; the -8.0 flat estimate has per-cell intervals of about +-8, no paired interval, and was run on one model although a second feasible model exists.

**What remains untested (I):** a manipulated factorial of required-tool presence by distractor load with end-to-end success on tool-calling tasks; a token-matched or schema-realistic version of the presence term (the vehicle's distractors carry minimal schemas, 2a); catalogue-scale behaviour of the presence term (Appendix S is within pools that already contain the needed tools, at modest pool composition).

**Assessment:** the *decomposition idea* (recall times conditional selection) and the *absence cost* are established in several forms; the *presence term* has a close null result at small pools for current models; the *manipulated factorial* was not found. The candidate's claimed contribution is **partly covered**; its remaining content is the interaction and the catalogue-scale presence term, which the closest evidence does not establish either way (Appendix S is composition at a fixed pool; the only catalogue-scale contrast is one model with wide intervals and understated token load). Uncertainty: three queries is a thin search for a gap whose natural vocabulary varies; leads not followed: TRAJECT-Bench, the ToolBench retriever pilot, TheMCPCompany's GPT-5-versus-small-model retrieval result.

## C2. Value and price of a recovery path

**What the sources establish (F)**
- FitText (`r1-fittext`, StableToolBench, 16,464 APIs, 765 solvable tasks, GPT-5.4-mini headline, solver repeats not stated, success by a GPT-5.4-mini judge with eight evaluations per task, a virtual API server with cached plus LLM-simulated responses). Pass rate: static query 57.6, single-pass 62.1, multi-turn re-retrieval 70.7, scattershot 77.6, memetic 84.3 (Table 1, L489-L493). Tokens including the downstream solver: 26.1k (single-pass), 45.2k, 45.6k, 60.5k (Table S14, L1696-L1703). Static retrieval "changes its frame in 0 of 2,047 invocations"; first-tool accuracy stays within about a 5.5-point band; the authors conclude the gain comes from recovery after feedback (L1744-L1752). Among wrong-first-tool tasks, the correct tool is present in the top 5 for about 7% (G2-category) and about 23% (G2-instruction) under static retrieval versus 44 to 83 and 65 to 80 under dynamic variants (Table 2, L540-L549). Inconsistencies in the same source: the stated unsolved rates (45.9% to 34.4%) do not match Table 1's complements (42.4% and 15.7%), memetic is below scattershot on G2-instruction (65 versus 80), and on a legacy solver memetic is below static (32.2 versus 34.8) (worker-reported). No static comparator at equal cumulative tokens; tasks without a miss are not separated (U).
- ComplexMCP (Tables 2-3, L350-L440): one-shot RAG versus an iterative `retrieve_tools` tool offered from the start (on-demand discovery, not recovery after a restricted initial exposure; reviewer-reported, L326-L331). Gemini-3-flash success: full context 55.31 (L283-L285), RAG k=30 13.47, k=60 27.65, iterative 36.88 (the iterative variant closes about a third of the k=60-to-full gap); Claude-opus-4: full 41.84, k=30 11.34 (worker-reported), k=60 24.82, iterative 25.52 (+0.70, within the 2.13-point per-task resolution). 47 tasks (resolution 2.13 points per task), SD over three runs; per-component average tokens only (the iterative variant's prompt tokens 334 versus 6,795 at k=60, tool-response tokens similar); cumulative tokens not reported.
- ITR: fallback row in a confounded ablation (miss rate 6.1% without fallback at one exposed tool; 2.4% with fallback at two; no success or token cost of the fallback itself).
- PlanBench-XL: enforced extra exploration after a wrong termination adds "less than 5 percentage points" (L537-L558); agents often fail to recover even when a useful tool re-appears.
- TheMCPCompany: Missed Tools are 12.5% of 24 analysed failures (3 tasks); instruction drift dominates; agents with a missed tool often acknowledge and terminate (TheMCPCompany text L1138-L1155, as cited by the S4E critic).
- Tool-Reflection and PALADIN concern recovery from execution errors, not from missing-tool exposure (search results only; not fetched).
- Three logged queries across C2 (S4F-Q004, Q005, Q010): no with-versus-without fallback study that reports success and cumulative tokens on a tool catalogue at equal tokens was found.

**What remains untested (I):** a static comparator at equal *cumulative* tokens; over-search cost on tasks with no miss; deterministic effect-scored MCP catalogues; token categories; system-initiated versus model-initiated recovery as a factor; whether FitText's recovery gain survives an evaluator that is not an LLM judge over a simulated server.

**Assessment (revised after review):** the core effect "re-retrieval after feedback raises success at a measurable token price" is **partly covered**, with FitText the closest and most direct instance in a retrieval setting; it is confounded (static retrieval uses the raw query while dynamic variants reformulate it; success is judged by an LLM against an LLM-simulated server; the numbers contain internal inconsistencies, above), and ComplexMCP and ITR are supporting but weaker or confounded evidence. A presence-without-use control design exists in another setting (Tool-Use Tax's NoopTool chain: CoT, function-call style, no-op tool, full tools; text distractors, one to four tools; `s4f-2b-tooluse-tax-2605-00136` L164-L182). The candidate's distinctive design elements (equal-cumulative-token comparator, no-miss tasks, natural versus induced misses, deterministic scoring, token categories) were **not found covered**. PlanBench-XL's result suggests recovery value is bounded by selection, not only by retrieval.

## C5. Stability of conclusions across accounting conventions, evaluators and horizons

**What the sources establish (F)**
- Effective Token Cost (`s4f-2b-effective-token-cost-2601-14224`): ETC = non-cached input + alpha x cached input + beta x output with alpha in {0.1, 0.3, 0.5} and beta in {3, 5, 7}; the authors state trends are "nearly identical" across the grid, with an exception at alpha at least 0.3 and reranking depth 20 (L91-L98, L182-L187, L562-L568). Scope note (review-corrected): the "fewer than 3% of input tokens are cached" remark belongs to the reranking-effectiveness analysis (Recall@5); end-to-end, search inputs are mostly cached, and with alpha at least 0.3 reranking at depth 20 shifts the trade-off curve (L476-L480, L560-L568), so ETC contains an actual convention-dependent conclusion. The abstract says reranking "reduces end-to-end token cost" while raw token totals in Table 7 rise with reranking (worker-reported; the reduction is relative to raising reasoning effort). One run per configuration; Table 2's best value equals the highest of five runs (worker-reported).
- Scaffold Effect (`s4f-2b-scaffold-effect-2607-22585`): the same two models under three harnesses on 50 tasks, one trial per cell. Pass rates differ by 0 to 8 points with intervals mostly including zero while tokens per solved task differ about 23 to 42 times (Goose 28,142 and 36,950; OpenHands 841,201 and 843,286; OpenCode 1,147,740 and 1,546,977; Table 3). Token total = prompt + completion + cached, failed trials in the numerator, no cached/fresh split, no price weighting, totals only for one harness.
- Agent token spend (`s4f-2b-agent-token-spend-2604-22750`): "cache reads dominate both raw token volume and dollar cost" for one model (costs split into non-cached input, output, cache creation, cache read); output priced about 80 times cache read; no policy ordering is compared across conventions.
- On-device agents (`s4f-2b-ondevice-adaptive-2511-03728`): the same method set under two LLM simulators changes absolute F1 and the relative standing of compression baselines (compression baselines "degrade" under one simulator and "improve" under the other on Multi-Tool; Baseline Multi-Tool F1 0.83 versus 0.51; the metric is tool-invocation F1 against required tools, not task success), and for cloud-delegation the judge score for Combined is 2.68 versus Baseline 3.79 while F1 is 0.99 (worker-reported for the judge table; F1 rows verified).
- ComplexMCP: price ratio cached:uncached:output 0.1:1:6; prompt share 91.9% of tokens versus 87.1% of cost for one configuration (L294-L296, text garbled; worker-reported).
- Token reduction versus cost (r3-tokennotcost, stored): 38.4% fewer delivered tool-output tokens, +6.8% billed cost, CI [+2.8, +11.3]. Validity audit: evaluator-human disagreement 18.5% across deterministic and LLM-judged benchmarks.
- Three logged queries (S4F-Q006, Q007, Q011): no study running the same tool-exposure policies through several evaluators or accounting conventions with rank-stability analysis was found; no reporting standard exists (each paper defines its own convention).

**What remains untested (I):** convention- and evaluator-stability of *tool-exposure policy* conclusions specifically; cache regimes for exposure policies that mutate the prefix; flips at the level of dominance relations.

**Assessment:** each axis has **partial prior art in other domains** (coding agents, deep search, on-device agents); the exposure-specific stability question was **not found**, but the existing results make a mixed outcome likely (invariance where cache share is small, divergence where it is large or where evaluators differ). The arithmetic-risk objection stands: the accounting part is deterministic given logs.

## C6. Stepwise exposure policy: which steps pay, and composition

**What the sources establish (F)**
- On-device agents (L300-L339, L454-L457): component ablation with variants Tool-Efficient (compact schemas with JIT schema loading and full interaction history, a compound step), Memory-Efficient (context-state memory) and Combined, in two simulators, 406 tasks. Initial tool context 3,246 tokens, 2,120 after minification, 347 with JIT (6.1x and 9.4x). Gemini-simulator F1 (cloud / multi-tool / on-device+cloud): baseline 1.00 / 0.83 / 0.88; Tool-Efficient 0.93 / 0.86 / 0.73; Memory-Efficient 1.00 / 0.87 / 0.89; Combined 0.99 / 0.93 / 0.94. So the token-saving step alone **lowers** tool-invocation F1 in two categories and raises it in one, and Combined is best in some categories only (Memory-Efficient is 1.00 versus Combined 0.99 in cloud delegation, and 0.73 versus 0.72 on-device+cloud under the second simulator): effects are non-additive, and a single-step token-sign criterion would have mis-scored the compound JIT step in one category. The method needs a fine-tuned 3B model and simulated users; paired tests are reported without intervals (worker-reported).
- ITR (L617-L628 Table 6): ablation of instruction count, tool count, re-ranker and hybrid retrieval with deltas in "API-success" points and tokens per step; no intervals; no held-out composition; deltas inconsistent with the overall gain (worker-reported: 19 points summed versus 15). Savings fall with horizon: 95.0% at loop 1, 73.1% at 5, 57.0% at 10, 39% at 20 (arithmetic over assumed per-step sizes, L331-L334).
- Tool Attention (`r3-toolattention`): component ablation with measured tokens and **projected** success (a simulation harness, "not a live end-to-end agent evaluation", L496-L499); static pruning without a recovery path is worse than the full-schema baseline in the projection.
- Enrich-Retrieve-Rank: offline enrichment versus raw descriptions: +5.8 / +9.1 / +25.6 points Match@1 at full / hint / name-only exposure on 1,252 queries per cell (McNemar p<1e-6), but +/-0.1, 0.0, -4.4 on public data. Selection only.
- Description-component ablation (smells paper, L1790-L1830, L2468-L2484): 25 runs over five model-domain pairs, five component configurations; compact component combinations "often preserve behavioural reliability while reducing unnecessary token overhead"; the authors state a selection-bias limitation and call for extending component ablations to progressive-disclosure mechanisms (L2334-L2346).
- Two logged queries (Q008, Q009): no study with component ablations of a staged exposure policy reporting per-stage success and cumulative multi-turn tokens on MCP catalogues with deterministic scoring.

**What remains untested (I):** deterministic, multi-turn, cumulative-token component ablation of staged exposure on MCP catalogues with composition validated on held-out tasks; recovery as a scored component without a tokens-only sign rule.

**Assessment (softened after review):** component ablation as a claim type appears in **three** sources of very uneven strength (one measured study with a compound step and F1 as the metric; ITR, which names no model and whose numbers may be arithmetic; Tool Attention, whose success is projected), with the lesson (non-additivity; a token-saving step that costs accuracy alone) visible in the measured one; the *controlled, deterministic, held-out* version was **not found**. Mechanism novelty is low, consistent with the dossier; the contribution would be evidence quality. The on-device result supports the withdrawal of the per-step token-sign criterion.

## Cross-cutting observations
- Evidence quality in this literature is uneven (single runs, no intervals, simulated or projected success, LLM-judge success, internal inconsistencies between abstracts, text and tables). A candidate whose contribution is "carefully controlled evidence" has room, but must expect closely related claims whose numbers do not reconcile.
- Gaps were searched with three or fewer queries each; the vocabulary of this area is fragmented (tools, APIs, skills, capabilities; retrieval, discovery, routing, exposure). "Not found" here carries that limit.
