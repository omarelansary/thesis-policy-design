**Not pre-registered.** This control was added after seeing the matrix, to test an
objection raised against it, and is reported as exploratory.

The objection: every cell runs at a 12-call budget, but 44% of long-bucket tasks
have a reference chain deeper than 12 (max 33). If the budget truncates agents
before they can act on retrieved tools, the long-bucket collapse would be an
artefact of the ceiling rather than of exposure, and H2 would be measuring the
wrong thing.

Re-ran the 50 long-bucket tasks of the subset on `minimax-m3` / `rag:32` at
budget 30, matched task-for-task (`evals/cr_budget30/`, $1.13):

| | budget 12 | budget 30 |
|---|---|---|
| pass | 20.0% | 18.0% |
| successful tool calls, mean | 4.7 | 5.1 |
| **tasks reaching the budget** | **5 / 50** | **0 / 50** |
| total calls incl. failed, mean / max | 11.3 / 47 | 13.6 / 102 |
| E3 / E6 / E7 | 43 / 73 / 19 | 39 / 69 / 24 |

**Negative — the objection does not hold.** Raising the ceiling 2.5× moved pass
rate by −2.0 points (one fail→pass, two pass→fail, i.e. run-to-run noise) because
at budget 30 *no task reached the budget at all*, and at budget 12 only five did.
The agent stops itself at ~5 successful calls out of 30 permitted; it is not being
truncated, so the long-chain gap cannot be attributed to the ceiling.

The same table shows where the effort goes instead: 13.6 attempted calls per task
