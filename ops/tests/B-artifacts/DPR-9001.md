# Operational test: raise default per-thread message cap from 12 to 20 (wording change, step 6 of ops/PROTOCOL.md)

Content-neutral mechanism test under AUTH-001 (per the lead's TASK.md). Not a thesis or policy question. ops/PROTOCOL.md is not edited.

**Proposal.** Replace the default "12 messages per thread" with "20 messages per thread". Proposed wording for step 6, appended: "Default per-thread message cap: 20 (an AUTH may lower it; only the human may raise it)." [S] The number 20 is the value given by the lead for this test; no empirical derivation is claimed.

Note [O]: in the current ops/PROTOCOL.md the cap of 12 appears in the "Bounds" section, not in step 6. Step 6 (Challenge rule) states the 2-round limit. The change would therefore touch either step 6 or the Bounds line; the lead's TASK.md says step 6.

**Requirements it answers (by id).** None. No REQ applies; this is an operational test.

**Evidence it uses and what it does not show.**
- [F] ops/PROTOCOL.md "Bounds": "Three agents; 2 rounds per issue; 12 messages per thread". Shows the current default; not why 12 was chosen.
- [F] ops/PROTOCOL.md step 4: the lead sends each agent one roster message (3 messages). TASK.md states the lead's 3 roster messages count toward the cap.
- [O] Under the cap-12 accounting, 3 messages are consumed by the roster, leaving 9 for agents. A single issue at the 2-round limit takes request, response, rebuttal, response = 4 messages (counting an opening request); with steward evidence replies to two agents and an escalation, one disputed issue plus one fact check can reach or exceed 9. [I] Therefore 12 leaves little room for a second issue, so a thread can hit the cap before the 2-round rule is exhausted.
- [F] ops/PROTOCOL.md "Failure modes": messages being lost or mistaken as completion is a known risk; a hard cap reached mid-dispute would force an early stop. [I] Slack reduces the chance of the cap, not the protocol, ending a dispute.
- Not shown: no measured thread length history exists in ops/tests/ (contains only prompt-A.txt, prompt-A2.txt, prompt-B-lead.txt) [O]; ops/SAFEGUARDS.md and ops/DESIGN-DECISIONS.md are placeholders [O]. So no evidence that 12 was ever too small. [U]

**Alternatives considered and the strongest case for each.**
1. Keep 12. Strongest case: the cap is a budget safeguard; the 2-round rule already bounds disputes; the protocol says an AUTH may lower but only the human may raise bounds, so the lead has no standing to raise it by default; a higher default weakens a safeguard with no observed need.
2. Raise the cap per thread only through the AUTH (no change to default). Strongest case: preserves human control; fits the existing "only the human may raise" rule. This is arguably more consistent with the protocol than my proposal.
3. Cap 20 as default (this proposal).

**Falsifier and assumptions.** Falsified if threads run under the current protocol (with the 2-round rule) complete, including escalation, within 12 messages, i.e. a worked count of the full required sequence (3 roster + request/response x2 rounds + steward verdicts + escalation + final) is at most 12. Assumptions [S]: roster messages count; the steward replies to both peers separately (2 messages); more than one issue per thread is typical. Also falsified by a human statement that bounds are only raised per AUTH.

**Objections received (CH ids) and responses.** CH-9001, round 1 (design-challenger), four objections. Responses:
1. Authority. Conceded as to effect: no agent or the lead can raise a default; this DPR can only be a request to the human, and it has no force unless the human approves. Not conceded that a proposal is itself a raise. Evidence: ops/PROTOCOL.md "Bounds" ("an AUTH may lower but only the human may raise").
2. Falsifier. Conceded. The challenger's worked count (lead roster 3, request 1, verdict 2, request to challenger 1, four dispute messages, escalation 1 = 12) is at most 12, so the falsifier as I wrote it is met. Note the count is exact with zero slack, and in this thread I skipped one request, so it was 11. Remaining disagreement is a judgment, not evidence: whether zero slack is a feature (budget forces escalation) or a defect (any extra lead message exceeds it). Not settled by repository evidence.
3. Provenance. Conceded on both. TASK.md line 7 says only "max messages in the thread = 12"; the roster-counts statement came from the lead's tasking message, not TASK.md. The "known risk" claim about lost messages and the cap is my inference [I], not [F]; PROTOCOL "Failure modes" does not list it.
4. No evidence for 20. Conceded: 20 is the lead's test value [S], and PROTOCOL.md does not define what counts as a message. Defining the counting rule would be a prerequisite to changing the number.

**Not claimed.** That 20 is the right number; that the cap was previously a problem; that the lead or any agent may raise a bound (the protocol says only the human may); that this is approved. Status: proposed. The statement about D13 naming the decision owner is not asserted here; it awaits the evidence-steward's verdict.
