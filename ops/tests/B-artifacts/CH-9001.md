# Title: Raising the default per-thread message cap from 12 to 20 exceeds anyone's standing, is unevidenced, and is contradicted by the proposal's own falsifier

Operational (content-neutral) mechanism test under AUTH-001. Not a thesis or policy question. I argue the cap stays at 12 as instructed; I do not claim 12 is the right number in any absolute sense.

**Objection.**
1. [material] Authority. `ops/PROTOCOL.md` "Bounds": "(defaults; an AUTH may lower but only the human may raise)". Going from 12 to 20 is a raise of a default bound. DPR-9001 admits this (its Alternative 2 and "Not claimed"), yet proposes it. No agent, including the lead, can raise it; a proposal can only be a request to the human.
2. [material] The proposal's own falsifier is met. DPR-9001: falsified if a worked count of the full required sequence is at most 12. Worked count (see Evidence): exactly 12, zero slack, for one disputed issue plus one steward fact check. So the stated falsifier fires. A tight cap is the intended budget safeguard; further issues go to a new thread or to the human.
3. [minor] Provenance mislabelled [F]. (a) "TASK.md states the lead's 3 roster messages count": TASK.md line 7 says only "max messages in the thread = 12". (b) The cited "Failure modes" section lists four failures (unspawned recipient, waiting mistaken for completion, two agents one file, claimed approval); none concerns lost messages or the cap. The "hard cap forces early stop" claim is the proposer's inference, not [F].
4. [minor] No positive evidence for 20: no thread-length history in `ops/tests/` (prompt-A.txt, prompt-A2.txt, prompt-B-lead.txt, raw/), `ops/SAFEGUARDS.md` and `ops/DESIGN-DECISIONS.md` are placeholders (each contains "# placeholder"), 20 is the lead's test value (proposal says [S]). The counting rule (what counts as a message) is undefined in PROTOCOL.md; fixing the number before the rule is premature. Note also that PROTOCOL.md's header cites WD-004 in DESIGN-DECISIONS.md, which is currently a placeholder, so the cited rationale record is absent [O].

**Evidence.** No EXT ids apply (repository operational files only; the steward's EXT-0006 on D13 is unrelated to the cap).
- [F] `ops/PROTOCOL.md` line 29: "Three agents; 2 rounds per issue; 12 messages per thread; no web searches; ..." and line 28: "Bounds (defaults; an AUTH may lower but only the human may raise)".
- [F] `ops/PROTOCOL.md` step 6 (Challenge rule) states the 2-round limit; it does not contain the cap (the cap is in the Bounds section), so the task's "step 6" label is itself inexact [O].
- [F] `ops/threads/T-20261016-90/TASK.md` line 7: "max messages in the thread = 12".
- [F] `ops/AUTHORIZATIONS.md`: AUTH-001 is the only entry; it has no clause on bounds or raising defaults.
- [I] Worked count of the required sequence in this thread: lead roster 3; proposer to steward 1; steward verdict to two agents 2; proposer to challenger 1; challenge, response, rebuttal, response 4; escalate 1; total 12. Assumes roster messages count (only the lead's tasking message says so) and that the steward replies separately to each peer (as it did). If roster messages do not count, 12 leaves 3 spare.
- Unchecked: whether the lead's own SUMMARY or other lead messages count; I did not read lead logs.

**What would resolve it.** (a) A human statement (not in the register today) allowing agent-proposed raises of default bounds; or (b) a worked count of a protocol-required sequence that exceeds 12 and that I missed. Evidence that would favour 20 and that I did not find: measured thread lengths over the cap, or a recorded incident of a dispute cut off by the cap. I looked in `ops/tests/`, `ops/DESIGN-DECISIONS.md`, `ops/SAFEGUARDS.md`, `ops/PROTOCOL.md`, `ops/AUTHORIZATIONS.md`; "not found" applies only to those places.

**What I tried to break and could not.** The proposer's observation that the cap sits in Bounds rather than step 6 is correct. Its arithmetic that 12 leaves little room for a second issue is correct (nine slots after the roster, 12 total by my count for one issue). The proposer flagged its own weaknesses (no history, human-only raise) honestly.
