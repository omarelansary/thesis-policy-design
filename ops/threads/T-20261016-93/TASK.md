## What the proposal must establish, in this order
1. **The decision problem before any solution.** Decision points, the information set at each (what is available by construction; what can legitimately be inferred; what exists only after execution; what is prohibited as a runtime signal), the action set (including graded exposure and "expose everything" and "change nothing"), and the cost structure (who pays for deciding, discovering, using; what is cached; what turns cost). A **signal register**: each signal a policy might use, with class R (available at decision time by construction), D (derived from separate offline data, with its leakage risk), X (post-execution only), P (prohibited: gold tools, reference trajectories, benchmark annotations). Tool count, model-visible tokens, cumulative token consumption and billed cost are distinct quantities; compare objective formulations (for example constrained, scalarised, Pareto reporting) and say what each assumes. Do not choose numbers.
2. **Mechanism families, broadly.** At least: expose all; static curation; one-shot query-conditioned retrieval; model-driven on-demand discovery (search/describe tool, deferred loading, provider-native); hierarchical or graded disclosure; code-mediated access; learned/trained selection; per-turn dynamic registry; session working set. Say which are viable under which regime, which are excluded by scope or evidence, and why. Do not pre-commit to retrieval, staging, graphs, gateways, code mediation, registries or recovery.
3. **Challenge the multi-stage assumption.** At least one simpler policy per family must be written out as a complete comparator, not a straw man. State the conditions under which a stage is justified (what it buys, what it costs, what it can lose) and the conditions under which a single stage or no restriction dominates. The stage count stays unset.
4. **Recovery / later capability disclosure.** Is it necessary? What triggers it (which observable signal, class R/D/X/P)? Who initiates it (model or system)? What does it cost when nothing was missed? What does it do to caching, turns and comparability? What happens when its trigger is wrong?
5. **A decision-flow representation** (graph or equivalent; a text diagram or Mermaid is fine) with the minimum number of nodes the logic needs. Every node states: what determines the decision, what information it needs (signal classes), what it costs, what happens when its assumption fails, and how it would be tested.
6. **Per consequential decision, a decision card** with five separate parts: (E) empirical evidence with EXT/FND ids; (T) theoretical reasoning and its assumptions; (J) engineering judgment; (H) untested hypotheses with falsifiers; (C) counterevidence and plausible failure cases. Empty parts are written as "none found in <where looked>", never omitted.
7. **Validation strategy** with falsifiers (not a plan that can only confirm): baselines, outcomes (success, cumulative tokens by category, billed cost under stated conventions, turns, overhead of the decision mechanism, recovery value and price, cases where nothing was missed), ablations, and what result would make a component not justified. No numbers for margins or sample sizes beyond symbols and the dependencies that fix them; the known vehicle limits (S4G section 2) are stated as threats to validity.

## Lead's points to attack or test (my [I]/[S] seeds: not facts, not conclusions, not requirements)
- Fewer exposed tools does not imply fewer cumulative tokens or lower billed cost: cached static prefixes (see claim-ledger rows C3-09, C3-24, C3-27, C3-03b), extra turns for discovery (C3-14), tool-list changes that may invalidate caches (C3-03b, K3), selector tokens. Is a regime gate ("does restriction pay at all here?") a real decision, and what information fixes it?
- A cascade of filters is only worth its stages if the decider's cost scales with the candidates it reads and recall losses compound; if that is wrong a single stage dominates. Is that argument supported or only plausible?
- Every removal is irrecoverable without a re-add path; every re-add path costs something even when unused. Is the trade necessary, and is it a model-initiated or system-initiated act? What does the evidence say about models failing to initiate search at all (S4I section 5, MCP-Atlas v3 note)?
- Legitimacy of signals: the user request, catalogue metadata, trajectory so far, tool errors and the model's own calls are runtime-observable; "this tool turned out relevant" is not, until it is called. A stage-five "I found this tool relevant" (REQ-O14-15) needs a named observable behind it.
- Possible prior-art overlap: vendor deferred tool search and its cache-preserving append design (C3-27), Executor, FitText, ComplexMCP's iterative retrieval, Skill Blocks. Do not assert novelty; say what remains uncovered, with the bounded-negative limit of every "not found".
