**N-6/SKILLMEM (what bears on it; largest library): PARTLY.** [F] The texts bear on it as follows. (a) Mechanism and motivation: CodeMem pairs search/load of tools with a saved-skill library and states the cross-session claim without measuring it (`:196-199`, `:229-232`). (b) Cost side of persisted memory: Skill-Pro stored tokens 816 vs up to 391706 (`:773,793`); SkillLens pool growth raised retrieval time 3.21× and agent tokens 22.7% (`:1262-1266`); MemSkill first-turn tokens 1,313 vs 4,949 at k=5 and cost 1.302× higher for the retrieval arm (`:446-447`, `:405-432`). (c) Survey framing: skills as amortized trade-off, "selective" access (`:1351-1352`, `:1333`). [F] Largest tool/skill library in any experiment: **not stated numerically in any of the five texts as a tool/skill count**. Skill-Pro and SkillLens give no count of units in the lines read (SkillLens: "initialized with antigravity-awesome-skills", `:1537-1538`); CodeMem gives tasks (25) and tools per task (2-8, `:488-489`), not registry size; MemSkill has 6 skills (`:440`). [O] Hence none of the five reports an experiment with a large (hundreds to thousands of tools) MCP-style catalogue. [U] I did not read the whole of SkillLens appendix (e.g. beyond `:1589`), the whole of Skill-Pro (`:120-636`, `:914-1030`), or the whole of MemSkill (`:120-340`, `:465-835`, `:866-1190`), so a count stated there is possible; greps for `\d+ skills/tools/nodes/APIs` in the three papers found no match in text read or searched.
