**Objections.**
1. **"Impossible" at 10,000 tools (section 1).** The range 2.5-10 million tokens per call uses 250 tokens per tool (DMB dev document, EXT-0084 lines 51-55: 292k for 1,168 tools) and about 1,000 (vendor, EXT-0023 lines 96-98). The stored ComplexMCP passage gives about 100 per tool or less ("over 300 tools (approximately 30,000 tokens)", EXT-0075 lines 32-34), which gives about 1 million at 10,000 tools, equal to the 1M-token windows the vehicle itself uses for two of four models (EXT-0084 lines 54-55). The three sources differ by ten times and the brief reports the upper two; "above any current window" and "impossible" are not established. Also "placeholder schemas understating it" (section 1; 02 section 8 "stub schemas understating it") is stated as fact; the digests as quoted in SUM-0004 (second-hand, which I did not re-read) say the rendered size is "unverified" and the figure "not clean", and the direction is not established. The Speakeasy blog also lists 1,000+ tools as untested.
2. **Speakeasy cell (section 1 bullet 2).** "In the one vendor cell with 40 tools, a simple task cost more with progressive disclosure ($0.072) than with all tools loaded ($0.045) (vendor, one run of unstated size)". (a) The same cell has semantic search also costlier ($0.050); static is cheaper in 2 of the 8 dynamic-versus-static dollar comparisons and in neither of the other two sizes (EXT-0023 lines 83-99). At 40 tools the complex task favours progressive by 3 times ($0.051 versus $0.146) and at 100 tools the simple task by about 5 times; (b) "one run" contradicts the vendor ("a handful of test runs", line 153); (c) the sentence "At small catalogues the answer may well be 'show everything'" rests on that one comparison.
3. **Toollery row.** Only the natural-replay regime (hit rate 0.989 for the full library) is reported. The same table gives the cache-suppressed regime: $0.037879 versus $0.000951, and the text "With caching suppressed, Toollery reduces billed input cost by 97.5%" (EXT-0061 lines 33-34; EXT-0062 lines 28-29). The conclusion drawn by the authors ("depend on cache state and traffic reuse", line 37-39) is the accurate one; the brief's "did not give a clear billed saving" is the natural-regime reading only.
4. **ToolMenuBench row.** The same table (EXT-0028 lines 42-48) has CMTF at 85.7% success with 1,125 tokens against 32.1% and 56,062 for all tools. The brief cites the table for "below full exposure" only. CMTF's visibility is "determined by the next causally necessary action" with "gold trajectories fixed" (lines 33-34, 29-30), which suggests gold-path information (class P) and so a non-legitimate policy; I could not verify how CMTF obtains the causal path. Omission of the row, without that remark, is one-sided.
5. **ComplexMCP row.** "Best retrieval variant" is the Iterative RAG arm, in which the LLM is given a `retrieve tools` tool (EXT-0075 lines 48-54): that is a search-only discovery arm, the closest stored analogue of vendor deferral. It lost 16-18 points to full context at about 300 tools (EXT-0026 lines 103, 112 and the Gemini-3-flash pair, whose assignment of 55.31 is by token-column match in SUM-0004, not by table layout). The brief does not say so, and its rival row calls vendor deferral "the strongest simple rival". The benchmark has 47 scenarios, three runs (EXT-0075 lines 62-64), and the +-values are run-to-run standard deviations, not intervals over tasks. The brief applies an interval standard to DMB and none here.
6. **Reachability row.** "Fully reachable tasks: 36.7% (k=4) to 75.3% (k=32)" is the *share of tasks* that are fully reachable (EXT-0053 lines 79-80), but the sentence sits after pass^3 figures and reads like a pass rate. The pass^3 = 0.0% statement is for rag:8 (line 69-73: "rag:8 was additionally run to pass^3").
7. **The 8-point difference.** The bold "Expose-all ... cost 8 points against the curated pool" is followed in the same row by "a ±8 interval per cell cannot resolve differences below roughly 10 points". Both cannot stand: either the 8 points is a finding or it is not distinguishable from zero. The unpaired figure (03: 9.8-11.3) ignores that all cells share the same 150 tasks; a paired interval would be narrower, and the paper reports paired deficits agreeing within 2.7 points for rag:8 (EXT-0053 lines 91-96). Neither paired nor unpaired interval is reported for flat.
8. **Two-stage router.** "A two-stage router and single-stage retrieval were both within noise" (section 4 last row). In Table 7 `hier` is below `rag:32` in all four models (28.0/32.7/32.7/36.0 versus 28.7/34.7/36.7/40.0, mean -2.7), and which `k` is "single-stage" is not stated (`hier` is above rag:8, mixed against rag:16). That is the inference the brief elsewhere forbids (non-significance read as match).
9. **OpenAI.** The same page says mixing eager and deferred functions in an Agents API session is "generally not recommended" (EXT-0012 line 38), which bears on a "core plus search" rival (brief section 5), and is omitted.

**What would resolve it.** Report the three per-tool figures and soften "impossible" to a range with its sources; show the cells the table contains (Speakeasy, Toollery, ToolMenuBench) or say that rows were chosen; name Iterative RAG as the search arm; relabel the reachability sentence; make the 8-point statement consistent; replace "within noise" by the numbers.
