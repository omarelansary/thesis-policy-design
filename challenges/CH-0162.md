# Title: The floor/ceiling requirement is applied unevenly, MCP-Atlas is called "mid-range" though the cited page shows a compressed top band, and contamination is absent from sections 7-10

**Objection.**
1. *Uneven application.* s4 revision 2 makes floor/ceiling a hard requirement "checked on a baseline-arm pilot ... reported scores of other model generations do not count". In s7, AppWorld (no score of any route-generation model on any stored page) is rank 1 for P2 and E2E with the requirement unevaluated, while MCPMark is rejected for E2E on a reported floor ("floor on gpt-oss-120b", L214) and Toolathlon-based options are listed against a reported 13-20%. gpt-oss-120b is on the route, so that rejection may stand, but the standard is "reported score" for the rejected and "assumed passable until the pilot" for the preferred. The same rule must be used or the s7 ranks must be labelled conditional on the pilot for every candidate.
2. *MCP-Atlas is not "mid-range".* s7 E2E #2 says "current open models are mid-range, no floor problem"; P3 #2 says "No floor problem for current open models". The v3 page the lead checked says: the open GLM-5.1 reaches 75.6% and is "statistically tied with GPT-5.5"; "the frontier is compressed: the top three models span 78.2-82.2% pass rate, and cross-judge ranges are only 2.1-4.6 points, so small swaps near the top should be interpreted as evaluator uncertainty rather than stable capability gaps" (`v5-bm-i13-arxiv-html-2602.00933v3.txt` L298, L735). That is a ceiling/compression warning from the authors about exactly the kind of small difference V5 must resolve. The correction to CH-0139 replaced a floor problem by the claim "no floor problem" and did not look at the ceiling that CH-0140 named in the same breath. The keyless subset may be easier or harder than the full set [U]; the register has not measured it.
3. *Contamination.* s2.2 lists contamination risk as a scored property and s1.4 names it as an overlooked point, yet s7-s10 contain the word nowhere (checked by search). AppWorld has been public since 2024, its tasks and docs are public, train/dev solutions are released (README L355), and its test ground truth is in encrypted bundles asked not to be posted; MCP-Atlas publishes 500 of 1,000 tasks and v3 shows public-versus-private gaps for open models (Kimi K2.5 is "the only model where private performance exceeds public", L731); the lead did not check the +3.8 point figure it cites from CH-0139. P3 uses train/dev reference solutions as gold, which is also the part most exposed. Contamination matters little for P5 billing but is relevant for P2, P3, E2E accuracy.

**Evidence.** [F] register L95, L107, L189, L212, L214; `v5-bm-i13` L298, L731, L735; `v5-bm-b14` L355. [O] search of s7-s10 for "ontaminat" found no match (lines 87, 91, 144 are s4-s5).

**What would resolve it.** One rule for floor/ceiling across all candidates (conditional on pilot, or by the same kind of evidence); replace "mid-range" by the authors' compression statement and say what it means for a 2-5 point effect; add a contamination line to each ranked candidate (even "not assessed") and to s10.
