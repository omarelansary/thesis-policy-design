# Title: The ranking still leans on "deterministic, no judge" after the lead accepted that deterministic scorers are not shown to be more reliable

**Objection.** CH-0141 was accepted and R4 now says there is "no preference by scorer type" and that BFCL state checks (20% misaligned) and tau2 (9.8%) are no better than judge-scored benchmarks. DPR-0010 s0, the basis of s7, states the reason AppWorld heads the list as "offline, deterministic, has no judge, no simulator, no keys", and s7 repeats "unit tests, no judge" (L200, L211) and "deterministic call match" for P3. Some of those properties are real practical advantages: a judge must be served by the route's open-weight models or by a named outside model; the per-arm scorer check (R4) is cheaper for a unit-test scorer than for a judge. But that is route-servability and cost, not reliability. The AppWorld evaluator has no audit, which is different from reliable (CH-0159 on the issue list). The register should give the practical reasons by name and not let the word "deterministic" carry an implied reliability advantage that R4 withdrew.

**Evidence.** [F] register L67, L188, L200, L211, L230; DPR-0010 s0. [I] the reading of what carries the ranking.

**What would resolve it.** In s7, replace "deterministic" as a ranking reason by "no judge to serve or validate" and "scorer check per arm is cheaper", and keep "reliability unknown" beside AppWorld's scorer.
