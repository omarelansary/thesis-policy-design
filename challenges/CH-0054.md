# Title: the E1 restatement answers a different question (expose-all's share beyond the window) and cites a vendor cell that says the opposite of "small share"

**Grade: SERIOUS for (1) and (2); MINOR for (3).** (02 section 8; brief section 6 item 7; 03 section 1 row 4.)

**Objection.**
1. **Conflation of two shares.** 02 section 8 says "For catalogues beyond the window the share is not small by construction: ten thousand definitions at about 250 tokens ... about 2.5 million tokens per call". That is the share of the tool block in *expose-all*, a configuration that by the lead's own argument cannot run there, so E1 cannot measure it. What E1 can measure beyond the window is the tool-definition share in *restricted* configurations (prefix, scope statement, appended definitions, discovery results). Whether that share is small is exactly what is open. If it is small at the sizes that matter (the brief's section 7 "what would count against" lists "the tool block being a negligible share of tokens at every size that matters"), then among feasible restricted arms the token criterion barely separates them, and the token-minimisation question is moot even though expose-all is infeasible; what remains is the feasibility (reachability) question, not the token policy question. So v3's "could end the line" is not as wrong as 03 row 4 says: it can end the *token-objective* line beyond the window, not the problem of staying feasible. 03 section 1 row 4 ("HOLDS IN PART") draws the line at "the research problem", and 02 section 8 asserts that E1 "cannot end" it.
2. **The vendor cell does not support "small share".** 02 section 8 says the 40-tool cell where progressive cost more "is consistent with" a small tool-block share (EXT-0023). The same table lists the static configuration's initial tokens at 43,300 against 1,000 "Simple Task Tokens" (EXT-0023 lines 83-85); whichever way those columns combine, the tool block is the dominant token term in that cell, yet static was cheaper in dollars ($0.045 versus $0.072). The cell is consistent with a billing effect (cached prefix), the Toollery pattern (EXT-0061/0062), and says nothing about a small token share. [I] The cache attribution is mine; the vendor does not say.
3. **E1's information at the sizes where it can run.** E1 measures expose-all only where it fits (small `n`, the regime where the share may indeed be large or small), and restricted arms at every `n`. Its information about the motivating regime comes only through the restricted arms. 02 section 8 says "E1 is the cheapest first measurement and has high information value"; it needs agent runs (not authorised), and no cost is given. The claim "cheapest" is unsupported by any stored cost figure.
4. **What E1 is still good for (I tried to break this and could not):** it localises where overhead (discovery calls, prefix re-reads, selector tokens) rather than tool definitions dominates, and it feeds the break-even accounting. I agree with the lead's "absolute saving versus overhead" quantity.

**Evidence.** 02 lines 107-113; EXT-0023 lines 83-85, 101; EXT-0061 lines 25-39 and EXT-0062 lines 25-32 (natural replay hit rate 0.989 for the full library); brief section 7 last paragraph.

**What would resolve it.** Say which configuration's share E1 measures beyond the window; restate the outcome set as "token policy question is moot / live, feasibility question unaffected"; remove or correct the EXT-0023 citation in section 8.

**Response (lead).** ACCEPT in part: E1 can end the token-saving line, not the feasibility line; "impossible" softened to "infeasible in practice" with a 1-10 million range; the vendor cell is no longer cited as "small share". [rounds used: 1 of 2]

That closes items 1 and 2; item 3 ("cheapest" unsupported by a stored cost) is not repeated, but "cheapest first measurement" remains without a cost figure (minor).
