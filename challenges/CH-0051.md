# Title: R-pool(m) is not "the same construction" as DynamicMCPBench's curated pool, "demanding, not flattering" is asserted and rests on a difference the brief itself calls unresolvable

**Grade: SERIOUS.** (02 section 4 table row R-pool and consequence (ii); 03 section 1 row 5; `design/package/A-problem-and-objective.md` line 60.)

**Objection.**
1. **False identification.** 02 says R-pool(m) (tools the task needs plus randomly drawn distractors) is "the same construction as the 'curated pool' of DynamicMCPBench (EXT-0053)". The source says otherwise: each candidate is offered "the tools it needs plus a fixed set of distractor tools, half of them same-name tools on other servers" (EXT-0079 lines 26-28) and "plus eight distractors, half of them same-name tools on other servers (the rest near-misses)" (EXT-0081 lines 22-23). The vehicle's pool therefore has structured, adversarial distractors, not random ones. Random distractors make a pool easier; the DMB numbers (curated about 8 tools) cannot be used as the value of R-pool(m). The paper also says its Appendix S varies the pool's composition (EXT-0081 lines 30-32), which I could not read.
2. **Not well defined.** For R-pool(m): "the tools it needs" (the reference route only? equivalence-set members? "latent" prerequisite tools that ComplexMCP says retrieval misses, EXT-0026 lines 68-73?), the distractor distribution (uniform over the catalogue? near-misses?), the seed and number of draws (the reference's success then varies by draw; a paired comparison against a random reference adds reference variance), and `m` (a free choice, the text admits). None is specified.
3. **Which statement is right: "demanding, not flattering" (v4) or "weaker and favours restriction" (package A line 60)?** Neither is derived in the text. My reading [I]: as a *success bar*, R-pool(m) is at least as hard as expose-all whenever distraction costs success (on the one stored cell: curated 44.0 against flat 36.0 on minimax, EXT-0053 lines 58-65), so noninferiority to it is harder than to expose-all; v4 is right in that sense, on that vehicle. As a *licence*, it is weaker: passing it says nothing about "no loss relative to showing everything", and the direction can reverse where the pool excludes routes expose-all can use (live mode, alternative equivalent routes; in the replay vehicle novel routes score as failing anyway, EXT-0079 lines 55-59, which is why the sign holds there). So package A is wrong that it "favours restriction" on this vehicle's success side, and v4 is wrong to state "not flattering" as general. I could not find a mechanism by which a gold pool is more lenient on the cost side.
4. **The only support is a number the brief calls unresolvable.** The 8-point gap 44.0 versus 36.0 is one model, one draw, per-cell interval "roughly +-8 points" (EXT-0053 lines 68-71); brief section 4 row 3 says an interval of that size "cannot resolve differences below roughly 10 points". By the brief's own standard the support for "demanding" is not distinguishable from zero. (The paper's own prose treats the 8 points as a real "distraction" cost, line 89-91; the cells share the same 150 tasks so paired intervals would be narrower than the unpaired ones the brief computes. Neither is reported; see CH-0055.)
5. **No calibration.** At `n <= n_fit` both R-full(n) and R-pool(m) exist; the protocol never compares them to check that R-pool(m) is a usable proxy for the reference it replaces. That comparison is the only evidence available for the direction and size of the difference.

**Evidence.** EXT-0079 lines 22-29, 55-59; EXT-0081 lines 21-33; EXT-0053 lines 56-71, 83-93; EXT-0026 lines 64-73; package A line 60. Not read: DMB Appendix S, section 3.4.

**What would resolve it.** Delete "same construction" or restate it as "analogous to"; give the R-pool definition (needed set, distractor distribution, seed, `m`); say in which sense it is demanding and in which it licenses less; add the R-pool(m) versus R-full(n) comparison at sizes that fit to the protocol.

**Response (lead).** ACCEPT: not claimed to be DMB's construction (EXT-0079, 0081 cited); distractor rule, seed and `m` flagged unset; direction declared unresolved with both readings quoted; calibration of R-pool against R-full required wherever both exist. [rounds used: 1 of 2]

**Round 2 (challenger, 2026-10-19): CLOSED, minor residual.** 02 section 4 is honest about direction: it states the proviso (R-pool success at least expose-all's), the single cell that supports it with its interval, and package A's opposite reading, and says neither is derived. The false identification is gone. The calibration step is the right resolution for sizes up to `n_fit`. Residual R5 (minor): (a) no acceptance criterion says what calibration difference permits using R-pool above `n_fit`; (b) calibration at `n <= n_fit` cannot show the relation at `n > n_fit` (distraction grows with n), so above `n_fit` the use of R-pool is still an extrapolation, which section 4 should say in the calibration paragraph as it does in the next one.

**Outcome.** resolved (conceded) with minor residual R5.
