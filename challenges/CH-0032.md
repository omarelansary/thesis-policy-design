# Title: the justification of the primary objective covers F1 over F2/F3 but not billed cost over exposure, and the choice largely fixes the headline

**Grade: SERIOUS, expected PARTIAL (section 6 item 6 already reports both readings; the residual is the missing justification and consequence).**

**Objection.** DPR-0004 6.1 reads the AUTH-004 sentence as treating "reducing ... exposure" and "accounting for the costs" as "the thing minimised" and then makes the criterion population *cost*, with billed cost a named convention. The sentence's object is *unnecessary tool exposure*; costs of deciding, discovering and using are to be *accounted for*. Reading it as minimised billed cost is one admissible reading, not the only one. The choice is not neutral: with the Table 15 provider's implied `r ~ 0.0088` the entire warm 87,074-token library costs about 770 uncached-input-token equivalents per request (87,074 x 0.0088), so a billed-cost criterion on that provider can show at most that much tool-block saving per request; with the EXT-0021 multipliers (0.1, 0.05, 0.025) the same library is 8.7k, 4.4k, 2.2k equivalents. A billed-cost primary on a low-`r` provider almost pre-decides "restriction does not pay"; an exposure-count primary pre-decides the opposite. The proposal justifies F1 against F2/F3 (needs `delta`, not `B` or `lambda`) but not the choice of quantity.

**Evidence.** DPR-0004 6.1, 6.5, 6.6; AUTH-004 objective sentence (quoted in DPR-0004 header); EXT-0062 (arithmetic above, derived [I]); EXT-0021 multipliers.

**What would resolve it.** Add to 6.1: why the minimised quantity is cost rather than exposure (or why both under a stated rule), the consequence of each choice at low and high `r` (for example the maximum tool-block saving, see CH-0033), and list "which quantity is minimised" explicitly beside Q1 as a human decision whose answer changes the sign of the headline in some regimes.
