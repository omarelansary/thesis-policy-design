# Title: the regime gate D3 has no registered determinant; as specified it is a configuration choice, not a runtime decision

**Objection.** DPR-0001 admits D3 is "a real decision only if its cost and determinants can be stated" and that this "is neither established" (sections 2, 7). That is honest but then D3 stays in the decision-point list and in the policy space as if it were a node. The quantities that decide whether restriction pays are, in the governing record, not catalogue size alone: (i) load probability and reloads (Skill Blocks' net saving formula), (ii) session length and repetition (compgateway saved 392,000 and 1,220,000 tokens over two sessions but 128-256 for a 9-tool agent), (iii) the share of cost carried by the cached static prefix (one corpus: about 75% of reconstructed cost, mostly cache traffic, prefix not separable into prompt and tools), (iv) the achievable cache hit rate (84.6 to 99.5% on self-hosted vLLM falling to 16 to 26% under concurrency; vLLM eviction is not provider caching). Of these, none is in S1-S15 as a class R signal at D3: expected session length and load probability are future quantities (class X or unknown), and S9 (cache fields) exists only after the first call. The register's D3 inputs (S2-S4, S15) reduce the gate to a rule on `n`, `L(t)` and provider capability, i.e. a threshold on catalogue size. Vendor thresholds of that kind exist (Anthropic at least 10 tools or over 10K tokens, a blog at 20+ tools and over 5K, MCP guide 1-5% of context, Claude Code 10%) and the ledger records that "no derivation [is] published" (K9). Importing any such cut would be the "fixed cut-off" that REQ-O14-08 says is not a policy, and would be an arbitrary threshold (AUTH-004).

Two readings follow; the DPR should choose between them or say it cannot: (A) D3 is made once by the operator offline from regime facts (an evaluation stratification variable, not a policy node), or (B) D3 is a runtime decision using inferred session length and cache state, which needs those inferences' signal classes and their error costs. Under (A) a node labelled "regime gate" in the decision flow would misrepresent what the policy decides; under (B) the register is incomplete.

**What would resolve it.** The DPR states which reading (A or B) it holds, and for (B) adds the inferred-quantity signals with class and error cost. Experiment only needed to locate the gate, not to answer whether D3 is a decision.
