# Title: Figure 2 routes Regime B into a decision rule that presupposes a reference; the frontier claim at n > n_fit licenses no selection, and the extrapolation caveat does not bound the extrapolation

**Grade: SERIOUS.** (02 sections 3 and 4; brief sections 2, 6 item 3, 8 question 4.)

**Objection.**
1. **Internal inconsistency.** In Figure 2, Regime B ("no expose-all run exists... claim: frontier and gap to an oracle pool") flows through Q2, S3, S4 to S5, "Decide by the rule in section 6; feasible = noninferiority shown". In Regime B noninferiority to what? To R-pool(m) with a margin, which section 4(iv) says is still undecided; or to nothing, in which case F is empty or undefined and S5 returns no choice. The figure draws a decision where the text says there is none. A frontier is a Pareto set, which package A (F4, line 55) already recorded "yields a frontier, not 'a policy' (REQ-O14-08 asks for one)". The supervisor's motivating regime is 10,000 tools, i.e. Regime B. The brief says this openly (section 6 item 3, question 4); the figure does not.
2. **n_fit is small relative to the motivating regime.** n_fit is "roughly window / mean definition length". With the stored per-tool figures (about 100 tokens in ComplexMCP, EXT-0075 lines 32-34: "over 300 tools (approximately 30,000 tokens)"; 250 in the DMB dev document, EXT-0084 lines 51-55; 1,000-1,300 in the vendor blog, EXT-0023 lines 83-98) and a 200k window, n_fit is about 150-2,000 tools; with a 1M window up to 10,000 at the ComplexMCP figure. For the common case in the brief (200k windows, 250-1,000 tokens per tool) n_fit is a few hundred; the claim for 10,000 tools extrapolates by one to two orders of magnitude.
3. **The caveat is a label, not a bound.** Item (iii) says extrapolation is "supported only by how the arm-versus-reference gap behaves as `n` rises toward `n_fit`". Nothing in the protocol says what behaviour would support it, what would refute it, or why retrieval recall, near-duplicate density and hierarchy depth, which change with catalogue size beyond what a few hundred tools exhibit, would follow the same trend. Subsampling a large catalogue to nested sizes also changes the distractor mix. No extrapolation test is stated.
4. **Regime A's reference degrades.** "Fits the window" is not "works well": ComplexMCP says GPT-4o and DeepSeek-V3 decline beyond about 300 candidates (EXT-0026 lines 69-73) and DMB's flat cell lost 8 points (EXT-0053). Noninferiority to a degraded R-full(n) gets easier as n approaches n_fit, so the claim "feasible in Regime A" weakens exactly where it matters; the ladder measures the degradation curve but Figure 2 uses R-full only. (Whether to add R-pool(m) as a second reference in Regime A is a design decision, not mine.)

**Evidence.** 02 lines 44-63, 65-74; package A line 55; EXT-0075 lines 32-34; EXT-0084 lines 51-55; EXT-0023 lines 83-98; EXT-0026 lines 69-73.

**What would resolve it.** Redraw Regime B to end at "frontier report; selection requires a human-set margin against R-pool(m)"; state numerically (with sources and ranges) what `n_fit` is for each model on the closest vehicle and in the motivating example; state what trend of the gap would count for or against extrapolation, or state that no extrapolation claim is made.

**Response (lead).** ACCEPT: Figure 2 split into S5A (Regime A rule) and S5B (frontier, gap, trend; a policy only with a human margin or token budget); `n_fit` about 200-4,000; extrapolation supported/undermined by the trend of the arm-versus-R-full gap. [rounds used: 1 of 2]

**Round 2 (challenger, 2026-10-19): CLOSED for the objection; new residuals R4 and R6.** The inconsistency (a feasibility rule applied where no reference exists) is removed in text and figure, and the extrapolation now has a stated supporting and undermining pattern. Residual R4 (introduced by the correction, should-fix): the n_fit range "roughly 200 to 4,000" uses the 250 and 1,000 tokens-per-tool figures only, but 02 section 8 and the brief section 1 also use ComplexMCP's about 100 per tool (EXT-0075), under which a 1M window holds about 10,000 tools. Then "10,000 tools is at least 2.5 times n_fit on these figures" is true only on the two figures and the brief's section 6 item 3 range omits the third; the headline example sits on the boundary of Regime A and B depending on the per-tool figure. State n_fit with all three figures and say the 10,000 example is at the limit under the lowest. Residual R6 (minor): in Figure 2, S4 has unconditional edges to both S5A and S5B (the regime branch is drawn before Q2, not at S5), and S6 follows S5B although S5B may choose nothing; "gap stays roughly constant" is undefined; "the observed trend would continue" is itself a claim above n_fit.
