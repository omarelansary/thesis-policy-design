# Title: the added navigated-hierarchy arm is a legitimate dropped alternative, but it is mis-costed ("one call per level") and its stated motive is the one AUTH-006 warned against

**Grade: SERIOUS for the description; the inclusion itself I could not break.**

**Objection.**
1. **Not an invented stage.** P4g exists in the project's own earlier packages (DPR-0002 F4; `design/package/C-policy-proposal.md` line 55 "N1 + group headers + N3 (P4g...)"; DPR-0003 lines 54, 61, 89, 99 list it as a comparator). v3's arm list omitted it. A mechanism family dropped between packages is a legitimate restoration. I do not claim it is an invented stage.
2. **Motive.** 03 section 2 item 3 and 02 section 5 justify the arm as "the most direct realisation of 'a question at each stage' in which the model, not a system selector, answers each stage's question". AUTH-006 (verbatim, line 373): "Do not invent stages, thresholds, empirical benefits, or novelty merely to resemble the supervisor's example." The restoration is defensible on the first reason (a dropped alternative) and not on the second (resembling the supervisor's picture). The brief row (section 5) repeats the second reason. Also, "ask yourself one question" (E7) does not say who answers; the lead's reading that the model answers is one of at least two (REQ-O14-12 records "whom or what answers each question ... is not stated").
3. **Mis-costing.** 02 section 5: "It costs one extra model call per level, so its token cost is exactly the kind of overhead the criterion counts". Earlier project records state a second, per-turn cost: DPR-0003 line 63: "The list is a per-turn cost growing with `n` (P4) or with the number of groups (P4g)". In EXT-0023 lines 56-58 the tool description of `list_tools` "includes the structure of available sources and tags", i.e. a hierarchy summary sits in the prefix and is re-read at every call; the vendor's "about 2,500 initial tokens regardless of toolset size" is measured at 40-400 tools only, and 1,000+ tools is listed by the vendor as untested (EXT-0023 lines 159-161, "What's next"). At 10,000 tools the top level of the hierarchy is not shown to be small. The brief's premise "a small stable prefix" (sentence 1) is therefore an extrapolation from <= 400 tools that is not labelled.
4. **Budget semantics.** B counts "discoveries"; a navigation to one tool takes d levels plus a describe, so equal `B_s` between ranked search and navigation is not equal opportunity; matching on tokens or on calls must be stated. Where the hierarchy comes from (catalogue metadata, servers, a clustering built on development data = class D with leakage rules) is not stated; DMB's `hier` router uses servers (EXT-0053 lines 40-41), a property of the vehicle's catalogue.
5. **Evidence label.** The brief says "a vendor example exists (progressive search, EXT-0023)". The vendor's own caveat (EXT-0023 line 153: "preliminary results from a handful of test runs") is not carried.

**Evidence.** DPR-0003 lines 54, 61, 63; AUTH-006 line 373; EXT-0023 lines 54-62, 153, 159-161; REGISTER REQ-O14-12; EXT-0053 lines 40-41.

**What would resolve it.** Justify the arm as a restored alternative only; replace "one call per level" by the two cost terms (calls per level and the prefix/list tokens as a function of the number of groups); label the "small prefix" as untested above 400 tools; state hierarchy provenance and the budget unit.

**Response (lead).** ACCEPT: restoration of a dropped alternative; listing tokens added to the cost; hierarchy source and budget unit flagged unset; "replaces ranked search"; "measured to 400 tools only". [rounds used: 1 of 2]

**Round 2 (challenger, 2026-10-19): CLOSED.** 02 section 5 and brief section 5 now state the two cost terms and the 400-tool limit. Minor wording note only: brief section 2 sentence 1 still says "small stable prefix" without the 400-tool label (the label is in the brief's section 5 table and in 02).

**Outcome.** resolved (conceded).
