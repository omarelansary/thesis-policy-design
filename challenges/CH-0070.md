# Title: Tool-Overuse "about 20% and 37.5% spurious" is the authors' reading of group means with unusual group membership; the three studies are not comparable arm to arm

**Grade: SERIOUS (comparability) / MINOR (arithmetic is right).**

**Objection.**
1. Arithmetic and wording are the authors': 80.2 and 62.5 are in the Figure 2 caption (arxiv-2604.19749.txt:428-435) and the text says "a 19.8% tendency to spuriously invoke tools" and "error rates rising to 37.5%" (:464-467). So 100 minus irrelevance detection is the authors' own equation; I could not verify from the stored text how a response is scored beyond "a correct response should either explain the tool's irrelevance or provide a standard text response without a function call" (:461-463), i.e. a failure is any function call [F], but malformed output is not excluded in the text read [U].
2. **Group membership (computed [I] from :408-427):** the mean of Haiku-4.5 85.11, GLM-4.6 84.96, GPT-5.2 79.42, Gemini3-Pro 77.85 and DeepSeek-R1 73.55 is 80.18, so "Frontier" = those five, with DeepSeek-R1 inside; the mean of Qwen3-8B 79.07, Qwen2.5-32B 73.75, Qwen2.5-7B 65.16, Llama3.2-3B 52.06, Llama3.1-8B 42.70 is 62.55, so "open-source" = five models of 3B-32B. The figure is not "frontier vs open models" in general. Per model the spurious share ranges 14.9% (Haiku) to 57.3% (Llama3.1-8B). A mean hides a 4-fold spread.
3. **Different constructs, same models, different numbers [I, from the two papers]:** Qwen2.5-7B-inst: NTA 34.8, DT 54.7, irrelevance-spurious 34.8. Llama3.1-8B-inst: 62.5 / 99.7 / 57.3. DeepSeek-R1: 17.6 / 42.6 / 26.5. Qwen3-8B (think off): 4.1 / 36.2 / 20.9 (the other paper's Qwen3-8B mode is not stated in the text read). Magnitudes differ by up to 2x between constructs for one model; in two cases the DT and irrelevance rates are far apart (99.7 vs 57.3). `05` section 4 treats "about 20-37%" and "1-96%" as two prior ranges for two arms: they are not on one scale, and the irrelevance data come from the BFCL irrelevance set (:459-460), which is not designed so that the task needs the missing tool.
4. The paper's own limitation says its experiments "focus exclusively on code-as-tool scenarios (Python interpreters) for mathematical reasoning" (:1306); `05` repeats it for the irrelevance result, but 3.2 follows BFCL (:459). Which statement applies to the irrelevance experiment is [U].
