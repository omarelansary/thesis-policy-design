# Title: R4 states a preference for deterministic scorers that none of its cited sources states and that the register's own s6 data does not support

**Objection.** The R4 basis cites the Agentic Benchmark Checklist (ABC), MT-Bench and Feuer et al. The ABC text, as stored, treats whole-string matching, substring matching, LLM-as-a-judge and state matching as parallel scheme types, each with its own pitfalls, and recommends a pilot of the judge's accuracy and self-consistency (O.c.1). It does not say deterministic checks should be preferred (`m03` L456-487). MT-Bench and Feuer et al. concern judge bias and schema adherence; they are evidence that judges need validation, not that state checks are better. The SUM-0065 M11 verdict is "PARTLY (the checklist recommends, it does not require)" but that is about the strength of the pilot recommendation, not about the missing preference.
The register's own s6 rows point the other way or are neutral: BFCL v4 (state plus response checks, deterministic) 40 of 200 audited tasks (20.0%) disagree with expert review, and Epoch finds defects in 24 of 50 sampled items; tau2 Retail (database state plus an NL judge) 11 of 112 (9.8%); MCP-Atlas (LLM claim judge) 12 of 89 (13.5%); LiveMCPBench (LLM judge) 29 of 95 (30.5%, SUM-0077 E4, not shown in the s6 row); MCPMark (programmatic verifiers) 12 of 28 Postgres tasks pass without the work; Toolathlon (state scripts) has a report that models downloaded the ground truth. These are different audits with different protocols, so I do not claim they rank scorer types. The point is that no stored source shows deterministic checks to be the more reliable class, and ABC lists their failure modes (O.b.2, O.b.3, tau-bench's empty-response success).
A rule worded as a preference will move rankings (it favours ATRBench, Toolathlon, MCPMark, AppWorld and disfavours MCP-Atlas, LiveMCPBench, VitaBench) without a basis.

**Evidence.** `m03` L456-487; SUM-0065 M10, M11; SUM-0066 M27, M28; SUM-0077 E2, E3, E4, E5; SUM-0068 A22; SUM-0078 E23, E18; `17` s3 R4 and s6.
[F] the quotes. [I] my reading of "no stored source states the preference". [U] I read ABC only around L440-490 and by grep for "prefer" and "LLM-as-a-judge"; I did not read the whole checklist.

**What would resolve it.** Reword R4 to what the sources support: a scorer of either kind is validated on a pilot of the subset actually used, its error rate on that subset is reported, and a judge, if used, is checked for position, verbosity and self-enhancement bias (see CH-0156). If the lead keeps a preference for deterministic checks, label it the lead's reasoning (CC1) with the reason (no stochastic judge variance, no judge cost on the route), not as a source-based rule.
