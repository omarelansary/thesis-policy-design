# Title: The P3 settlement for AppWorld rests on a requirement the lead removed, on a replay design the lead itself proposes, and on a definition of "requirement" written after the proposer's corrections

**Objection.** s8.1 settles P3 "for AppWorld" because "P3's revised hard requirements (reproducible tool answers, no live drift) are met natively by AppWorld and only partly by MCP-Atlas; the lead had weighted MCP realism, which is not a requirement. The lead concedes on the evidence of section 5 (I1 items 1e, 6)." Four problems.
1. *The concession does not follow from its evidence.* I1 item 1e gives the share of keyless tasks in MCP-Atlas (5 of 100 by enabled tools, 18 by called tools); I1 item 6 says AppWorld's definitions are files. Neither says AppWorld is better for P3; they say the MCP-Atlas pool is smaller and AppWorld is offline.
2. *"Only partly by MCP-Atlas" is contradicted by s7 P3 #2.* The same section proposes "tool results recorded once and replayed for paired cells", which is the way to meet H4 and H5 on MCP-Atlas. If replay is adequate, MCP-Atlas meets the requirement; if replay is inadequate (CH-0163 argues it is, for arm-divergent calls), then s7 P3 #2 and E2E #2 are unsound. The register cannot hold both.
3. *Circularity.* The requirements used to settle the disagreement are "revision 2", adopted from the proposer's corrections X1-X6 (s8.1 last bullet) and from CH-0147, which moved H3 out of the candidate list. After the move, the remaining discriminators between the two P3 candidates are H4/H5 (reproducibility), pool size (165 against about 90) and scorer. "MCP realism is not a requirement" is true of the written H1-H5 but s1.2 itself flags that "real-world has no stated target" and INT-T-021 s2 asks to prefer established benchmarks "for real-world or end-to-end performance claims". The lead declared the consideration a non-requirement and then used that to resolve the disagreement in the proposer's favour. The disagreement has not been resolved by evidence; the honest state is "open, with the decision turning on whether the claim must concern MCP deployments" (the proposer's own falsifier, DPR-0010 s10).
4. *Construct fit is not discussed.* P3 s4 asks for "the probability that a model searches, unprompted, when the needed tool is deferred". AppWorld tasks need 8.2 unique APIs on average (Test-N, max 17; `v5-bm-b13` L735-736), with login and prerequisite calls (the README says agents must "log in before calling an API that requires authentication", L949), so a task has many needed tools; deferring one of them does not isolate a single search decision. s7 L188 claims AppWorld "meets every revised requirement natively except that it is not MCP"; the construct problem (many needed tools per task, prerequisites) is the proposer's own caveat in DPR-0010 P3-1 and is not carried over. The human's instruction (INT-T-021 s1.3) names "prerequisite dependencies" as a property to assess; no card has the column (CH-0152) and s7 does not assess it for the rank-1 candidate.

**Evidence.** [F] register L219, L189, L194; I1 items 1e and 6; `v5-bm-b13` L735-736; `v5-bm-b14` L949; INT-T-021 s1.3, s2; DPR-0010 P3-1, s10 falsifiers. [I] the circularity reading.

**What would resolve it.** Record P3 first choice as open between AppWorld and MCP-Atlas with the deciding question stated (must the claim concern MCP deployments; does replay work, CH-0163), or give evidence beyond I1 1e/6. Carry the multi-need and prerequisite caveat into the AppWorld P3 entry and say how counting is done (per needed API, task as cluster).
