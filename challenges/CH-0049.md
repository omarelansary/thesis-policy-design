# Title: the stage-inclusion rule says "cheaper" without a tie or error rule, conflicts with the epsilon rule of section 6, and adds a decision while claiming not to

**Grade: SERIOUS.** (02 section 5 "Reading rule"; section 6; brief sentence 4.)

**Objection.**
1. **"Cheaper, on paired data" is undefined.** A point estimate? A paired interval excluding zero? Beyond epsilon? This is the very tie problem section 6 was written to remove. With a point-estimate reading, a component is kept whenever noise favours it; with an interval reading and the power in section 7, almost nothing is kept (see CH-0057). The sentence "it does not add a decision" is wrong: it adds a second decision rule alongside S5.
2. **Conflict with section 6(a).** The epsilon rule picks "the simplest arm whose estimated cost is within `epsilon` of the minimum". The inclusion rule keeps a component if its arm is cheaper than the arm without it. Take arm B = A plus component Y with cost(B) = cost(A) - 0.5 epsilon, both feasible: inclusion keeps Y, the epsilon rule picks A. The two rules can disagree on the same table; the document does not say which governs the reported configuration.
3. **Multiplicity.** The matched pairs add one feasibility test and one cost test per component (core, P1 L1, P1 L2, P3, navigation, plus the core variants) on top of the six contrasts used in section 7. CH-0040 (a), still open in its round 2, asked that the arm space be enumerated so that family-wise error is computable; v4 adds arms and adds no enumeration.
4. **Path dependence, noted but not bounded.** Section 9 lists it as a weakness. A component whose benefit depends on another (P3 only with an interface; a P1 level-2 only with level 1) is tested only in the pairs present in the table. The result "Y not kept" is conditional on the pair chosen, so the brief's "a stage is kept only if..." (sentence 4) reads as a property of the stage when it is a property of the table.
5. **Navigated hierarchy is a replacement for ranked search, not an add-on.** "The same arm without it" is then ambiguous (search-only? no interface?). The test answers "which of two discovery mechanisms is cheaper", not "does this stage pay".
6. **Selection again from the same data.** Components are kept on the development-split comparison; confirmation (S6) is of the chosen arm only. If confirmation fails, what happens is unspecified (CH-0040 (b), still unaddressed in v4).

**Evidence.** 02 lines 79, 89-93, 120; CH-0040 round 2 (a), (b); arithmetic above is mine and needs no source.

**What would resolve it.** Define "cheaper" (interval, epsilon, or point estimate with the stated consequence), state precedence between section 5 and section 6, enumerate the arm table size before the power statement, say what the result of an unconfirmed arm is. Answerable now except the error rates.

**Response (lead).** ACCEPT: stage inclusion is now a consequence of the single rule of 02 section 6, which governs; replacement compared with what it replaces; matched pairs counted in table size; multiplicity (CH-0040 a, b) stays open. [rounds used: 1 of 2]

**Round 2 (challenger, 2026-10-19): OPEN, one residual (serious, answerable now).** The conflict with the epsilon rule is removed in 02 section 5 and the replacement case is handled. Residual R2: (i) 02 section 5 says a component is "kept" if its arm is in S "and is not preferred away by the parsimony preference". Under option (b) of section 6 (no epsilon) there is no preference, so every component in any arm of S is "kept" by mere membership of S, and S contains every feasible arm when data are few. That is the original problem in reverse (underpowered default = keep), and a component in an arm of S has not been shown to pay. Say: under (a) a component is kept only in the arm chosen; under (b) no component is declared kept or dropped. (ii) Brief section 2 sentence 4 says a component is kept "only if ... feasible in success and not shown more expensive in tokens". That is S membership alone, weaker than 02 (which adds the parsimony preference) and weaker than the earlier "only where a stated test justifies it". Align brief and 02. (iii) "simplest" (fewest components) has no stated order (is a navigated hierarchy simpler than P1 level 1?).

**Outcome.** open with residual R2.
