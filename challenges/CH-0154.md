# Title: Four rules say more than their cited sources, and the Verdict column reads as verification of the rule when it verifies a quotation

**Objection.** CC1 says every selection rule cites a stored methods source or is marked as the lead's reasoning. The Verdict column is the stewards' quote-check, but it sits in a table whose rows are the rules, so "CONFIRMED" reads as "the source supports the rule".
- **R1** (different benchmarks per experiment are sound when chosen for the construct; the choice rule fixed before outcomes). The sources are the abstract of Benchmark Lottery (abstract page only; no affiliations on the page; SUM-0065 M1) and Bean et al. (items should represent the task space, S3.3). The abstract says the relative performance of methods can change by *choosing different benchmark tasks*. That supports the claim that benchmark choice must be justified and its sensitivity shown, and it cuts against reading one stage's conclusion from a benchmark chosen for that stage and then combining the stages into one statement about V5-R (the thesis claim is about the combination). The row says so only in part ("none prescribes it either [U]"). The verdict "CONFIRMED" is for the quotation. R1 is the lead's reasoning (the row also says so in its last sentence); the table should say that in the verdict.
- **R3** adds "freezing also covers tool-set sizes, padding and prompt variants". Neither Kapoor nor Hofman says that. Kapoor's finding is about missing held-out sets in agent benchmarks, with a lenient criterion (a holdout counts if one exists *or the designers indicate intent*, SUM-0065 M7). Hofman et al. is a qualitative study of ML graduate students working under a protocol (SUM-0065 M6 note), and its "provided it is adhered to" sentence is about pre-registration of a learning pipeline. The extension to experimental factors is sensible and is the lead's reasoning.
- **R7** (constructed tasks are valid only with a documented method and checked ground truth). Gill et al. say LLM-generated variants are "often valid according to the annotation guidelines" and "less challenging" (SUM-0066 M32); they do not set a validity condition. The condition is the human's requirement (INT-T-021 s2: "explicit scientific justification, validated ground truth, documented construction methodology, and appropriate controls against bias"). Cite that as REQ, not as literature.
- **R9** (choose-after-seeing and selective reporting are real risks) rests on Leaderboard Illusion, which documents a platform operator giving vendors private testing and retraction on one leaderboard (27 private variants by one vendor). That is an analogy for researchers choosing benchmarks where they win; the row concedes that no systematic measurement of papers doing so was found. Labelled CONFIRMED, it reads stronger.
- **R5** treats estimation-only reporting as a rule while s1.2 says adequacy depends on the human choosing estimation-only or a margin. That is a rule that presupposes one reading of an open decision.
Section numbers in R3, R4, R5 ("§5", "§4.1-4.2") were not checked by the stewards (SUM-0065 header), and the table prints them.

**Evidence.** `17` s3 L62-73, s1.2 L19; SUM-0065 M1, M6, M7; SUM-0066 M30, M32; INT-T-021 L41.
[F] sources as cited. [I] that the Verdict column will be misread; and that R1's use of the Lottery abstract is looser than the table implies.

**What would resolve it.** Split the last column into "quotation on the stored page" and "supports the rule: yes / partly / lead's reasoning / human requirement" and fill it row by row; delete the printed section numbers that were not verified; mark R1, R3 (extension), R5 and R7 accordingly.
