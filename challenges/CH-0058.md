# Title: LOMEKWI section 6 is described with the wrong direction and the wrong object in the supervisor-facing brief, and its "0.02" is not a success rate

**Grade: SERIOUS (a factual error in `01` line 75, which goes to the supervisor; the design consequence is small).**

**Objection.**
1. `01` section 4 last row says: "in a tool-use game small models almost never explored visible tools until told one exists". The text says the opposite about size. In the Recognition MCP experiment the **smallest** models probe tools freely and the **largest** (27B, 32B) stop: Qwen3.5 tool calls per episode 3.1 at 2B falling to 0.2 at 27B; episodes with no tool call 61/180 at 2B and 154/180 at 27B (LOM:622-624). The authors' reason for the small end is necessity: "the tools are their only viable route to a win and they probe them freely" (LOM:627-628). Also, Qwen3 peaks at 0.93 recognition at 8B (LOM:483). "Small ... almost never explored" is not what the paper reports for any family at its small end. Section 6 is also not "a game": it is a long-division task with decoy tools (LOM:271-276); the game is Lomekwi (section 5).
2. `05` section 1 writes "(success credited 0.02)". 0.02 is **oracle discovery** (the probability that the one working tool was called), not task success (LOM:478-481). The same model's overall win rate is 0.88 and 157 of 180 problems were solved by hand (LOM:485-487, 624-626). The model that "never calls a tool" mostly *succeeds*. A reader will take "0.02" as a failure rate.
3. The mechanism the authors give is that a model that can do the task by hand has "no reason to spend budget probing" (LOM:633-636), and that probing is a hedge when told a tool exists (LOM:794-800: "treating a cheap tool call as a hedge rather than a concession"). That setting has a manual route; in our setting the missing tool is by assumption needed. The paper's own necessity result points the other way for us (models without a manual route probe), and under a bill criterion not probing when the task is solvable is the cheaper behaviour. `05` section 2.1 uses the 0.02 -> 0.60 jump as a reason that "the scope statement's content may decide whether models search"; the hint is "exactly one of the T tools can solve the problem" (LOM:290-292, 657-659), i.e. an existence guarantee plus a count of candidates, not a scope statement. [I] The inference is possible but is a stretch; it should be labelled so.
4. The tool-prior effect was measured on Qwen2.5/3/3.5 under 35B only (LOM:476-477); the Claude models appear only in the creation game. `05` does say "small Qwen models"; `01` does not.

**Evidence.** LOM = `evidence/literature-20261019/arxiv-2607.16961.txt` lines 271-292, 476-487, 619-636, 654-659, 783-801, 829-843. Checked by me against the stored text; numbers 154/180, 0.02, 0.60, 0.2, 5.6 are copied correctly. Not checked: the appendix prompts (A).

**What would resolve it.** Rewrite `01` line 75 to say what the paper reports: in a long-division task with up to 20 visible decoy tools, the largest sub-35B Qwen models mostly solved by hand and rarely called tools unless told that one tool works; small models that could not do the arithmetic probed freely. Replace "success credited 0.02" by "oracle tool called in 2% of scored episodes while 157 of 180 problems were solved by hand". Label the carry-over to scope statements as [I].
