# Title: "Pilot 1 reduces to reading cache-read and cache-write fields" is right for one question and understates an honest cost claim

**Grade: SERIOUS (it sets the expected size of the work and the strength of the conclusion).**

**Objection.** Reading the fields answers *whether an append hits the cache on provider X in configuration Y*. It does not give a cost claim for a policy. Missing:
1. **Whole-trajectory bill, not one field.** Output tokens (discovery requests, search-turn text, thinking) are a separate price line, usually at a multiple of input; extra rounds each re-read the prefix at the read multiplier; failed trajectories cost too. The claim needs cost per attempted and per successful task (`04` section 6 item 2 itself requires output tokens), with every call's `usage`.
2. **Breakpoint placement for the appended block.** "The prefix is untouched" (EXT-0093 line 70) is about the original prefix. Whether the *appended* definitions are themselves cached from the next turn depends on explicit breakpoints (limit of four, EXT-0093 line 66 / EXT-0096) or automatic caching, and on the 5-minute or 1-hour TTL; otherwise they are re-billed as fresh input each turn. This is a harness choice that changes the number.
3. **Minimum cacheable length and thresholds.** 1,024 to 4,096 tokens by provider (EXT-0104 lines 73-75, 125-128); a restricted tool block plus a short system prompt can fall under it, in which case the saving from restriction in tokens is a saving at full price, not at 0.1x, and the cache study shows regressions below the threshold (EXT-0104 lines 115-119).
4. **Provider differences.** Savings range from about 28-41% (Gemini) to 79-81% (GPT-5.2) for the same design in the one study that compares providers (EXT-0103 lines 171-173); Anthropic read multipliers differ by model (`04` section 2). Anthropic's explicit-breakpoint model and OpenAI's automatic one need separate checks.
5. **Whether deferred definitions are billed.** EXT-0007 line 84: "You provide every tool definition in the `tools` array and set `defer_loading: true`". SUM-0004 V9 recorded that definitions are still sent. The `usage` fields must show whether deferred tokens are counted; this is the E1 question and is not settled by a cache-field read.
6. **Noise and sample.** "A handful of trajectories" gives no variance; cache hits are best-effort, TTFT varied heavily in the cited study (EXT-0104 lines 148-153), and the study waited more than 24 hours between conditions (EXT-0103 lines 137-142) to avoid contamination. A pilot without cold-start control can mislabel a miss.
7. **The arithmetic in `04` section 3 is not "published multipliers" alone.** The table assumes a warm cache for the whole block, no output, no extra rounds, and counts a single write; `05` section 2.1 says "the cost model is arithmetic from published multipliers" as though settled. It is a first-order model with those omissions.

None of this says pilot 1 is large. It says the right framing is "a check that the documented mechanism holds on provider X plus a full-usage accounting of a few matched trajectories", with the claim limited to that provider, model, TTL and breakpoint layout.

**Evidence.** As cited. All passages read by me.

**What would resolve it.** Replace "reduces to reading the fields" by the list above in `05` section 2.1 and in `04` section 8 item 4; state the claim's scope (provider, model, TTL, layout).
