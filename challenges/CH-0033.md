# Title: "worth testing against the rivals" is asserted, not argued: no regime is named in which C-A could beat cached expose-all, and the bound on its possible saving is not stated

**Grade: SERIOUS (AUTH-005: "why it is worth testing against them, not instead of them").**

**The strongest rival, stated at full strength.** Expose-all in a deterministic, stable, cached prefix (C-0), and vendor-native deferral with a vendor core (P3). C-0 has no selection cost, no initiation risk, no recall loss, no extra turn, no unmeasured instruction, and no offline estimation. Its tool-block cost under a warm cache is `r*N_f*p_u` per request: on the only measured provider (Table 15, `r ~ 0.0088`) that is about 770 uncached-input-token equivalents for an 87k-token library, so the entire maximum saving of any restriction there, `(1-rho)*r*N_f*p_u`, is below that. Against it C-A has to pay for `epsilon` (recall), `d_s` per discovery, the unwritten instruction, and the A1 offline cost, and its success side rests on usage concentration (HYP-0014, no measurement), prompted initiation (HYP-0010, no measurement), and a model-based sizing rule. What favours C-0: Toollery natural replay (no billed advantage; CI includes zero, V3), `rag:32` ~ `flat` on one model (36.7 vs 36.0, V6; but with a +-8 interval and flat on one model only), strong models mitigating distraction (EXT-0026 via the map), OpenAI's advice to compare before choosing a default (EXT-0012), and Anthropic's own rule that a small non-deferred set is the baseline (EXT-0009). What would make it lose: a provider with higher `r` (all EXT-0021 values are >= 2.8 times the Table 15 value), a catalogue that does not fit the context or is long enough to cost accuracy (flat costs 8 points on one model/one draw in EXT-0053; EXT-0033/0034 via the map), long horizons with many turns re-reading, or cold caches (ratio `rho`).

**Objection.** DPR-0004 section 3 and HYP-0013 say the candidate is "non-dominated in some regime that can be named before the run" but never name one in terms of the quantities the proposer's own model identifies (`r`, `n*L`, `T_bar`, cadence `g/Lambda`, and a distraction effect). Without it the claim is not tested against the rival's strongest region and cannot be falsified by choosing a regime where C-0 trivially wins or loses.

**Evidence.** EXT-0061, EXT-0062 (V3), EXT-0021 l.32, EXT-0053 (V6), EXT-0012, EXT-0009; DPR-0005 sections 3.1, 5.

**What would resolve it.** State, from the proposer's closed forms, the region of (`r`, `n*L`, `T_bar`, `g/Lambda`) in which restriction's *maximum possible* billed saving exceeds the C-A overheads, and name the distraction effect that would have to be present when it does not; HYP-0013 then claims non-dominance in those regimes only.

**Round 2 (challenger): DOWNGRADE serious -> minor; "never name one" was overstated.** DPR-0004 3.2a states the saving bound `(1-rho) r_eff N_f p_u`, names four candidate regimes, says C-0 is the answer if none holds, and does not claim a numeric region (symbols only; the cost-mechanics experiment is step 1 of 6.7). That is what can be asked at this stage. Residuals: (i) regime (iii), the context-overflow half, conflicts with the CH-0024 limit (A1 not estimable where `pi_ref` cannot run); (ii) regime (iv), exposure as the criterion, makes any restriction win by construction, so it is a criterion choice (CH-0032, O2) rather than a regime; (iii) regime (ii), cold caches, is shared by vendor-native deferral, so it does not by itself favour A1 over P3; (iv) HYP-0013's front matter still says "regimes that can be named before the run" without naming them.
