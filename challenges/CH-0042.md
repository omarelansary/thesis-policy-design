# Title: the token objective with an aggregate noninferiority constraint has perverse optima (tail dilution, expensive-task shedding, uncounted compute, regime-dependent triviality)

**Grade: SERIOUS for D4's choices; the objective itself is the human's instruction (AUTH-006) and I do not argue against it. The objections concern the formulation F1 and the measurement.**

**Objection.** (errata section 0; 02 nodes D4, R5; package/A-problem-and-objective.md F1-F7.)
1. **Tail dilution (the main one).** F1 constrains *mean* success. A restriction that makes a rarely needed capability permanently unusable passes whenever the mass of tasks needing it is below `delta`. In a 10,000-tool catalogue with a long tail, the tail's total mass can be below any plausible `delta`; D4 then prefers the arm without search interface, scope statement or recovery (cheapest in tokens), if such an arm is in D3 (CH-0041). This is the sacrifice of a stratum that package A already recorded as F7 ("a mean can be maintained while a stratum is sacrificed; an omitted tool is a hard failure") and that F1 does not exclude. Reintroduction, the supervisor's example (REQ-O14-15), is then justified only if the tail mass exceeds `delta` on the dev distribution, which is a property of the benchmark mix. Related: tasks that need no tool, or only core tools, dilute any harm.
2. **Expensive-task shedding.** Cumulative input tokens grow roughly with the square of the trajectory length (each turn re-reads history). A small fraction of long trajectories can dominate `C_tok`. Under a mean-success constraint a policy may spend all of `delta` on abandoning the most expensive tasks (a turn cap, a small search budget `B_s`, a model that answers early). `B_s` and trajectory caps are in G's parameters. Nothing in F1 detects this; per-length-stratum success and a cost-by-length report would.
3. **Failing early is cheap.** An arm that makes the model give up or answer without tools saves tokens in exactly the trajectories it fails. Within `delta` this is legitimate under F1; it is also what a non-initiating model does in a search-only design (HYP-0010 unmeasured). The token saving of search-only could therefore be partly "saved by failing", and the report of tokens must be split by outcome (success vs failure) to see it.
4. **Work moved to uncounted components.** Only *input tokens* are counted. Output and reasoning tokens (re-read later as input only if they stay in history), embedding calls, index builds, BM25 ranking, rule engines and latency are outside `C_tok`. An embedding or lexical selector therefore has near-zero token cost, and an LLM selector reading the catalogue is expensive (DPR-0002 section D, `a_1 n_0`), so the criterion biases towards non-LLM selectors for reasons unrelated to the policy's quality. The errata should list non-token compute as a recorded quantity.
5. **Regime dependence of what is informative.** At the closest vehicle's scale the flat tool block is about 292k tokens (S4G l.28) against (assumed) a history of the order of thousands to tens of thousands of tokens [S], so any restriction wins on tokens and the choice is decided entirely by the success constraint; at a small catalogue the tool block may be a small share of the total and arm differences may fall below trajectory-length noise [S, no trajectory data in the record]. The token criterion would then discriminate least where the choice is hardest. (EXT-0062 is single-step: 87,074 against 2,196 input tokens per request, no history.) Report the tool-block share of total tokens per arm (the lead already lists categories; I ask that the share be a stated diagnostic).
6. **Token minimisation can raise billed cost.** In EXT-0062 (Table 15, natural replay) the restricted arm has 2,196 input tokens against 87,074 yet billed input cost $0.000819 against $0.000749 (hit rate 0.144 against 0.989). The lead records billed cost separately (good), but a thesis on a "tool-access policy" will be read as a cost thesis; the token-optimal arm may be billed-cost-worse in a regime the vehicle itself measured. Say so in the presentation, with the interval caveat (the paper's interval for that cell comes from EXT-0061, which I did not re-read).
7. **Measurement instrument.** "Model-visible input tokens" is not defined as to tokenizer or provider usage field; the errata notes only that deferred definitions are not in the context window (a vendor description, EXT-0009 l.25-37; billed treatment of deferred definitions is unknown, V2 CANNOT DETERMINE in RV-0003 section 5). v2 named "tokenizer" as a human decision; v3 does not.
8. **Noninferiority formulation.** One-sided, `delta` undecided, reference possibly a substitute: acceptable as a template. It lacks (a) a per-stratum guard (point 1), (b) a minimum-detectable-difference statement relating sample size to `delta`, (c) a statement that dominance uses intervals rather than point comparisons (errata section 1 "iff `DeltaY >= 0` and `DeltaC <= 0`" is a point-estimate statement).

**Evidence.** Errata sections 0 and 1; package A F1-F7 (read lines 55-60); EXT-0062 (read, Table 15); S4G l.28 (via CH-0002 and package A; the governing file line 28 read); DPR-0002 section D (read). The quadratic-growth statement (point 2) is arithmetic on the estimand as written, not a measurement.

**What would resolve it.** Report success and tokens stratified by trajectory length and by outcome; add a per-stratum noninferiority guard or state F7 as a rejected alternative with reasons; list non-token compute; fix the tokenizer/usage-field convention; report tool-block share. All of these are specification changes the lead can make now; the size of each effect needs data.

**Response (lead, round 2).** 01 section 0 revised: input tokens per call as reported by provider usage fields, decider calls included, extra calls counted, failed trajectories included, cost never conditioned on success; `O_dev / N_dep` charged; stratum guard F7 (rare-capability and trajectory-length strata at least) added to F1; non-LLM compute recorded not in criterion; the EXT-0062 natural-replay cell cited as a reason to record tokens and billed cost separately. [rounds used: 1 of 2]

**Round 2 (challenger): DOWNGRADE to minor; mostly resolved.** Points 1 (tail dilution), 2 (expensive-task shedding via the trajectory-length stratum), 4, 6 and 7 are addressed in text. The EXT-0062 numbers quoted in 01 section 0 match Table 15 (2,196 vs 87,074; $0.000819 vs $0.000749). Residuals: (i) point 3: tokens are not required to be reported split by outcome (success vs failure), so "saved by failing" cannot be seen in the mean; (ii) point 5: the tool-block share of total tokens is not named as a diagnostic (it falls under "token categories" only implicitly); (iii) "usage fields as reported by the provider" is the instrument, but whether the provider counts deferred definitions in its usage is unknown (V2 CANNOT DETERMINE, RV-0003 section 5); the instrument definition makes "model-visible" mean "provider-reported", which should be said; (iv) the stratum margins are undecided and the power consequence is in CH-0040 (c).

**Outcome.** partly resolved (conceded); residuals minor.
