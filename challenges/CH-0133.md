# Title: s4.1's headline overstates what the pages say about vendor practice, and the block-granularity explanation cannot account for the probe's partial hit rates

**Objection.** (a) "The vendors' own practice is V5's rule" generalises. V5-R06 is "the prefix identical across users of a deployment; anything per user after it". The checked page for Claude Code (A1/A8, SUM-0056) says its cache "is effectively scoped to one machine and directory" because "the system prompt embeds your auto memory paths" and the conversation opens with working directory, platform and OS, and a startup git snapshot decides whether sequential sessions share. So the vendor's own client puts per-machine and per-user content early and documents the loss; the user-level fix is documented for Agent SDK fleets only. What the pages support is "vendor guidance says static first, dynamic last" (F9, F11), not that vendor clients follow V5-R06.
(b) "Memory placed in the system prompt (Letta ..., Oh My Pi's ... ReasoningBank) ... is what A8 warns against": SUM-0056 says A8 ties the miss to differing directories and the git snapshot "not to per-user content as such"; the generalisation to memory content is the scout's, and C13/C14 are unchecked leads.
(c) `15` cites OpenAI's "restrict with `tool_choice`/`allowed_tools` instead of removing" (F11) as vendor practice that strengthens V5. `10` L39 records that `allowed_tools` is rejected (HTTP 400, "Invalid tool choice") by the university's LiteLLM proxy on every model that reached it, and `10` L49 says HYP-0018 cannot be tested by it. `15` does not say the strongest vendor-recommended alternative to editing the array is unavailable on V5's route. Likewise A4 (stored `v5-ls-a01-claude-en-prompt-caching.md.txt` L171): without tool search, a deny rule removes the definition and invalidates the cache.
(d) "[I] Block-granular caching is one candidate explanation for the partial, block-wise hit rates the human's probe saw." `10` L27-32: the cached counts are multiples of 16 (gpt-oss), 64 (GLM-5.3), 128 (MiniMax, GLM-Flash) or 32 (Qwen), and two models cached only 59-69% of the same prompt (Qwen 3,296 of 5,574). Block granularity loses at most one partial block (under 128 tokens of a prompt of about 5,000 to 5,500); it cannot explain a 31-41% shortfall. It explains "block-wise", not "partial". `15` also leaves out `cache_salt` (F16, SUM-0057: a salt isolates caches per request group in multi-tenant deployments), a candidate reason for non-sharing that the page itself names.

**Evidence.** SUM-0056 A1, A8; SUM-0057 F11, F16; `design/package-v5/10-p0-university-api-reading.md` L27-32, L39, L49; stored a01 L171.

**What would resolve it.** Narrow the headline to "vendor guidance"; attribute the A8 generalisation to the scout; add one line that `allowed_tools` is rejected on the university route; restate the vLLM line as explaining the block multiples only, and name cache_salt and eviction as other candidates, all marked [U].
