# Title: "Semantic dilution" is quoted as the downside of enrichment; the Tool-DE paper presents it as a net-beneficial effect

**Objection.** `14` writes "Tool-DE: BM25s 36.41 to 39.35 but e5-mistral 40.02 to 38.85, documents 131.72 to 177.61 tokens, 'semantic dilution'". Placed after the e5-mistral drop, the quote reads as the cause of the regression. The paper says the similarity scores of expanded documents are lower "plausibly due to semantic dilution caused by increased length", then "Importantly, the reduction is asymmetric: positives drop only slightly ..., whereas negatives show much larger decreases ... This asymmetry yields sharper positive/negative separability and improves relative ordering, which directly benefits retrieval quality" (`v5-xp-arxiv-2510.22670.txt` L838-849; section heading L827-828 "discriminative gains despite similarity dilution"). The paper gives no explanation for the e5-mistral regression in the lines I read. The numbers themselves are correct (L323, L454, L375, L506, L227-229); the 131.72 to 177.61 are average token counts of ToolRet and Tool-DE documents, i.e. index text, which also fits the "searchable text" idea. In the rows I read, expansion raised the average for four of the five systems (BM25s, GritLM, NV-Embed, gte-Qwen2; L454, L467, L480, L493) and lowered it for e5-mistral (L506); the Qwen3-Embedding row was not read. `14` summarises this as "mixed".

**Evidence.** Lines as above.

**What would resolve it.** Either drop the quotation marks around "semantic dilution" or write "the authors report lower absolute similarity from expansion but sharper positive/negative separation; they give no reason for the e5-mistral drop".
