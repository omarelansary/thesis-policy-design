# Adversarial review of the benchmark register, sections 7-10, and of the application of RV-0027

Labels: [F] stated in a stored file, [O] observed by me, [I] my reading, [U] unknown. I read sections 1-10 of `17`, DPR-0010, the frozen lead ranking, RV-0027, CH-0157, `05`, parts of `04`, the INT-T-021 text, the I1 and I2 reports, and opened stored pages for AppWorld (paper `b13`, README `b14`, code `i24`), MCP-Atlas v3 (`i13`), MCPMark (paper and issue `e11`) and the SUM-0071/0077 notes. I ran no search or script and sent no message. I did not open the other cards' pages beyond those named; "not found" means not found in those places.

## 1. Verdict
Sections 7-10 are honest about much: the open choices are kept open (P1 native check, P4 vehicle), the completion table grades CC3, CC4, CC6 as partly, the constructed options are ranked under the same rules, and the V5 changes are reported and not made (I found none made). The structural weakness is that AppWorld heads five of six experiments on the strength of properties nobody in this workspace has measured, while material the register already stores about AppWorld is omitted or contradicted. Thirteen challenges: CH-0158..CH-0170, **1 blocking, 10 should-fix, 2 note**.

## 2. Objections, most important first
1. **CH-0159 blocking.** Stored pages say what the register leaves out or contradicts for AppWorld: full documentation about 142K tokens (register: "about 114k, unmeasured"), 250 scenarios x 3 tasks with scenario scores 30-50% below task scores (register: "scenario structure unchecked"), a first-class MCP server (register: "not MCP"), open-model scores of 24.4 and 7.0 on Test-N and Test-C (register: "open models in the paper"), unread issues on state reset, and a planned hidden test set. Asymmetric scrutiny: the benchmark with least inspection ranks first.
2. **CH-0158 should-fix.** Concentration (five of six experiments) is named nowhere in 7-10; the "agreement" of the two rankings is common-mode (same cards, proposer read no pages); "agree on P5 and P1" hides a merge (P5) and a base flip (P1).
3. **CH-0160 should-fix.** The P3 settlement uses the requirement revision the proposer's corrections created, contradicts the register's own replay design for MCP-Atlas, and rests on I1 1e/6, which do not say AppWorld is better. Open, not resolved.
4. **CH-0161 should-fix.** s9's "+/-7 points" ignores the clustering rule R11 (AppWorld Test-N = 56 scenarios); the promised pool and resolvable-difference columns are missing; MCP-Atlas judge noise (2.1-4.6) equals the effect size.
5. **CH-0162 should-fix.** Floor/ceiling applied unevenly; MCP-Atlas "mid-range" contradicts the authors' compression statement; contamination absent from 7-10.
6. **CH-0163 should-fix.** MCP-Atlas arm: replay cannot answer arm-divergent calls; subset defined by called tools while enabled tools include keyed ones; representativeness not reported.
7. **CH-0167 should-fix.** The E2E #1 session axis is constructed from a single-prompt benchmark and R-large padding can favour the policy under test.
8. **CH-0165 should-fix.** Fixes added as overlays leave old text (triage L144, cache-accounting sentence L82, MCP-Atlas cell); CH-0157 only partly applied.
9. **CH-0164 should-fix.** Licence statements inconsistent (captured definitions, MPT, Toolathlon "unusable" and P2 rank 3, VitaBench "MIT").
10. **CH-0168 should-fix.** s9 item 2 misuses "above n*"; three proposals from DPR-0010 s8 and the lead's own F7/F9 are missing.
11. **CH-0169 should-fix.** CC4 for AppWorld is "not met", limitations list omits main risks.
12. **CH-0166 note.** "Deterministic, no judge" still carries the ranking after R4 withdrew the preference.
13. **CH-0170 note.** MCPMark "51 tasks" is paper-v1; the exploit count is from a later state.

- Section 9 item 1: BFCL appends held-out functions to the tools list and replaces the user turn (I2 item 5); V5-R02 forbids editing the tools array and says discoveries are appended (04 L87). The item is accurate on the substance.
- AppWorld's licence text and the README request (L338, L1604-1618) are as s9 item 6 and s7 P5 describe them (my CH-0164 point 5 only extends the scope to logs).
- The numbers "5 of 100 / 18 of 100 keyless" are in I1 1e as the register states; "about 90 of 500" is the register's extrapolation, stated as conditional.
- CC1: R11-R14 are labelled [I]; no unsupported "CONFIRMED" claim found among them.

## 5. Alternatives the register did not choose, and what would favour them
- *MCP-Atlas first for P3 and E2E's real arm:* favoured if the claim must concern MCP deployments, if a replay or per-call fallback is shown to work (CH-0163), and if the AppWorld pilot shows floor or window failure (142K tokens).
- *AppWorld exposed through its own MCP server:* favoured if the README's server works with the route's client and gives MCP-formatted definitions for P5 without Docker (untested; CH-0159).
- *Constructed tasks over real definitions for P3 and P1:* favoured by the multi-need and prerequisite structure of AppWorld tasks (CH-0160) and by the lack of any native session axis (CH-0167).
- *tau2-bench or BFCL multi-turn for a small-catalogue arm:* favoured only if R-small is a regime of its own and floor, simulator and scorer-per-arm checks pass; s7 lists tau2 as an alternative and rejects BFCL; I found no new evidence against either.
- *Estimation-only reporting:* favoured by the clustering arithmetic; the register leaves the choice to the human, which is right (CH-0161 asks only for corrected intervals).

## 6. Disagreements preserved (no proposer/lead response yet; 0 of 2 rounds used)
- The register settles P3 for AppWorld; I hold it is open (CH-0160).
- The register presents the two rankings as agreeing on P5, P2, E2E and P1; I hold the agreement is common-mode and partly a merge (CH-0158).
- The register calls MCP-Atlas open models "mid-range"; I hold the authors' page says the top band is compressed (CH-0162).

## Overall view and uncertainty
Not a recommendation for or against any benchmark. The register's s7 is a useful candidate list when each rank 1 is read as "first, conditional on a pilot, and unmeasured on the route". The blocking item is fixable by editing the AppWorld card and s7 from pages already stored; the others are should-fix because the human, not the register, makes the choice. A reader who stops at s7-s8 would take away that the two rankings independently confirm AppWorld; I do not think the evidence supports that statement.
