## 2. The strongest rival, argued at full strength
**Cached expose-all (C-0) and vendor-native deferral (P3) with a vendor core.** C-0 has no selection cost, no initiation risk, no recall loss, no extra turns, no unwritten instruction and no offline estimation. Under a warm cache its tool-block cost is `r*N_f*p_u` per request. On the only measured provider (EXT-0062, Table 15) the implied `r` is about 0.0088 [I, six cells, reproduced twice], so the 87,074-token library costs about 770 uncached-input-token equivalents per request; the most any restriction can save there is below that, and the table reports a point estimate where the restricted arm costs more (+$0.000070, CI [-0.000524, 0.000462], "inconclusive": EXT-0061; V3). Accuracy evidence is mixed: flat cost 8 points on one model/one draw while `rag:32` tied flat on the one model that ran both (EXT-0053; V6, intervals about +-8); strong models are reported to have mitigated distraction (EXT-0026 via the map). Vendors advise comparing before choosing a default (EXT-0012) and keeping a small eager set (EXT-0009). C-A adds risks C-0 lacks: unreached needs (`epsilon`), non-initiation (HYP-0010, unmeasured; EXT-0047/0048, V8 PARTLY), a contract whose wording is unwritten, an offline cost.
**What would make C-0 win:** a low-`r` provider with dense traffic; a catalogue that fits the context; strong models; flat usage (HYP-0014 false); small deployment volume `N_dep`.
**What would make C-A (or P3) win:** higher `r` (all EXT-0021 multipliers are at least 2.8 times the Table 15 value; at `r = 0.1` the library is ~8.7k equivalents); distraction costing success at catalogue sizes where `pi_ref` still runs; cold or sparse caches (shared by vendor-native deferral); an exposure criterion (O2), which is a criterion choice, not a regime.
**Does the candidate's case survive?** As a conditional case, yes, and the proposal now says so in 3.2a: on the Table 15 price C-A cannot win on billed tool-block cost; it can win in the named regimes, whose numeric region is unknown and is the output of the cost-mechanics experiment. As evidence that C-A is the thing to test, no: the record does not discriminate among P0, P1, P3 and C-A, and the nearest rival to the rule is a size-tuned frequency core (P-sweep). The proposer's adoption of P-sweep as the arm A1 must not be dominated by is the right test; I expect it may well tie. C-B and C-C are not excluded by the record; my CH-0026 does not claim C-B is better.

## 7. Overall view and uncertainty
The package is a clear improvement in specification and honesty over the AUTH-004 template, and the proposer defended where it had reasons (CH-0026, CH-0027, CH-0033) and conceded where the algebra or the text was wrong (CH-0020, CH-0028). It is not shown that C-A is better than anything; it is a testable rule whose special cases are the rivals, with a regime in which it can win that depends on the cache-read price, catalogue size and distraction, none of which is measured on the tested providers. This is a statement about the record, not a recommendation to adopt or reject; the decision is the human's. Objections I now think I overstated: CH-0026 (title), CH-0027 point 1, CH-0033 ("never named"), CH-0032 (grade). I do not think CH-0020 was overstated as decisive: its stated reason for investigation was false until the arms were added.
