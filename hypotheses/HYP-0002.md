# A single selection step is non-dominated by a multi-stage cascade (null for the staged-graph idea)

**Hypothesis.** [hypothesis, not a finding] In the regimes tested, no specified multi-stage cascade dominates the best single-stage policy (plus its graded upgrade and recovery paths). The stage count of the supervisor's suggested graph (REQ-O14-12, REQ-O14-14 example) is not assumed. (Wording fixed after CH-0013: the falsifier is now the exact negation.)

**Why it is plausible.** The cascade model in DPR-0002 section D yields conditions, not a sign: an extra stage pays only if it is cheaper per candidate and its recall effect, `r_1 * r_2(n_1) - r_2(n_0)`, is not negative; with correlated errors compounding is small. EXT-0045/0046 concern JIT schema loading (a P3/P4 pattern, not a filtering cascade) and show recall loss, while the authors say it is most helpful with many tools that could not otherwise be supported: that regime points toward staging. Namespace-level first disclosure is shipped (EXT-0010). No extract in EXT-0007..0049 compares staged and single-stage selection on tools; governing items not re-read (ER-R, ITR, MCP-Zero, App. N) are weak per the challenger. This hypothesis is a *null to test*, not a belief that staging fails.

**What would falsify it.** The falsifier above.

**Assumptions.** Cost and recall are measurable per stage; the same scorer applies to all arms (REQ-O14-03); the cascade is written out completely before the test.

**Alternatives it competes with.** The staged-graph form (REQ-O14-12/13, class `unclear`; G.3 Q2 open); P4g is itself two-level.

**Not claimed.** That staging is wrong; only that its need is untested.
