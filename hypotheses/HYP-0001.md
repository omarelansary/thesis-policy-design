# Regime gate: a runtime-observable quantity determines whether restriction pays

**Hypothesis.** [hypothesis, not a finding] There exists a quantity observable at or shortly after the start of a session (for example early cache-read share from S9, or request repetition) that predicts whether restriction pays in that regime better than pre-session configuration alone (catalogue size, `L(t)`, provider, model). This is reading (B) of CH-0005; DPR-0001 adopts reading (A) (gate is operator configuration) until this is shown.

**Why it is plausible.** Net saving in a skill-loading accounting model depends on load probability and reloads, not size alone (EXT-0041, read); caching statements depend on whether definitions sit in a cached prefix (EXT-0008, EXT-0014, vendor). These quantities are partly learnable from early turns. Reasoning, not evidence that such a predictor works for tools.

**What would falsify it.** The falsifier above, tested by running the arms across a grid of regime variables under one scorer and checking whether any early-observed quantity improves prediction of the non-dominated arm beyond pre-session variables. No thresholds are set here.

**Assumptions.** Early-turn quantities are measurable (the closest vehicle records no cache fields and has no sessions, CAVEATS row 3 / S4G: HYP-0001 cannot be tested on it, CH-0013); regimes can be varied independently.

**Alternatives it competes with.** Reading (A); a vendor size threshold (rejected: arbitrary cut-off, K9 per CH-0005).

**Not claimed.** That any such predictor exists.
