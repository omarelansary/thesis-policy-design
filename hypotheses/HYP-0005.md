# Fewer exposed tools does not imply fewer cumulative tokens or lower billed cost (regime-dependent)

**Hypothesis.** [hypothesis, not a finding] The relation between number (or visible tokens) of exposed tools and cumulative tokens or billed cost is not monotone: in some regimes (cached static prefix, short sessions, extra discovery turns, cache-breaking list changes, selector tokens) fewer exposed tools gives equal or higher cumulative or billed cost.

**Why it is plausible.** Vendor documentation: tool-list modifications invalidate the cache and the resulting miss "can cost more tokens than the definitions you removed" (EXT-0014); discovery "adds a step" (EXT-0012); in one corpus the addressable saving after a token enters the cached prefix is far below nominal counts (EXT-0016/0018, map; tool-output study, not schema filtering). No measurement of catalogue filtering with success exists in the stored corpus (map).

**What would falsify it.** The falsifier above: monotone ordering in all tested regimes and conventions.

**Assumptions.** Cache fields or provider-reported counts available; conventions stated (DPR-0001 section 5).

**Alternatives it competes with.** The default assumption that exposure count tracks cost (AUTH-004 forbids assuming it).

**Not claimed.** That restriction never pays.
