# Billed-cost difference of restriction is determined by the hit-rate differential, not by the token count alone

**Hypothesis.** [hypothesis, not a finding] (a) There exists a regime in which fewer exposed tokens coincide with higher billed input cost (an inversion), and (b) at fixed hit rates the cost is monotone in exposed tokens; inversions arise only through a lower hit rate of the restricted arm, a longer decision path (extra turns) or a write premium, so that the sign of the cost difference follows the closed form of DPR-0005 section 3.

**Correction of HYP-0005 (AUTH-005 item 5c).** HYP-0005's falsifier ("no inversion observed in any tested regime") is the right negation of an existence claim, but "no inversion observed" is not evidence for monotonicity, and the Toollery table does not supply a verified instance either: the natural-replay difference is +$0.000070 per request with 95% CI [-0.000524, 0.000462] (EXT-0061, steward V3 PARTLY), so it is not a verified inversion. What the table does contain is a sign and size that change with cache state (about -97.5% cache-suppressed, indeterminate natural, about -89% forced-warm, computed from EXT-0062 [I]); that is heterogeneity across three cells, not a response curve. A monotonicity or response-curve claim needs the estimated curve with intervals by regime (part b).

**Revision after CH-0029 (challenger, serious).** (i) Part (a) is satisfied by an imprecise sweep and part (b) is linear by construction. Fixes: a "no inversion" or "model holds" conclusion requires interval half-widths below a human-set equivalence margin, otherwise the result is *inconclusive*, not falsification or support; `r` and `w` are calibrated on separate probes (cache and usage fields), not fitted on the cells they then predict. (ii) Part (a) is close to arithmetic once `h_r < h_f` can occur; its content is whether realistic designs produce `h_r < h_f` (HYP-0007), so (a) is kept only as the lead's required verified-instance criterion. Toollery's interval half-width is about 70% of the mean difference (challenger's computation), i.e. too imprecise for either.

**Why it is plausible.** A linear price model (uncached price p_u, cached read p_r) fits all six cells of EXT-0062 with p_r/p_u about 0.009 (DPR-0005 section 2; my arithmetic [I], not steward-verified, and the table's provider/model pricing is not in the extract). Under it, the restricted arm is cheaper iff rho(1-(1-r)h_r) < 1-(1-r)h_f.

**What would falsify it.** The falsifier in the front matter.

**Assumptions.** Single-step or agent-loop replays with provider-reported cache fields; the price model holds for the tested provider (r is estimated per provider, not assumed from the vendor page, EXT-0021).

**Alternatives it competes with.** Monotone cost in exposed tokens (the default assumption AUTH-004 forbids assuming); HYP-0005 as written.

**Not claimed.** That any regime of interest contains an inversion; that restriction never pays.
